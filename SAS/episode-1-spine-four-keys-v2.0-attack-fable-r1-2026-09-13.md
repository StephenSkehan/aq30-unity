# Adversarial review: The Friends with Four Keys, Spine v2.0 (Parts A to G)

Reviewer: Fable, independent of ChatGPT. Read in full. Part H ignored.

## Front 1. Violet, Day −37 to Day −19

**Objection.** The panel was hidden in the roof space over her paint racks, which the document places in the workshop behind the fish market (the Graph; A1 −380 to −80; Brad's Tuesday nights are at her bench "after she has gone"). Part G states the workshop's key population: Violet and Brad, for years, and nobody else. Violet writes in the true letter "I didn't make it." A signwriter who knows she did not make a ten-month water-gilded panel, and finds it wrapped above her own racks in a room with two keys, has an arithmetic sharper than the one she does for the house: the house closes on four, the workshop closes on one. She does the house sum and writes it down ("I know what that says"). She never does the workshop sum, and the document never has her notice that it exists. Worse, she carries the panel round the trade for a month (Day −70 to −40), is told by a signwriter "have you shown your lad", and still does not show the only other keyholder, her own pupil and the one friend she confides in, until Day −19, after the tin and the tube. Five weeks of not showing Brad is either suspicion (which contradicts "she said it was none of them") or an unmotivated authorial convenience so that Brad can hear from the café.

The competent act she does not take is the one she takes for the house: count the keys to the room the thing was found in. If the document instead means the house roof, the objection changes form but does not vanish: Brad would then be carrying a wet gilded panel through her house on the meter-box spare on many Tuesday nights over ten months, which is a different hole.

**Severity.** Serious, and fatal if the roof is reachable only through her locked workshop door and she is written as not noticing. Her named refusal ("to tell is to accuse", "she will not watch") can cover a woman who does the sum and refuses to act on it. It cannot cover a woman who never does the sum, because the reader will do it for her in chapter 3 the moment the café says "asking whose hand" and Ally says "he had a key to her workshop."

**Does it end the episode.** If she does the sum and acts, yes: no key to Brad's box, no Day 0 glass poured. If she does the sum and refuses to act, no, and the refusal is the premise.

**What would have to be true.** Either the roof space over the racks is not hers alone (a shared loft above a run of units, reachable from a neighbour's unit or the market's own store, so that "someone with my key" honestly covers it), or a row is added in which she does the workshop sum, writes it in the book as the thought she will not let herself finish, and the five weeks of not showing Brad are given a paid reason (pride is available: it was better than hers and he was her pupil). Either fix must be written; neither exists in the document.

## Front 2. Brad, Day −19 to Day 14

**Objection.** The kind line ("Brad paid me back the van money this autumn, every penny") is tactically inferior to a line that does the same work and cannot be caught. The document says its purpose is to kill the only motive in the room (C2, Week 3). A forgiveness line in Violet's voice (she let the van money go, never mind what Margo always said about paper) kills the motive equally, is unfalsifiable by Margo (Violet could have changed her mind between Day −100 and Day −19), produces no collision with the unpaid Civic Row account (no cash flow is claimed), gives Brad a consistent oral answer to the estate on Day 49 with the same write-off on Day 55, and removes Liam's Day 33 question entirely. "Repaid" is checkable on three facts Brad knows exist: Margo's knowledge of the calling-in and the sum (he was told she would write it up), the solicitor's account the money was for (Violet told him this herself on Day −110), and the barrel three of the four saw handed over. A man who spent four years building a name with his face on nothing, and who correctly rejects "empty the store", "accuse Liam" and "a written account", does not write the one money sentence that a living accountant can falsify when an unfalsifiable one is to hand. The load-bearing lock of the whole spine (C5, "a money lie only she can catch") therefore rests on an unmotivated error by the most careful person in the story.

The second act that serves him better, same day: telling the truth about when he read his note. He says at a quarter to six that he had "only just opened his" (A2, Day 14 17:30). A grieving man who had it since seven and sat with it because the note said "go together" is unfalsifiable and matches the habit all four know. The lie costs him nothing on Day 14 and buys nothing, and it is the hinge of chapter 7. Written as a plan it is stupid; written as a slip it is luck.

**Severity.** Serious. Not fatal, because both are fixable as wants rather than tactics, and the document's own closing line ("a man who kept the one thing that proved who he was, because she said he could not have made it") shows it knows this man has wants that beat his interest.

**What would have to be true.** The kind line must be written as Brad's vanity, his need for Violet's hand to say he was the one thing that went right, named as a want in C2 and paid, not justified as motive-killing (it is a worse motive-killer than the alternative). The box lie must be given a reason he holds on Day 14 (a fear that "since seven" invites "and you rang none of us") or be made a slip under Ruby's pressure and owned as such in D3.

## Front 3. The room, Days 14 to 47

**Objection.** The line is Liam's, and it is: "It wasn't in the barrel." The kind line says a sum arrived in cash this autumn. Violet did not bank (row 1: cash "more than painting explains"; row 7 shows the whole circle runs on cash). The money was for a solicitor's account not yet due. Where is it? Liam found the barrel on Day 2, handed it to the sister on Day 5 in front of Ruby and Margo, and HCPD logged it as property (row 24), so it was counted. Margo knows roughly what her mother's cash came to (row 2, her own file) and knows the van sum exactly (row 4, her file note). Two people at the table can do the subtraction without accusing anyone, and the document has Liam ask "where did you find the van money, all at once?" on Day 33 (which itself strains foundation 1: a sum "modest" enough never to be a motive is also "eight years' money" notable enough to ask about). The natural next line, at Day 14 or Day 33, is not "where did you find it" but "where did she put it." The document does not say what the four believe the repaid money's destination was, and until it does, "a money lie only she can catch" is a lie at least two of them can touch.

A smaller unanswered line, Margo's, Day 15: "Who fitted the lock?" The room's outward look says "no tradesman" (A3, Day 15) while A1 −35 puts a locksmith at the new lock and the new spare. A competent room names him before closing on a friend.

**Severity.** Serious for the barrel; cosmetic for the locksmith (one sentence: she fitted the cylinder herself and the locksmith cut from a key without seeing the house).

**What would have to be true.** The barrel's contents relative to the van sum must be written: either the mother's cash is large enough that the van money's presence or absence cannot be read from the count, and Liam never counted it (handed to HCPD sealed), or the room is given a destination it believes ("for the lawyer") and the document states that Margo alone can later learn the lawyer was unpaid. As it stands the barrel is a checkable fact sitting in plain view of the three people the lie is supposed to be invisible to.

## Front 4. Margo, Days 8 to 60

**Objection.** Her earliest competent action is on Day 8, not Day 50, and it is professional, not accusatory. She is the deceased's accountant helping the administering sister with the books, and she knows a private loan ledger exists, that it recorded a loan to Brad Collins of a sum in her own file note, in Violet's hand, and that Violet told her in August she was calling it in for the hearing. The document has her say the book "is not among them. Shrug." An accountant does not shrug at a missing receivables record of an estate under administration; she tells the sister's accountant on Day 8 exactly what she tells him on Day 45, because the estate is entitled to know its receivables and the sister's people are the ones asking. The document's own justification ("a book nobody else knew existed") is a reason nobody else could ask, not a reason she could withhold.

**What happens.** The estate's letter to Brad goes out around Day 11 and his "settled between us" is on record before or as the envelopes land on Day 14. The kind line then arrives into a room where Margo already holds Brad's oral answer and, within days, the accountant's note that the Civic Row account was outstanding at death (he is settling the estate's debts in the same weeks). Her reading forms in week 3, not week 8. In week 3 the room has not landed on anyone, Margo still trusts the table, and she has a non-accusatory reason to speak in front of him ("Brad, the sister's people want a date and a figure for that repayment; I have to tell them something"). Brad reads it, and goes around Day 25. The Liam arc (Days 33 to 47), Brad's defending him and losing, and the shape of Margo's guilt after the vanish all collapse or thin. The episode does not end, but its second act is rebuilt around a different clock.

Failing Day 8, the same action is available on Day 14 the moment she reads a written repayment claim, for the same professional reason, with the same effect.

**Severity.** Serious. It attacks the timing of the load-bearing lock, and the lock's shape (a reading she keeps for ten days rather than accuse) survives only if the reading cannot form five weeks earlier.

**What would have to be true.** Margo must hold a written, paid reason for the Day 8 shrug: for instance that she believes, and has believed since Day −2000, that an unpapered cash gift refused paper against her advice is not a receivable but a private matter of Violet's, and that Violet's August intention was a confidence she had not been instructed to act on. That row must exist in C5 and B, and its cost (an accountant putting a dead friend's privacy above the estate's books) must be paid.

## Front 5. Chapter 7

**Objection.** The document contradicts itself on whether the boat owner is aired. A5 Day 290: Ally "gives it to Del and does not air it." B6 row 57: "To Del. Not aired." F10: "Tips to Del never on air. Pass." A5 Day 311, describing the chapter on air: "The boat owner saw him at her door at twenty past. The letter the four read together at nine had been in his hands since morning. Ally claims no more than that." If the sighting airs, chapter 7 breaks the rule F10 says the episode never breaks, and Brad, listening 400 miles away (R13), learns on Day 311 that a witness places him at the store, two weeks before the panel is described, with no reaction written in C2. If it does not air, then "the letter had been in his hands since morning" is unsupported on air: what Ally can source is that he had his key seven hours before he said he had, and "the letter" is an inference she has told the listener she is not making.

Smaller sourcing faults in the same chapter: "had held his key since seven" assumes a Saturday-postmarked item was in a PO box at first clearance on Monday, which Ally cannot know and a listener with a box will say so; "Margo at six" is sourced only through the survivors' memory of the evening's calls, since Margo is dead; and "the autumn the café says she was calling it in" attaches a name to a debt the café heard without one (row 4: "no name"), so the link to Brad is Ally's inference from the letter, not the café's evidence.

**Severity.** Serious, because "Ally claims no more than that" is the honesty the chapter stands on, and as written the reader cannot tell what "that" is.

**What would have to be true.** Either the boat owner consents to air, row 57 and F10 are rewritten to allow one consenting witness on air, and C2 gains a Day 311 row for Brad's response; or the sentence is struck from Day 311 and the chapter is rebuilt to turn on the key alone, with Ally's line amended to "his key", not "the letter", and the three smaller inferences flagged as inference in her mouth.

## Front 6. The whole

**Objection.** The earliest competent action by any person that ends the episode before its time is Violet's, between Day −75 and Day −40: counting the keys to the room the panel was found in (Front 1). Nothing else in the spine ends it early. Del's every route is computed and holds under R19, R22 and R27. The solicitor, the locksmith, the boat owner, the market manager and the skipper each hold something without a hypothesis to attach it to, and that is written honestly. Ruby's and Liam's routes to Del are open and produce nothing, as the rule in Part C requires. The three actions that change the episode's shape without ending it are Margo's Day 8 (Front 4), Brad's truthful answer about the box on Day 14 (Front 2), and Liam's barrel line (Front 3), and all three cluster on the same object: the money the kind line says was paid.

One foundation is under strain rather than broken. Foundation 1 rules the van money "unenforceable", yet row 4 gives Margo a list in Violet's hand naming Brad and the sum, and Margo's testimony of intent to call it in, held by an estate administered by a sister who has already litigated against the deceased. That is not an unpapered loan; it is a thinly papered one, and Day 55's write-off is a choice by the sister's solicitor, not a fact of the world. It does not end the episode (Brad is presumed dead by the time any claim could be served), but foundation 1 should say "written off" rather than "unenforceable".

**Severity.** Serious as a whole, not fatal: the device is intact, the mark as reason holds, Violet's refusal to watch holds, and Del holds. What does not yet hold is the claim that the money lie is catchable by one person, at one time, and only from Day 50.

**What would have to be true.** The Front 1 roof row, the Front 3 barrel row, the Front 4 Day 8 row and the Front 2 want row, each written into Parts A to C and paid, and the Front 5 contradiction resolved one way or the other.

## Verdict

**REWORK**, of rows and not of the device, with high confidence that the rows named above must change and moderate confidence that they change by one sentence each rather than by structure. The single finding that drives it: the spine's own load-bearing lock (C5, "a money lie only she can catch, and the episode fails here and nowhere else") depends on Brad choosing the one falsifiable money sentence when an unfalsifiable one was to hand, on a barrel three of the four watched change hands and nobody has counted on the page, and on an accountant shrugging for thirty-seven days at a missing receivables ledger. Each of those is a place where the money lie is caught earlier or by someone other than Margo, and each is currently unwritten rather than paid. The Violet roof sum is the only finding that can end the episode outright, and it is answerable by a single world fact about the roof or a single row in her book; until one of those exists, the episode's first competent reader does the arithmetic Violet never does. Fronts 5 and the locksmith are document faults, not story faults, and should be fixed in the same pass.
