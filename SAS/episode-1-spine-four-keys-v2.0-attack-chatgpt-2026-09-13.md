### 1. Violet, Day −37 to Day −19

**Objection:** Once the tube turns, Violet knows either she is interfering with her own belongings or somebody among a five-person knowledge pool entered her house. Before showing the panel to Brad, the competent act is to place the panel itself, or an independent record of its appearance, mark and provenance, beyond that pool’s control. She already visits a solicitor on Day −19 and takes the panel into town. Instead, she creates one vulnerable copy of everything, locks it somewhere Brad can reach after her death, then tells Brad exactly what event will release the key.

Her refusal to watch does not answer this. Preserving evidence would neither identify nor accuse a friend. The justification about keeping the letter from her sister also does not cover the panel. Most damagingly, telling Brad does not preserve her trust in him. It gives one of the five possible intruders the operating instructions for defeating her precaution.

**Severity: Fatal.** Brad’s successful substitution depends on Violet making the evidence uniquely vulnerable and then explaining that vulnerability to him.

**What would have to be true for this to be answered:** Violet would have to regard any independent record or custody of the panel as equivalent to accusing Brad, and that equation would need to be established strongly enough to explain why she trusts a suspected member of the pool with the release mechanism but not her solicitor with the object’s existence. It is not established now.

### 2. Brad, Day −19 to Day 14

**Objection:** On Day 14 Brad has the panel at his own bench beside the true letter and the missing book. He burns the letter and book but keeps the only physical object capable of exposing him. The act that serves him better is to burn the panel with them.

The document answers that it is the best thing he has made and he wants it. His later conduct defeats that answer. He burns it immediately when Ally describes it. Therefore possession is not an overriding compulsion. It lasts only while he believes the risk is low. But the risk is already existential on Day 14: the panel came from Violet’s locked store, several witnesses can describe it, and retaining it can prove both access and substitution. Hanging it on his wall under his new identity is worse still.

**Severity: Fatal.** Burning it on Day 14 removes the landlady’s discovery and the event that produces his arrest. His emotional attachment currently exists only to force him to preserve the solution.

**What would have to be true for this to be answered:** Brad’s need to possess and display the panel would have to be an established compulsion stronger than his instinct for survival, with a convincing reason that the public description on Day 325 breaks that compulsion when the original theft and identification risk did not.

### 3. The room, Days 14 to 47

**Objection:** Margo says:

> “Vi said she told only the four of us. She did not say only the four of us could have learned it. Why are we treating the investigation she asked us to make as proof that one of us did it?”

The letter itself distinguishes those propositions. Violet tells them to list who has been near them because somebody could have learned the location without being told directly. The genuine version expressly includes somebody watching. The forged version retains the outward investigation. Checking whether anyone overheard the four disclosures does not exclude someone observing Violet, watching the yard, following one of them, or otherwise discovering the pot.

R20 does not answer this. It establishes that nobody overheard the four tellings. It does not establish that the hiding place was unobservable. “Told only you four” is therefore not a closed pool. The document repeatedly calls it one, but repetition does not complete the inference.

**Severity: Fatal.** The room cannot competently close inward on Violet’s three sentences. Without that closure, the movement toward Liam is not an investigation corrupted by Brad. It is four adults discarding the central alternative Violet herself instructed them to investigate.

**What would have to be true for this to be answered:** The four would need evidence that nobody could have learned the hiding place except through one of them, or a compelling shared reason for rejecting that possibility. The present facts establish neither.

### 4. Margo, Days 8 to 60

**Objection:** Margo’s earliest competent action is on Day 8. She tells the sister not merely that a private book is missing, but that the missing book recorded a specific loan to Brad, that Violet had told her in August she was calling it in, and that Margo holds a dated note of that conversation.

The document’s reason for withholding this is false on its own facts. Margo does not understand the money as a gift. Violet called it “what she was owed,” named the debtor and sum, and said the loan was recorded in the missing book. Nor does being a private rather than business receivable remove it from the estate. Margo is helping the administrator identify Violet’s papers and property. Whether the debt is enforceable is for the administrator and solicitor, not for Margo to decide by silence.

**What happens:** The potential debt, the missing record and Margo’s dated note enter the estate file on Day 8. Brad is approached while alive and before the room lands on Liam. His account of settlement no longer arrives privately to Margo on Day 50. When the kind line is read, its correspondence with Brad’s estate position can be examined by others while the store can still be canvassed and the boat owner can still place Brad there that morning. The ten-day solitary lock never forms.

**Severity: Fatal.** The Day 8 silence is not privacy. It is suppression of a known potential estate asset by the accountant helping to assemble the deceased’s paperwork.

**What would have to be true for this to be answered:** Margo would have to lack the substance of the loan information, have no contact with the administrator or her solicitor, or disclose it and have the administrator expressly decline to pursue it. Parts A to G state the opposite.

### 5. Chapter 7

**Objection:** Ally airs no contradiction. Brad habitually clearing his box at seven is entirely consistent with Brad saying at quarter to six that he had “just opened” the envelope. Collection and opening are different acts. He could have collected it at seven, carried it unopened, and opened it later. The note’s instruction to act together even supplies an innocent reason for delay.

The boat owner could destroy that innocent reading by placing Brad at the store at 07:20, but Ally deliberately does not air the sighting. What remains on air is an ambiguity presented as a lie.

A listener checks the actual words and finds:

> Brad did not say the envelope reached his box that evening. He said he had just opened it.

**Severity: Fatal to the Chapter 7 suspect turn.** The advertised tell does not establish early knowledge or early access.

**What would have to be true for this to be answered:** A source would have to establish that Brad claimed the envelope itself arrived or was collected that evening, or that he invariably opened mail while standing at the box. Neither fact exists in Parts A to G.

### 6. The whole

The earliest competent action that ends the episode is Violet’s on Day −37: retain the contaminated tobacco and tin as physical evidence and have the material recorded or examined.

C1 says Del would treat the incident as contamination beside garden chemicals, with no suspect and nothing actionable. That does not answer preservation. It predicts only the initial response. If Violet dies weeks later in a locked house, a retained exhibit showing an abnormal concentration becomes prior evidence of deliberate interference. That is precisely the new evidence of an act R19 requires. The unidentified man leaving on Day 0, the changed lock and the subsequent death are then not assessed as an isolated domestic fall.

The world’s assertion that there was “no offence” fails because it converts “no identified offender” into “no potentially deliberate act.” Those are not the same proposition.

**Severity: Fatal.** A retained sample can prevent the clean accident finding or reopen it before Brad’s posthumous-letter mechanism completes its work.

**What would have to be true for this to be answered:** Testing would have to be incapable of distinguishing the dose from innocent contamination, or no medical, police or laboratory route could retain the material for later comparison. The spine currently states neither.

**REWORK. Confidence: high.** The decisive finding is Front 3. “Only four people were told” never becomes “only four people could know,” and Violet’s own instruction confirms the difference. The entire accusation engine requires four competent adults to overlook the central logical distinction in the document they are painstakingly analysing.
