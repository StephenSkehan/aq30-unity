# THE FRIENDS WITH FOUR KEYS, SPINE v2.0.2
## ADVERSARIAL REVIEW, FABLE, ROUND 2 (2026-09-13)

Read: Parts A to G in full, the header notes for v2.0.1 and v2.0.2, Parts E and F. Part H ignored. Where an objection below contradicts a stated fact of the world, the paragraph says which fact and why it fails on the document's own terms.

### FRONT 1. Violet, Day −37 to Day −19

**Strongest objection.** The −75 row says Violet does the sum for the panel "the way she later does it for the house," and that "it does not close on anyone." That is the v2.0.2 answer to round one, and it is false by the document's own Part D. The loft being the whole row's answers where the panel lay. It does not answer whose hand made it. D1 states the panel is her alphabet done better than she can do it, water gilded, with a maker's mark; row 11 and the −70 to −40 row have the trade saying "yours" twice and "have you shown your lad" once; D1 says of the four "exactly one gilds letters for a living." The set of hands in Havenbay that can write her alphabet is two: hers and her pupil's. Half the market could have put a board in a loft. Half the market cannot letter it. So the sum closes, on Day −75, on the one name, and the document has her not making it for five weeks on a reason written as pride ("she will show him last, when she has an answer to hold against his"). Pride covers the delay in showing him. It does not cover her writing that the sum fails when it does not. Then the tin arrives on Day −37: someone with her house key has tried to poison her, and someone with her alphabet and, in practice, workshop access has hidden a signed panel over her racks. Two intruders or one; the intersection of "can write my alphabet," "holds my workshop key" (Part G: Brad has for years) and "knows the meter-box spare" is one person. The competent act she does not take is the sum itself, done once, with the panel and the tin on the same page.

**Does it end the episode?** Yes, this episode. Not because she would accuse him (she would not; the premise holds there), but because a woman who has privately closed on Brad does not tell him the new pot on Day −33, does not show him the panel on Day −19, and does not lock it in front of him. Without the pot, no probe on Day −30 and no key taken in; without the key taken in, the three sentences that close the room on Day 15 do not exist; without the showing at the sheds, foundation 2 has no hour in which he learns his name is locked away. The forged letter machinery survives (he still owns her post), but the room's closure and the reason he kills both go.

**Severity.** Serious. Not fatal, because the premise already pays a refusal of this exact shape at Day −29 (she will not watch because watching tells her which). The document only fails by writing the −75 sum as arithmetic that does not close instead of arithmetic she will not hold.

**What would have to be true.** The −75 row and D1 must say the same thing: she sees that the hand is one hand, and refuses it, and that refusal is the same refusal as the key on Day −29, paid at the same price. The "half the market" line can stay only as the thing she writes to herself in order not to write the other thing. Secondary, cosmetic: Liam is in her kitchen on the Monday evening of Day −35, the day she fitted a new cylinder herself, and the document gives her no line when he asks why. The C5 note that she lied about cigarettes with total confidence suggests the line exists; it needs writing, because "she does not say why" to a man who rehung her back door and is looking at a new lock is a gap a reader will feel.

### FRONT 2. Brad, Day −19 to Day 14

**Strongest objection.** The graph row for Day −19 and the A1 row at 10:00 both state "from that hour Brad knows the store opens only on her death." No row gives him that fact by any route. What he sees at the sheds is a woman lock three things in a store and put a new padlock on. What he is told, when he asks, is a joke about her sister. Row 16 has the solicitor able to tell nobody. Row 15 has the locksmith's book, which he never sees. Nothing in Parts A to G puts into his possession the two facts his whole plan runs on: that keys will be posted to the four on her death, and that one of the four is him at his box. Without those facts the written act does not serve him. Her death without a posted key leaves the store exactly where C2 says a faked death leaves it: a locked box with his mark and her account inside, to be opened by the sister's bolt cutters or by nobody. C2's own rejected alternative ("the panel stays in the store, the four open it one day") is what killing her produces unless he knows the keys are coming. The document's method is the access ledger: a fact needs a holder, a date and a route. This fact has a holder and a date and no route, and it is the hinge of foundation 2. The −18 to −10 row compounds it: he is drafting a forgery of a letter he has not read, for a store he has no written reason to believe he will ever have a key to.

**Severity.** Serious as a hole in the document; fatal to the reason if left unwritten, because the act written (she dies and the store opens) is not an act that serves him unless he knows the disposition of the keys.

**What would have to be true.** She told him, at the sheds, in a sentence the document writes: that the four get the keys if anything happens to her and her sister gets a padlock. That is consistent with the joke about her sister, with a woman who has just lodged death instructions, and with her not suspecting him. It is one line. It must be in A1 at −19, in row 16 or a new row as a fact Brad holds from that hour, and C2's −3 row must cite it.

**Secondary, serious.** C2's verdict claims every action is coherent from inside, and one is not: the lie on Day 14 evening. All four know he clears his box at seven (chapter 7 airs it as the habit all four knew). He chooses a lie that the habit contradicts, when the truth ("I've had it since seven and waited, because she said together") is the innocent man's line and invites nothing. The document's reason ("since seven invites and you went there alone") is a guilty man's inference; no one at that table would make it of an innocent Brad. This is a good tell, and chapter 7 needs it, but it is an error he makes, not a coherent act, and C2 and pre-flight 2 should say so rather than claim a coherence the row does not have.

**Tertiary, cosmetic.** The v2.0.2 answer on "repaid, not forgiven" gives the wrong reason. It says a forgiven debt is still an estate's claim and the sister's solicitor would write to him. The sister's solicitor writes to him anyway on Day 48 about the repaid debt, and writes it off on Day 55 on his bare word; a forgiven debt would have gone the same way on the same day. The line is still defensible, but on the ground that Margo heard Violet calling it in hard in August, so "forgiven" is a louder lie to the one ear that matters than "repaid" is. C2 should carry the true reason.

### FRONT 3. The room, Days 14 to 47

**Strongest objection.** Ruby holds a proven lie by Brad about his own whereabouts (row 8: he told the four a wedding; it was a marathon in another city; she saw the results page). Liam holds Brad pricing his van for cash, spring maybe (row 9). Margo holds a cash trade with falling declared turnover (row 7, locked by confidence). D1 says in so many words that these three facts, pooled, "name a man making a name for himself somewhere else," and gives that as the reason the true letter had to go. But the three facts do not live in the letter. They live in the room, one per head, for thirty-three days of meetings whose method is to work the lists against each other and whose members are "sure it was one of them." The line the document has not answered is Ruby's, on Day 40 or Day 47, when Brad defends Liam and she wants his defence discounted: you told us a wedding and it was a marathon; what else have you told us. It is the most natural sentence in that room. Brad's answer ("I run; I didn't want the fuss") lands nowhere by itself. But it puts his weekend absences on the table, and once they are on the table Liam's cab question sits beside them, and the room has a man who was leaving. On Day 62, at Del's MP interview, three friends who say "he was planning to leave; he lied about where he went" turn a presumed drowning into a possible voluntary disappearance, and the halt's platform camera holds the 05:47 until Day 92. The document's account of the room's silence on Brad is one sentence ("Nobody lists money") and one word for Meeting 4 ("worked against each other"). Neither answers Ruby.

**Who says it.** Ruby, because she drives the room, holds the fact, and is the one Brad's defence of Liam is aimed at. Not Liam: Brad is his only defender and he would not turn on him.

**Severity.** Serious. The room is the episode's engine; a fact this ordinary, held by its driver, needs a named reason to stay unsaid, and row 8 gives none ("says nothing" is not a lock).

**What would have to be true.** Either Violet's instruction must be shown holding the room's method to outsiders hard enough that nobody lists each other's lives (and the document must then explain how the same room lands on Liam on letter-facts alone), or Ruby's silence about the marathon must be written and paid as hers: what it costs her to have checked up on a friend and to say so in front of him. Meeting 4 needs writing out, not naming.

**Secondary, cosmetic.** A3 Day 15 has the four's third sentence as "she took the key in and said nothing, and it stopped." Row 30 lists the forged letter's kept frame as "the lock, the pot and the four" and omits the key taken in. Liam's empty pot dates the taking-in, but nothing but the letter can tell them "it stopped." Row 30 must carry the sentence, or Day 15 must lose it.

### FRONT 4. Margo, Days 8 to 60

**Strongest objection.** Her earliest competent action is the first question any accountant asks when told a debtor says "settled in cash": what cash was found. Day 5 says the barrel went to the sister sealed and logged by weight, "never counted in front of anyone," and Day 33 says "nobody can check and nobody wants to." Both are true of the room and false of the estate. The barrel is estate cash; the sister's accountant has counted it by Day 45, and on Day 45 and Day 50 that man is on the telephone to Margo about this very loan. The letter says Brad repaid the van money in cash "this autumn, every penny." Violet banked nothing; cash repaid went into the barrel. Margo does Violet's business books and knows her autumn outgoings. So: the barrel's count, set against the van sum and six weeks of a life she can account for, either contains the repayment or does not. If it does not, the kind line is false on arithmetic and not on a reading, and the load-bearing lock (C5 Week 8) is gone, because what she holds is no longer "what it means" but a number. What happens then: she does not need the room and does not need to accuse; she tells the estate's accountant the count is short of the sum the deceased's own letter says was repaid, the estate does not write it off on Day 55, and when Brad goes over the cliff on Day 60 she is holding a fact, not a reading, and the guilt lock ("she chooses she was wrong about him... because her certainty rests on a reading") cannot take her. She takes it to Del in Week 9 as a forgery allegation with a number attached. Under R27 that is a tampering hypothesis; the sheds are canvassed; the boat owner names Brad at 07:20 on Day 14; the halt camera still holds Day 61 until Day 92. The episode ends about Day 80, Margo alive, Ally never arriving. Pre-flight 13 says nothing decisive rests on a small number. This does: the barrel's count relative to the van sum, and the document never states it.

**Severity.** Serious, and fatal if the number falls the wrong way. Foundation 1 ("modest") implies the count exceeds the van sum and is silent, but the document has to say so, and has to say Margo asked.

**What would have to be true.** Written in A3 at Day 45 or 50 and in C5: Margo asks the count; it is given; it exceeds the van sum by enough that it cannot distinguish repaid from unpaid against a baseline four years stale; the route is computed and produces nothing, per the sweep's own rule. Then her reading stands on the one leg that actually holds it, the book missing from a locked house. The unpaid Civic Row account, which the document leans on in row 33c, should be demoted: it was unpaid because it was not yet due (the month ended on the Thursday), and Margo knows that.

**Secondary, serious.** Foundation 1 says the van money is on no paper Violet ever signed, "thinly papered only by Margo's note of a conversation." A1 at −120 says "the list, in Violet's hand, names Brad and the sum," held in Margo's file. A dated list in the creditor's own handwriting naming debtor and sum is not a note of a conversation, and handwriting is the very thing the world later stamps as hers. Margo's competent action on Day 45 is to hand it over. The estate then holds the deceased's contemporaneous record against the debtor's bare word, and "nothing to claim" on Day 55 is no longer the solicitor's advice. It does not end the episode (Brad goes on Day 60 regardless), but it removes the Day 55 write-off and changes what Margo tells Del on Day 62 about why he might have gone. The two descriptions of the paper must be reconciled.

### FRONT 5. Chapter 7

**Strongest objection.** Ally airs: "there are eleven hours of that Monday nobody has accounted for." She knows this to be untrue when she says it. Three weeks earlier (Day 290) a consenting caller accounted for 07:20 to her in detail, and she gave it to Del. A5 at Day 311 concedes the sighting "is why Ally believes what she airs, and she never says it." That is the correct decision about the sighting and the wrong sentence about the hours. The document's rule for her is that she airs only what she can source and nothing in her voice claims what it cannot; here she sources the gap by concealing the one account she has of it. It is the protagonist's integrity, on the one chapter the document calls the suspect turn, and a listener cannot check it, which is exactly why it matters. Beside it, smaller: "had his key from seven" is inferred from habit, not sourced for that Monday (no one places him at the box at seven on Day 14), and her claim states it as done; and the row's "three survivors' own accounts" is two survivors and one dead woman's time relayed by Ruby's memory of a phone call.

**Severity.** Cosmetic in mechanism, since every fix is a wording, but the wording is the chapter's claim and the character's rule, so it must be fixed rather than noted.

**What would have to be true.** Ally's line must be "nobody has told me, on the record, where he was" or the on-air ask the document already uses for the platform woman ("if you saw him that Monday morning, write to me, not to anyone else"). The key-from-seven claim must be phrased as the habit and the inference, not the act. Margo's time must be attributed to Ruby's call. Two cosmetic checks a listener will run: the Saturday postmark from a solicitor's office (answerable if posted after the last Friday collection, which the document should say instead of "posts on Day 12"), and a Havenbay post office box lobby that is open at seven, which G should state as a fact of the town.

### FRONT 6. The whole

**Earliest competent action that ends it before its time.** The Civic Row solicitor, on or about Day 6. C1 at −3 has the solicitor's response to Violet as "advise police; cannot compel." A solicitor does not advise police over four sealed envelopes and a joke about a sister; she told him something a lawyer answers with "go to the police," which in this world can only be that someone has been in her house. Nineteen days later the same client dies unwitnessed at the foot of her own stairs with a man's back in the lane at half past ten. The solicitor learns of the death by Day 6 at the latest (he posts the envelopes after the funeral). The coroner has opened and adjourned; nothing is closed and R19 does not yet bite. Row 16 says he "could tell nobody: client confidence, contents unknown," and C8 says he "volunteers nothing to the other side." Contents unknown is true and irrelevant; the fact of the instruction and the fear she stated are what he holds, and "the other side" is the sister's litigation, not the police. The world's fact fails because the same document has her tell him enough to be advised to police. A telephone call to Del on Day 6 ("she lodged death instructions three weeks ago and I told her to speak to you") puts a sergeant on the death before the envelopes go out. Del re-interviews the four in Week 2, learns from Liam that she changed her lock and moved the spare three weeks before dying, knows from the solicitor that four keys are about to be posted, and asks each of the four when theirs arrived. Brad's "evening" is now a lie to police, and a postmaster who sees him at seven every morning breaks it inside a day. A suspicious-death officer with one suspect who lied about the key holds a tampering hypothesis under R27; the sheds are canvassed; the boat owner gives 07:20 and the board between two doors. Brad is alive and in Havenbay. The episode ends in Week 4.

**Severity.** Serious. Privilege is a real answer (it survives death and belongs to the estate, which is the sister), and a solicitor may well stay silent; but the sweep's own rule is that an open route that reaches an authority and produces nothing is only not a suppression if it is computed and written down, and this one is neither. It is a professional's silence, unpaid, on a fact the document itself put in his hands.

**What would have to be true.** Either C1 at −3 loses "advise police" and Violet tells the solicitor nothing but the instruction, so that he holds no fear and the route is empty by construction; or row 16 and C8 compute the Day 6 route explicitly, name privilege as the lock, and name the cost (a lawyer who let a client's stated fear die with her and posted her envelopes anyway). The first is cheaper and touches nothing else. The second is truer to the world and adds a small figure to chapter 3's bitterness.

### VERDICT

**PROCEED WITH CHANGES.** Confidence: high that the device holds and that every finding above is answered by a row, not by a story; moderate that the two character locks (Violet's refusal at −29 and Margo's ten days) survive readers, which is a test the document already asks for and this review cannot run. The finding that most drives the verdict is Front 6: the Civic Row solicitor was written as having advised police and as able to tell nobody, and the uncomputed route between those two cells ends the episode in Week 4 with Brad alive in Havenbay; it is the earliest, it is a professional's, and it is the document's own rule that it be written. Close behind it, and cheaper, is Front 2: Brad's knowledge on Day −19 that the store opens only on her death has no route, and foundation 2 stands on that hour. Fix those two, state the barrel's count against the van sum (Front 4), write Meeting 4 so Ruby's marathon has a reason to stay unsaid (Front 3), reword the −75 sum as a refusal rather than a failure (Front 1), and change Ally's one false sentence (Front 5). Nothing here requires the machinery to move.
