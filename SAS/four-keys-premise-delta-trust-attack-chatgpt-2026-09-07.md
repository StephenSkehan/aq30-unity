# PREMISE DELTA v0.1 (THE TRUST VERSION): CHATGPT ATTACK · 2026-09-07 · VERBATIM, THEN DISPOSITION

*Result of the attack prompt at the end of `four-keys-premise-delta-trust-v0.1-2026-09-05.md`, run by Stephen in ChatGPT on 2026-09-07. Saved unedited from his paste. Disposition and Stephen's ruling follow the verbatim block.*

---

1. **Violet's behaviour**

Strongest objection: "Probably nothing" cannot carry the behaviour assigned to it. Violet writes four posthumous letters, obtains a new padlock, distributes four keys, preserves physical evidence and constructs a procedure triggered by suspicious death. That is not embarrassment management. It is a covert death protocol. A sharp, practical woman who has reached that threshold would test the danger while alive.

Her selective silence is worse. She asks Brad, two other signwriters, a café and a cinema about the panel, so she is demonstrably willing to investigate an oddity. Yet she tells nobody about the tin because admitting to two cigarettes a day would embarrass her. The story asks the audience to believe that Violet protects a trivial lie more fiercely than her life. It also makes her consult Brad about evidence potentially connected to someone holding her key, thereby warning one possible culprit, while withholding the domestic evidence that makes the panel alarming.

**Fatal.** Violet's silence is load-bearing, and the stated reason contradicts both her character and the scale of her preparations.

It would be answered only if posthumous contingency-making were already normal behaviour for Violet, the tin carried a consequence far more serious than embarrassment, and speaking to any of the four would create a concrete danger she could reasonably foresee. None of that is currently true.

2. **The forged letter's load**

Strongest objection: the supposed chronology is impossible on the face of the two letters. The key letter says, "I've written them down and put them behind the door." Therefore the locker letter already existed when Violet later wrote that the four were "the only people I trust." On Day 19, the obvious question is:

> If Violet had already put this accusation in the locker, why did she subsequently write that she trusted all four of us?

The delta claims the room will interpret the forged letter as "later knowledge," but Violet's own wording establishes the opposite order. No dating evidence from the solicitor is needed. Basic reading establishes it.

A second unanswered question follows immediately: what new fact supposedly converted trust into certainty? Every allegation Brad includes concerns existing facts Violet already knew. The forgery supplies no intervening discovery. "I know now" is an assertion without an event behind it.

**Fatal.** The room cannot credibly accept the forgery, and everything downstream requires it to do so.

It would be answered only if the documents did not themselves establish their order and the forged letter contained a credible reason for the change in Violet's belief. As written, neither condition holds.

3. **The police route**

Strongest objection: the key letter itself creates a police matter. A recently dead woman says two unexplained incidents may matter if her death was not natural or accidental, identifies preserved physical evidence and directs four civilians to a secured location. With no prohibition against police involvement, contacting them is the ordinary response, especially for Ruby.

"You'll know what to do" is not freedom. The plot requires all four to make the same abnormal decision: wait five days, assemble privately, enter the locker and handle possible evidence without first informing police. No shared reason for that decision exists. Brad needs the delay, but the other three do not.

If even one recipient contacts police on Day 14, officers need not open a homicide investigation to ruin Brad's opportunity. They can document the letter, advise the recipients not to disturb the locker, attend while it is opened or record who accesses it. Any of those outcomes threatens recovery of the genuine letter and the evidence connecting Brad to the locker.

**Fatal.** The delta removes the explicit police barrier without replacing the behavioural function it performed.

It would be answered only if each recipient had a compelling, character-specific reason not to contact police before the opening, and the chosen delay arose naturally from circumstances rather than from the plot needing Brad to reach the locker first.

4. **The reader**

The change loses the hook and delays it. "One of her four closest friends may have been planning to kill her" immediately provides murder, danger, a closed suspect circle and a question. "These were the only four people she trusted" provides relationship setup. The keys and locked door promise undisclosed information, but the listener does not yet know that the information contains a crime, a threat or even a meaningful mystery.

The later reversal may produce a sharp beat when the forged letter is read, but it does not retroactively strengthen the opening minute. The listener must first sit through names, trust and document administration on the promise that something interesting is behind a door. The tested version gives them the interesting thing immediately.

**Serious.** This may not destroy the episode, but it deliberately replaces a tested true-crime hook with deferred setup.

It would be answered only if blind listeners consistently remain engaged through the trust passage and identify the locked door itself as the reason they need to continue. Existing approval of the former opening is evidence for the version being removed, not for this replacement.

5. **The chapter 7 tell**

"Trust, then accusation" is worse than "thought, then knew." It is louder, and the letter wording makes it logically immediate.

An ordinary player does not need Ally's chapter 7 comparison. The key letter already says the locker account has been written and deposited. The same key letter then declares trust in all four. When the locker letter accuses one of them, the contradiction is sitting in front of everyone on Day 19.

If Liam, Ruby and Margo fail to notice it, they do not look traumatised or credulous. They look unable to follow two short letters. The eventual chapter 7 reveal then risks producing irritation rather than satisfaction because Ally is credited with discovering something the player saw five chapters earlier.

The old certainty gap was quieter. "I think" becoming "I know" could pass as an emotional or evidential progression until dating exposed it. The trust gap does not wait for dating because the key letter supplies the sequence itself.

**Fatal.** It destroys the intended delayed tell and damages the intelligence of the central cast.

It would be answered only if the apparent order of the two statements were genuinely ambiguous until chapter 7 and ordinary chapter 2 readers did not identify the contradiction. The current text fails both requirements.

**REJECT. High confidence.** The decisive finding is that the key letter explicitly says the locker material has already been written and deposited, so the forged accusation necessarily predates Violet's later declaration of trust. That single sentence breaks the room's belief, the Day 19 sequence and the chapter 7 reveal.
