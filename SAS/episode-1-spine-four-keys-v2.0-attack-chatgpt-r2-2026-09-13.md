### 1. Violet, Day −37 to Day −19

**Objection:** Violet does not place a camera covering the hidden pot after the tube is turned.

She need not restore the key or provoke another entry. A camera watching the now-empty pot records Brad checking it on Day −9. That identifies the one friend seeking a spare whose location was disclosed only after the suspected poisoning. It also connects him to both disturbances before she shows him the panel.

The stated reason, that watching would tell her which friend it was, does not survive Violet’s other behaviour. She changes the lock, controls disclosure, records times, constructs a posthumous inquiry, cuts four keys and engages a solicitor. She is already investigating. She merely postpones the answer until she cannot act on it.

**Severity: Fatal.**

It ends the episode on Day −9. For this to be answered, Violet’s refusal to know must be powerful enough to override her demonstrated methodical competence and instinct for self-preservation. That contradiction must be believable in the woman herself, not certified by the sweep.

### 2. Brad, Day −19 to Day 14

**Objection:** At 17:30 on Day 14, Brad gives the one answer he has spent fourteen days preparing not to give.

When Liam says his note arrived in the afternoon, Brad should answer with his prepared truth: he found his at seven and waited because Violet said they must go together. That answer is innocent, consistent with his habits and impossible to disprove. Instead, he invents an afternoon sort he knows does not exist.

Liam’s unexpected wording does not explain this. It gives Brad no reason to match the timing of yard post with the timing of a PO box. More importantly, his prepared answer already accommodates any time at which the others received theirs. The unforeseen question does not defeat the preparation.

**Severity: Fatal.**

This is not merely a mistake. It is the author manufacturing the later tell by making the most prepared person in the story discard a perfect answer for a needless, checkable lie. For it to be answered, Brad must have an established conversational compulsion to mirror another person’s account even when doing so contradicts facts he knows and a line he has rehearsed for two weeks.

### 3. The room, Days 14 to 47

**Objection:** Margo says:

> “She told only us where the key was. That doesn’t mean only we could find it. Who watched Vi put it there?”

The document establishes that the pot cannot be seen from outside the yard. It does not establish that nobody else could enter the yard, follow Violet through the gate, watch her while the gate was open or search beneath an obvious step. A gate latched from inside is not an impermeable boundary. “Whoever watched it was already in” does not mean “whoever watched it was one of us.”

The letter proves whom Violet told. It does not prove everyone who could have learned independently. Its own proposed outward explanation, that somebody watched one of the four use the spare, inexplicably excludes Violet herself.

The statement that every route runs through the five is therefore not a fact of the world. It is an inference, and the physical facts given do not support it.

**Severity: Fatal.**

The pool does not close. Liam does not become the necessary answer. For this to be answered, the world must establish that no outsider could have entered or observed the yard, searched the pot, or watched Violet place or use the key during the relevant period. The current spatial facts do not establish any of those things.

### 4. Margo, Days 8 to 60

**Objection:** Her earliest outcome-changing competent action is on Day 50, immediately after the accountant reports Brad’s answer.

Before then, she has missing paperwork and an unsurprising statement in Violet’s letter. On Day 50 she has the complete pattern: Violet intended to collect the debt, the only record disappeared from a locked house on the night Violet died, the posthumous letter conveniently says the debt was repaid, and Brad has just relied upon the same proposition with the estate.

At that moment Margo can state a specific tampering hypothesis to Del. Under R27, that permits a canvass of the store. The boat owner then identifies Brad as having opened Violet’s door before the group and moved a flat parcel between stores. This occurs while Brad is still alive and locally reachable.

The claim that Del would have “nothing” confuses insufficient evidence for a charge with insufficient reason to investigate. Margo does not need to prove murder. She needs only to articulate why the document may have been substituted. Her facts do that.

**Severity: Serious.**

It changes the outcome even without an immediate arrest. Brad becomes the known pre-opening visitor before his disappearance, the letter cannot continue pointing cleanly at Liam, and Margo cannot later interpret Brad’s disappearance as proof that she killed him with suspicion. For this to be answered, Margo’s fear of accusing Brad must credibly prevent her from reporting document tampering even after she recognises that the missing book and Brad’s estate answer converge on the same convenient sentence.

### 5. Chapter 7

**Objection:** Ally cannot source this assertion:

> “His post was in his box at seven, as it was every morning of his life.”

Her sources establish only that there is normally one overnight sort, that Brad habitually cleared his box at seven, and that he claimed his letter was absent that morning. They do not establish that this particular envelope was placed in his box before seven.

The envelope has no recorded delivery scan. A Saturday postmark does not prove Monday-morning placement. Sorting delays, missorting and exceptional handling remain possible. The counter clerk can disprove the existence of an official afternoon sort, but cannot prove the item’s presence in Brad’s box. “Every morning of his life” is also plainly beyond anything the witnesses can know.

A listener checking the claim finds that Brad gave a wrong explanation for a possible late item. They do not find proof that he lied about seeing the letter.

**Severity: Serious.**

Chapter 7 overstates its evidence at exactly the moment Ally is supposed to earn the suspect turn. For this to be answered, there must be item-specific evidence establishing that Brad’s envelope was physically in his box before seven, not merely evidence of ordinary sorting practice.

### 6. The whole

The earliest competent action that definitively ends the episode before its time is Violet placing surveillance over the empty pot on Day −29.

Brad checks the pot on Day −9. The recording identifies him as the person seeking the newly relocated spare after the second disturbance. Violet no longer has an undifferentiated fear involving four trusted friends. She has Brad, caught performing an act he has no innocent reason to perform. Everything dependent upon her remaining uncertain through Day 0 collapses.

Keeping the contaminated tobacco on Day −37 is earlier and would materially change the later death investigation, but it does not identify Brad. The camera does.

**Severity: Fatal.**

For there to be no such action, Violet’s refusal to watch must hold as an overriding character fact despite every contrary sign of her procedural competence.

**REWORK. Confidence: high.** The single finding driving the verdict is the room’s false arithmetic. Violet’s statement establishes whom she told, not everyone who could have discovered the pot. Because that distinction is available to four adults at the table, the closed pool does not survive competent thought, and the accusation machinery built upon it never starts.
