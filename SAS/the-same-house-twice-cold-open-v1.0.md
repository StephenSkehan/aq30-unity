# THE OPENING

*Rain on the studio window. The record light clicks on.*

**ALLY:** Every episode of this show starts with a voice. A message on the tip line, a tape, somebody on the record. This one starts with a call I can't play you. The woman who made it asked me not to record her, so I didn't, and what you get instead is me, my notes, and you deciding whether I'm telling it straight.

She rang yesterday afternoon. She gave me her name, which took some nerve, and you're not getting it. She's a care worker. Twice a week she visits a woman called Winnie. Winnie is eighty-one. Rich, sharp, and rude in the way you're allowed to be when you made your own money and have outlived everyone who ever told you what to do with it. She lives up in Highcliff, where the streets are quiet and nobody knocks. She pays for two visits a week and spends both of them explaining that she doesn't need them. As of this week, the visits are cancelled. When the office rang to check, Winnie backed it. My caller heard what she told them: I'm eighty-one, I am not a parcel, stop signing for me.

But my caller wasn't ringing about Winnie. She was ringing about the man who does Winnie's shopping.

His name is Colin. The garden first, years ago. Then the shopping. This year, her heart pills, because the boxes are fiddly and he's there anyway. He remembers how she takes her tea. He is, by every account I have, a genuinely lovely man, and Winnie likes him. Everyone likes him. My caller liked him. Liked. Past tense. Then a long pause, and: I've met him before.

Five years ago she looked after a man in that same house. Same garden, same kind man carrying the shopping up the steps. She watched the man she cared for get tired, then confused, and then she watched him die. Later she heard the house had gone to the man who carried the shopping. She says if you look at that house, just the house, you'll find it happened before that too. Same house. Twice.

She told the people she works for, months ago, in writing. The answer came back: not yours to discuss. Not with anyone. Not with her. So she rang a podcast instead, and made sure there would be no tape of her doing it.

Here's the part I can't get past. Winnie isn't missing. Nobody is holding her. She's at home, furious about the fuss, and the one person in Havenbay who is kind to her without pity is the man my caller says is killing her. If I knock on that door with all of this, I know exactly what happens, because it's what I'd do. She picks him.

Maybe my caller is wrong. Maybe she's seen one bad thing too many and started seeing it everywhere. That happens, in her job and in mine. So I'm not going to Highcliff to rescue anybody. Winnie would hand me my head, and she'd have a point. I'm going to look at one house, and take my time, and tell you what I find.

This is Echoes of Havenbay. I'm Ally Quinn. Episode one. The Same House Twice.

# WHAT IT GETS RIGHT

Checked against the step-4 brief before anything else was said about it.

- **Three named people, not four.** Ally, Winnie, Colin. The caller is *my caller*. The doctor is absent.
- **No banned vocabulary.** *Her heart pills*, and nothing clinical or procedural anywhere.
- **No em dashes.**
- **Ally never addresses something that cannot answer.** She talks to the listener, which is the frame.
- ★ **It turns the constraint into the hook.** *"This one starts with a call I can't play you."* The no-tape rule was a canon obligation and it became the first interesting thing in the episode.
- ★ **It answers the standing voice flag.** A reader said Ally's phrasing reads as weird across two separate works and that the noir styling had to go if it was the cause. There is no pastiche here. *"Here's the part I can't get past."*
- ★ **It states the episode's thesis in two sentences and is honest about Ally having no power.** *"If I knock on that door with all of this, I know exactly what happens, because it's what I'd do. She picks him."*
- **It keeps her doubt as an engine rather than decoration.** *"Maybe she's seen one bad thing too many."*
- **It promises no powers.** *"I'm not going to Highcliff to rescue anybody."*

**Lines to protect through any rewrite:** *I am not a parcel, stop signing for me* · *Liked. Past tense.* · *not yours to discuss. Not with anyone. Not with her.* · *She picks him.* · *Same house. Twice.*

# NOTES ON THE FILE

**Fable's own note, and it is correct.** Winnie cannot lawfully be in this opening in her own voice, because Ally has not met her on Day 1, so her entire personhood rides on two fragments of reported speech. **If readers do not feel her, that seam is where**, and it cannot be closed until lead 5 puts her in a doorway.

**My note.** The opening asserts that Colin is killing her and never shows a symptom. Spine v1.2 has the attempt already under way and Winnie visibly declining, and **present-tense danger is this premise's whole answer to "no jeopardy, too safe."** One clause would fix it.

**Alternative title offered by Fable:** *The Kind Man*, if the man belongs at the front rather than the house. Not ruled.
