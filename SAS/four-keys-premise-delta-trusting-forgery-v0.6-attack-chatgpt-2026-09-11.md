1. **Brad’s candour**

**Objection:** Volunteering the debt does not neutralise it. It gives Margo the first testable contradiction at the table. On Day 19, Margo knows Violet wrote everything down and knows the private loan book was missing from the books delivered on Day 8.

Margo says:

> “Half repaid in cash? Vi wrote down every dollar. Where is the book that proves your number?”

That question places together the only debtor, a dead lender, an unverified cash claim, a missing record and a man without an alibi. Speaking first may make Brad look composed. It does not make him the last person suspected.

**Severity:** Fatal to the room’s present convergence on Liam. The text asserts the psychological effect of Brad’s candour but does not earn it.

**Answered only if:** Margo has a compelling reason not to ask that question on Day 19, and the room has a credible basis for clearing Brad after considering the missing book, his motive and his lack of an alibi.

2. **Margo’s reading**

**Objection:** Her earliest competent action is on or immediately after Day 50, when she learns that Brad has paid the self-declared balance and the estate intends to close the matter. She tells the sister’s accountant that the amount cannot be verified because the validating book disappeared after Violet’s death and the debtor supplied the only surviving number.

The estate might still be unable to recover another dollar, but it cannot honestly treat the payment as verification. Margo can also tell the room or Del. “It is not proof” does not mean “it cannot be reported.” Suspicious circumstances are precisely what people report so that somebody else can seek proof.

Brad cannot reliably read her silence at meeting six. The true locker letter contains nothing about money. The estate’s demand does not identify Margo as suspicious because Brad publicly offered on Day 19 to pay the estate. Her withdrawal at the table could mean grief, exhaustion, disgust with the meetings or doubt about Liam. The statement that Brad “knows exactly what Margo knew and when” gives him information the story has not transmitted.

This creates a closed contradiction. If Margo acts competently, she is no longer silent. If she remains silent, Brad cannot know that her silence conceals the missing-book inference.

**Severity:** Fatal. Margo’s silence must both conceal her conclusion from everyone else and reveal it to Brad strongly enough to drive Day 60. It currently cannot perform both functions.

**Answered only if:** The text supplies a concrete signal that communicates Margo’s specific inference to Brad without communicating it to the room, and also establishes why her earliest competent action is not to warn the estate, Del or the others.

3. **One glass and her habits**

**Objection:** The habit excludes strangers, not outsiders. Violet’s solicitor is the first named outsider the room has not cleared. He is male, currently active in her life and trusted with unusual post-death instructions. The text does not establish whether she had known him for years. Her trade contacts create the same unresolved class. She had recently carried the panel to the café, cinema and two other signwriters.

“Four people were her whole week” does not establish that they were the only men she had known for years or might admit after dark. Nor does one glass exclude a brief caller to whom she offered nothing.

The police could weigh the habit as soon as the friends reported it. The room possesses firsthand confidence about Violet, but no exclusive evidentiary fact. More importantly, neither the police nor the room can convert “she admitted only people known for years” into “she admitted only these four.”

**Severity:** Fatal to the closed pool. The device converts a necessary condition into a sufficient one.

**Answered only if:** Every longstanding man Violet might admit is identified and competently excluded, or some further established fact reduces the possible admitted visitors specifically to the four.

5. **The showing at the store door**

**Objection:** Brad acts during the one-day custody window the new chronology creates. On Day −19 he watches Violet operate the new padlock with a key. The keys are not lodged until the next day. Brad is already the covert entrant who knows and uses her house spare.

A competent Brad enters while Violet is out, finds one of the padlock keys, copies it and returns it before she lodges the four originals. He now has the fifth key that sections 4 and 8 insist cannot exist. He can enter the store during an ordinary working morning before Day 0, discover the true letter and remove the panel without cutting, picking or visibly damaging anything.

This need not move the murder on Day 0 because the debt confrontation can still occur then. It does destroy the fourteen-day constraint, the Day 14 swap and the Chapter 7 inference built from his early receipt of the posted key.

**Severity:** Fatal to the post-death mechanism as written.

**Answered only if:** The complete physical custody of every padlock key from cutting through lodgment is established, with no period in Violet’s house or elsewhere that Brad’s existing covert access could exploit.

6. **The whole**

**Objection:** The earliest dated competent action is Brad’s on Day −19. The instant Violet shows him his panel and locks it behind a padlock whose key remains in her custody until the following day, he knows that the object exists, where it is and that she considers it inexplicable. His demonstrated access to her house gives him a route to the key before lodgment.

That action ends this episode before its intended time. Brad can sanitise the store before Violet dies. The four therefore cannot encounter the exact forged arrangement on Day 14, and Ally cannot later derive the Chapter 7 turn from Brad’s seven-hour advantage.

If that access is conclusively closed, the next earliest competent action is Margo’s Day 19 question about the missing book. That prevents the room from treating Brad’s candour as clearance.

**Severity:** Fatal.

**Answered only if:** The Day −19 key window is physically impossible and Margo’s Day 19 failure to connect the debt claim with the missing debt record is behaviourally compelled.
