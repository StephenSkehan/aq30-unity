## PASTE FROM HERE

You wrote five people for us: Nell Casey, Owen Pask, Sian Reddy, Margo Lisk and Bram Tolley. They are attached. They have been read and they are good, and the people who read them want to keep nearly everything. This is a revision pass, not a rewrite. **Keep the voice, the Tuesdays, the two cigarettes, the four-in-the-morning call, the receipts bag, the cheque and the note.** Cut nothing you were told you could keep.

**You still do not know which one did it, and we still are not telling you.** Everything below applies to all four of them equally. If you find yourself doing more to one of them than the others, stop and even it out.

### Five changes

**1. Every one of the four hides something from the other three.** Not a crime. A thing about themselves they have decided the others do not get: a talent, a history, a debt, a person, an appetite. Nell may or may not have known. It should be the kind of thing that, if it came out, would make the others say "I had no idea" rather than "I knew it". One each, worked into the passage, never announced.

**2. Every one of the four is better at something than they let on.** Related to the first but not the same. One of them can do a thing well and has chosen to be seen as ordinary at it, or has never shown it at all. Do not tell us why. Give it to all four, and make at least two of the four things completely harmless.

**3. Every one of the four gets one habit that means nothing.** A small physical or verbal habit, the kind you would not notice until somebody pointed it out, and that a reader could look back on later and think they had seen something in it. It must be genuinely innocent on the page. If it reads as sinister, it is wrong. Four habits, four people, and they must be different in kind: one physical, one verbal, one to do with objects, one to do with time.

**4. Bram is a working signwriter now, not a teacher.** Nell taught him and he is in the trade professionally: his own van, his own customers, some of them hers. Keep how they met and the Tuesday evenings. **Cut the line that he is the only one who can do her hand.** Nobody in this group is known for being able to imitate her lettering, and nobody has ever tried in front of the others. Replace it with what a second signwriter in a small harbour town actually is to the first one: a rival, a favour, a threat to prices, a person she sent the jobs she did not want. Choose one.

**5. Owen and Sian have twelve years of not liking each other. Give it a date.** One incident, not a pattern, that both of them remember differently, and that Nell was present for. Write it from neither side. Two lines at most, in whichever passage it sits better.

### Nell

One addition only: **Nell had a thing she owned that nobody else had.** Not valuable, not secret, just hers, one of a kind, and the sort of object a friend would recognise anywhere. Give it to her in one sentence and do not explain it. We may or may not use it.

### Constraints, unchanged

Two hundred words each at the outside; you may go to two hundred and forty for the four friends if the additions need it. No em dashes anywhere. Nothing gore-led. Grounded and contemporary. Do not use the names on the banned list from the first brief. No plot, no timeline, no theory about who did it.

### Deliverable

The five passages, revised, in full. Then one line only: **the change you made that you are least sure of, and why.**
