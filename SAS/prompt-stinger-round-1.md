## PASTE FROM HERE

Write **twenty** episode ideas for a true-crime podcast set in a contemporary mid-size coastal city. **A title and one sentence each. Nothing else.**

These are deliberately disposable. **Nobody is going to develop nineteen of them.** One reader will go down the list, and the only thing that matters is whether a line makes him want to know what happened next. **Write for that, not for a review.**

### Format, and it is strict because the reader's attention is the scarce thing

- **Title: six words or fewer.** Evocative, specific, and it must not give away the sentence.
- **Stinger: ONE sentence, thirty-five words or fewer.**
- **No proper nouns.** No names, no places, no institutions by name. A name in a one-liner costs a reader something and pays nothing back yet.
- **No investigator, no host, no podcast in the sentence.** State the case, not who looks into it.
- Number them 1 to 20. **No commentary, no rationale, no notes, no headers.** Just the twenty.

### What every one must contain

**A death.** Somebody is dead in all twenty.

**And for this batch, a murder in the clear majority of them.** These are aimed at a pilot episode, and twelve testers rejected the last pilot with the words *"no jeopardy, too safe, not real true crime."* A death ruled an accident that turns out to be a killing counts. A few non-murder deaths are welcome if they are strong; they will simply be filed as later-episode material.

### Five things to aim at

**Drawn from an independent evidence-led study of why true crime holds an audience. These are aims, not gates. Nothing here forbids anything.**

**1. A consequential question, not a crime category.** *"A murder happened"* is subject matter. The engine is a concrete uncertainty attached to a human consequence. Is the convicted person innocent. Who benefits from the accepted account. Is a missing person still reachable. Did someone trusted knowingly leave people exposed. **State the question and the consequence in the sentence.**

**2. Make the antagonist a person whose choice you want to understand.** The strongest true-crime-specific research found that curiosity about dangerous minds predicted consumption more strongly than curiosity about bodily violation. **The audience wants access to a person, not a situation.** A retention schedule is not a villain.

**3. Name a person and a threatened future, not a demographic.** A victim is not compelling because they are called kind or undeserving. **They become present through routine, work, humour, friction and relationships, and the harm lands because you understand what was interrupted.** You have one sentence, so this may be a single concrete detail. Make it earn its place.

**4. The strongest movement is reclassification.** An offender story becomes an institutional one. A disappearance becomes a chosen escape. **A solved case becomes a failure of the record.** The best stingers contain the moment the accepted account stops holding.

**5. Let the turn be a person, a lie or an identity.** Something a reader can be angry at or fascinated by. A correction that arrives as arithmetic is true and inert.

### Two prohibitions, and only two

**1. No elderly person as the central victim or the central figure.** Ruled for the pilot specifically. *"I don't think we can base episode 1 on an old lady or man. They may be fine down the track when we've hooked the player, but not for episode 1."*

**2. No word a general reader would have to look up.** No trade, legal, medical or procedural vocabulary anywhere in the twenty. A test reader hit a wall four times in twenty-one on single words. **If a sentence needs a specialist noun to make sense, it is the wrong sentence.**

### Make your own twenty diverse

**No two of your twenty should turn on the same central question.** If four of them are wrongful convictions, three of them are wasted. Vary who is dead, who is dangerous, who is left, and what kind of wrong the story is about.

### What you are not being asked for

No canon, no city detail, no research, no defensibility, no method, no access route, no structure. **Whether any of these can actually be built is somebody else's job, later, and worrying about it now is how the last field of forty-five produced one survivor.** Write the twenty most interesting one-line ideas you can.
