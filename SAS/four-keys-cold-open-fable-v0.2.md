This is Echoes of Havenbay. I'm Ally Quinn.

Nine months ago, on a Monday night, Violet Moore died at the foot of her own stairs. She was forty-four. She painted letters for a living: shop fronts, boat names, once, for a bet, the side of a bus. There was a post-mortem. The coroner called it an accident.

Two weeks after she died, four people got a key in the post, and a letter in her hand. Liam Bryce. Ruby Walker. Margo Rivera. Brad Collins. The letter said she thought one of them was planning to kill her. She did not know which, and might be wrong. She told them not to go to the police, and why. What she had found was behind a door only the four of them could open.

They opened it together, on the Saturday. Behind it was a second letter. That one said she knew. Beside it, something of hers. The survivors won't say what.

Seven weeks after the funeral, Brad Collins drove to Kestrel Head and was never found. Five months later, Margo Rivera's car was in the same car park. Her body came into the harbour three days after that.

Liam Bryce and Ruby Walker each went to the police within the week. Not to accuse. To say: I am frightened of him. I am frightened of her. A sergeant I trust in Harbor Ward told me they looked, properly, for six weeks. The letters are Violet's, lodged with her solicitor three weeks before she died, and the coroner's finding stands. They found nothing.

Now those two are taking each other apart on the front page of the Gazette, over a dead woman's letter. I wasn't there for any of it. Ruby has agreed to talk. Liam hasn't, yet. Before I record anyone, I'll ask them, on this microphone.

I don't know what the police couldn't find. But I'd like to sit with the people who loved her, and listen.

Goodnight, Harbour.
