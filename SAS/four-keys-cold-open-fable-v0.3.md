Nine months ago, Violet Moore died at the bottom of her stairs. She was forty-four. She painted signs all over this city, shopfronts, boat names, once the side of a bus. The coroner called it an accident.

Two weeks later, four people got a key in the post, and a letter in her handwriting. Liam Bryce. Ruby Walker. Margo Rivera. Brad Collins. The four people she loved most in the world.

The letter said she thought one of them was planning to kill her. She didn't know which, and might be wrong. She told them not to go to the police, and why. What she'd found was behind a door only the four of them could open.

They rang each other that night and agreed nobody would go alone. The first day all four could stand at that door together was the Saturday. Behind it, a second letter. This one said she knew. Beside it, something of hers. The two still living won't say what.

Seven weeks after the funeral, Brad Collins drove to Kestrel Head, the place people here go when they don't mean to come back. He was never found. Five months on, Margo Rivera's car was in the same car park. Her body came into the harbour three days later.

Liam and Ruby each went to the police within the week. Not to accuse. To say: I am frightened of him. I am frightened of her. A sergeant I trust in Harbor Ward tells me they looked, properly. The letters are really hers, and they found nothing.

Now those two are taking each other apart in the Gazette, over a dead woman's letter. I wasn't there for any of it. Ruby's agreed to talk. Liam hasn't, yet. Nobody's on this show without saying yes first, and you'll hear them say it.

I can't promise answers. But I keep coming back to those keys.

This is Echoes of Havenbay, I'm Ally Quinn. Goodnight for now, Harbour. Sleep tight.
