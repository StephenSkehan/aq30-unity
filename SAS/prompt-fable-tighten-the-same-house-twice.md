You wrote the cold open for *The Same House Twice*. It worked. It is being kept, and one thing about it has changed that changes what tightening means.

### The change: this is no longer Episode One

Stephen has ruled it out of the pilot slot and into **episode three or four**. His words: *"This story is not going to be a candidate for ep1 but it certainly is looking good for #3 or #4."*

**That is not a demotion of the writing.** It moved for a medium reason, not a craft one. As an Episode 1 opening it ran about three minutes fifty against a first-interaction budget of well under a minute, in a game where a fifth of installs are gone inside two minutes. **A podcast listener has already chosen to listen. A mobile installer forty seconds after download has not.**

★ **As episode three, most of that pressure disappears.** No tutorial, no first-interaction budget, no install cliff, and a listener who has already finished two cases and will happily sit with Ally.

### What survives the reassignment, and it is the whole brief

**One. Your listener is not new any more, so everything that orients them can go.**

Your first paragraph explains what the show does: *"Every episode of this show starts with a voice. A message on the tip line, a tape, somebody on the record."* By episode three they know. **The device inside it is excellent and must survive** — *"This one starts with a call I can't play you"* is the best opening move in the piece and it does double duty as hook and as canon. **Keep the move, lose the primer.**

The sign-off has the same issue. They know who Ally is and what the show is called. Keep whatever cadence you need to land the ending and drop the introductions. **And the line currently says "Episode one," which is now wrong.**

**Two. The jeopardy arrives too late even for a returning listener.** The murder is not explicit until about two and a half minutes, and your two best lines land at 2:30 and 3:32. **Get the danger in front of them inside the first forty-five seconds.** This is a re-sequence, not a rewrite: the material already exists, it is just in the wrong order for a screen.

**Three. Winnie is never shown to be unwell.** The opening asserts he is killing her and we see no symptom. The spine has the attempt already under way and Winnie visibly declining, and **present-tense danger is this premise's entire answer to a tester panel that called the pilot too safe.** One clause probably does it.

### The target

**About 300 to 330 words, roughly two minutes fifteen.** Down from 537. That is a real cut and it will cost something, so spend the cut on orientation, not on character.

**If you land at 350 and it is better than 310, take the 350.** The number is a direction, not a gate.

### Do not lose these

- *I'm eighty-one, I am not a parcel, stop signing for me.*
- *Liked. Past tense.*
- *not yours to discuss. Not with anyone. Not with her.*
- *She picks him.*
- *Same house. Twice.*
- The doubt: that the caller might be wrong, and that this happens in her job and in yours.
- The refusal of power: *I'm not going to Highcliff to rescue anybody.*

**And the voice, which is the reason you have this job.** A reader had said Ally's phrasing read as weird across two separate works and that the noir styling had to go if it was causing it. **Your draft fixed that and nothing in this pass may undo it.** No pastiche, no detective cadence, one person talking to one person in a pair of earbuds.

### The constraints that have not moved

Three or four named people at the outside. No procedural or clinical vocabulary: *her heart pills*, never anything past that. **No em dashes anywhere.** Ally never speaks to something that cannot answer. No body shown, nothing gore-led.

### The seam you identified, and you were right about it

You noted that Winnie cannot lawfully be in this opening in her own voice, because Ally has not met her on day one, so her whole personhood rides on two fragments of reported speech.

**That stands and cannot be fixed here.** It closes at lead five when she is in a doorway. **Do not solve it by inventing a meeting.** If a tighter version makes the seam worse rather than better, say so in one line and we will decide whether to spend more words on her rather than fewer.
