# PROMPT KIT — new episode pitch generation (v1)

**Status:** CURRENT, Stephen-approved 2026-08-24 · **Use:** paste the block below into an external model that has the AQ30 source pack loaded, or adapt it in-session.

**What it is.** A reusable execution-ready prompt for generating murder-led episode pitch candidates that arrive complete: full solution, evidence chain, agency map, criminal economy, production reality and a self-adversarial finding, rather than a seductive one-paragraph concept with an empty middle.

**Provenance.** Drafted by ChatGPT, hardened in review, finalised by Stephen 2026-08-24. Three of the review's proposals were deliberately softened in the final version and the reasons are worth keeping:

1. **The excluded recording shape is a scoped ideation boundary, not a proven diagnosis.** Tester evidence established that The Listener lacked jeopardy. It did not establish that "reconstruct a damaged recording to locate a missing person" was the causal mechanism. That was an inference. It is excluded here to stop a new candidate occupying the same play pattern, not because it has been proved fatal.
2. **The occupied-territory list is a working boundary supplied through review, not canon.** It is derived from the v3.1 pitch document, which the prompt deliberately withholds. Do not promote it to a source.
3. **The agency cost formula is a floor and a ceiling, not a template.** An earlier draft prescribed three class A, two class B and one class C, which would invite six decisions manufactured to fill a quota. The final rule is: at least four decisions, at least two class A, at most one class C.

**Before a pitch leans on it, check one thing.** Gate 3 offers a third clock structure, the session-contained window, which is new and good. We have no shipped tech for a timed in-session sequence. Confirm feasibility before accepting a pitch whose jeopardy depends on it.

**Related standing rules.** `critical-reviewer-charter.md` controls assessment. `pivot-brief-2026-08-23.md` controls what is currently true. `episode-1-vo-lock-v1.1-WIP.md` controls prose.

## THE PROMPT (copy from here)

# TASK: DEVELOP THREE NEW ALLY QUINN MURDER-PILOT CHALLENGERS

Act as a senior crime-thriller story architect, interactive narrative designer and constructive adversary for *Ally Quinn: True Crime Merge*.

Develop three genuinely distinct murder-pilot candidates designed to challenge the existing G, H and I premises on:

* cold-open compulsion;
* present-tense jeopardy;
* true-crime credibility;
* fair-play mystery construction;
* Havenbay specificity;
* podcast and evidence-medium integration;
* meaningful but affordable player agency;
* emotional consequence;
* compulsion to continue.

Do not call any new pitch superior merely because it satisfies this prompt. Tester behaviour, not internal judgement, establishes superiority.

## 0. PURPOSE AND DECISION CONTEXT

Three incumbent premises are awaiting tester evidence:

* G, "The Confession"
* H, "The Overnight Girl"
* I, "The Last Caller"

The new pitches are potential challengers for the same comparative table-read. They are not automatically entitled to join it.

At the end:

1. Force-rank your three new pitches.
2. Cut one of them.
3. Decide whether either remaining challenger earns a place alongside G, H and I.
4. State plainly if neither deserves to expand the slate.

Judge the new premises intrinsically before comparing them with the incumbents. Do not design them merely to attack a particular incumbent.

The cold opens must be suitable for blind tester presentation:

* readable aloud in approximately sixty to seventy-five seconds;
* presented without title or pitch letter;
* understandable with no prior Havenbay knowledge;
* free of unnecessary proper nouns;
* comparable in length and information density.

The decisive tester questions remain:

* "Which one do you need to hear the rest of?"
* "Would you come back tomorrow for it?"

## 2. WORKING ANTI-DUPLICATION BOUNDARY

The following occupied-territory list was supplied through an external review. Treat it as a working ideation boundary, not as a replacement canon source. Do not infer further incumbent details beyond this list.

### Taken by "The Listener"

* Nightly voicemails from a lonely regular caller.
* A wordless or interrupted recording as the inciting artefact.
* Reconstructing ambient audio to determine where someone lives.
* A woman already gone when the investigation begins.

### Taken by G, "The Confession"

* A confession given in person to a trusted mentor and recorded by him.
* The mentor becoming the last living witness and next target.
* A 1989 harbour death staged as a drunken fall.
* Paid silence and a payer known only by voice.
* A keeper who preserved receipts for decades.

### Taken by H, "The Overnight Girl"

* A competent adult executing a rehearsed childhood escape plan.
* A coded telephone ritual functioning as a dead man's switch.
* A body in cold storage.
* A patient professional moving through a trust network one polite conversation at a time.
* Breadcrumbs keyed to episodes in the podcast archive.

### Taken by I, "The Last Caller"

* A Tip Line confession interrupted mid-name by someone in the room.
* Room-tone audio forensics used to identify an anonymous caller.
* A search for a childhood witness now grown.

### Excluded shape for this ideation round

Do not use this structure:

> A damaged or interrupted recording is reconstructed through audio analysis to locate a person who is already missing.

Tester evidence has not proved that this structure caused *The Listener's* failure. It is excluded here to prevent another candidate from occupying substantially the same play pattern.

Audio remains available as evidence. It must not form the spine of another locate-the-missing-person reconstruction.

## 3. GENERATION METHOD

Before producing the answer, explore at least twelve materially different premise seeds.

Do not reveal private chain-of-thought or pad the response with discarded concepts.

Use this funnel:

1. Reject anything derivative of the occupied territory.
2. Reject anything that fails an objective kill-gate.
3. Reject anything whose hook collapses under mobile-game timing.
4. Reject anything whose agency would be unaffordable for a solo developer.
5. Develop only the three strongest survivors into full pitch packages.

Do not use numeric scores or percentages.

Cheap production cannot rescue a weak story. Production feasibility may break a close tie only after story gates are satisfied.

## 4. STORY PRINCIPLES

Apply these high-level craft principles without imitating any author's prose.

### Propulsion

* Open on a concrete anomaly involving murder or its immediate aftermath.
* Establish an understandable dramatic question immediately.
* Build a question ladder in which each answer produces a more consequential question.
* Deliver frequent earned answers.
* Make information behave like action.
* End leads through varied propulsion: discovery, contradiction, danger, irreversible action or meaningful decision.

### Cognitive clarity

* Keep the immediate objective clear while the larger meaning remains uncertain.
* Use concrete evidence and comprehensible cause and effect.
* Limit the number of active unknowns.
* Write for listening speed and mobile-sized scenes.
* Avoid exposition disguised as dialogue.

### Fair play

* Plant the decisive clue before explaining its significance.
* Give attentive players enough information to anticipate at least one major connection.
* Recontextualise existing evidence rather than introducing an unrelated solution.
* Never solve the case through coincidence, unsupported intuition, character stupidity or a villain monologue.
* Never hide information Ally already knows merely to deceive the player.

### Human consequence

* Give the murdered person one precise, grievable detail.
* Give the living keeper agency.
* Make the player's work visibly improve someone's situation.
* Resolve the episode's human question even if institutional justice remains incomplete.
* Never treat the victim solely as a puzzle component.

### Lead discipline

Every played lead must:

* answer one active question;
* and either create a better question or materially escalate the stakes.

Every played lead should perform at least two functions:

* answer;
* complicate;
* endanger;
* humanise.

A lead performing only one function is suspect. A lead performing none is a toll booth.

## 5. KILL-GATES

Apply kill-gates before comparative judgement. Any confirmed failure caps the verdict at REWORK.

### Gate 1: Cold-open compulsion

The blind cold open must make a stranger want the next section without prompting.

You cannot establish this internally. Mark the gate UNKNOWN pending table-read.

You may predict whether it will succeed, but must identify the mechanism and the risk rather than declaring a pass.

Agency cannot repair an inert opening. The cold open must work before the first decision.

### Gate 2: Murder-led present jeopardy

The pilot must be murder-led.

Murder must remain restrained and consistent with the twelve-plus noir-lite boundary. No graphic description.

If the opening victim is dead, an identified living person must face credible present-tense danger.

The antagonist must be actively doing something now. Institutional wrongdoing alone is insufficient.

State two jeopardy tracks separately:

1. jeopardy to the living victim, keeper or target;
2. jeopardy to Ally.

Judge the premise by the weaker track.

The central jeopardy must be expressible in one sentence containing a genuine countdown or closing window.

### Gate 3: The clock survives the medium

This is a merge game with energy regeneration at one unit per one hundred and fifty seconds. Players may experience an episode over multiple sessions and real-world days.

Do not use a countdown that expires while the player is asleep or away from the game.

Use one of these structures:

* **Day-stamped clock:** Events progress across authored days, with overnight gaps made plausible.
* **Lead-state clock:** The antagonist advances when specified leads resolve, so story time moves with play.
* **Session-contained window:** A short urgent sequence begins only after activation and resolves before the player returns to ordinary merge pacing.

Do not use a real-world timer that punishes absence.

If a premise depends on an hours-scale pursuit and cannot survive one of these structures, identify the structural failure.

### Gate 4: Midpoint activity transformation

The midpoint must change what the player does.

Valid transformations include:

* investigation becoming protection;
* reconstruction becoming interception;
* identifying a suspect becoming preventing another death;
* proving the past becoming disrupting something underway;
* following one trail becoming choosing between endangered people;
* evidence collection becoming controlled exposure, bait or rescue preparation.

A frightening revelation followed by more of the same investigation fails.

### Gate 5: Meaningful agency

Each episode must contain at least four decisions that alter:

* which leads spawn;
* what the player does next;
* what evidence becomes available;
* who receives protection;
* what risk is accepted;
* or which materially different ending occurs.

Dialogue-only variations do not count.

A single late branch automatically fails.

Each decision must provide:

* two credible options;
* incomplete but fair information;
* no obviously correct "good" choice;
* an immediate playable consequence;
* a later callback or consequence;
* a visible difference in leads, board work, risk or ending.

Agency must amplify an already compelling thriller. Do not assume choices created the jeopardy testers found missing.

## 6. AGENCY COST CONTROL

This is a solo production. Branching must be buildable.

### Branch-and-rejoin rule

* Divergence may last no more than three leads before rejoining.
* A branch cannot fork again before it rejoins.
* Shared scenes should carry the principal plot wherever possible.
* Exclusive content must earn its production cost.

### Cost classes

Tag every decision:

* **Class A:** No exclusive voiced scene. Changes requirements, generator access, evidence state, risk, lead order or which sibling lead spawns.
* **Class B:** Requires two to six variant spoken lines around divergence, rejoin or callback.
* **Class C:** Requires a fully exclusive voiced lead or short exclusive chain.

Requirements:

* At least two decisions should be Class A.
* No more than one decision should be Class C.
* Class B and C content must have a specific narrative payoff.
* Do not describe any branch as free. Even Class A requires configuration, balancing and QA.
* Prefer board-level consequences over dialogue-only consequences.

Report both:

* the number of leads an individual player experiences;
* the total number of authored leads required across all branches.

Do not assume a fixed ratio between them.

## 7. ALLY AND CAST REQUIREMENTS

Ally's player-facing identity leads with true-crime podcaster. She is also a licensed PI.

The pitch must contain:

* an early warmth action that costs her something;
* an early demonstration of investigative competence;
* evidential discipline;
* vulnerability before the climax;
* a consequential mistake, sacrifice or accepted risk;
* an irreversible change by the ending.

Supporting characters remain competent:

* Del converts evidence into lawful action and defines institutional limits.
* Gerald contributes experience, pattern recognition and personal history.
* Mo supplies human and waterfront knowledge.
* Arthur owns archives and institutional memory.
* Vega owns digital investigation.
* Priya owns forensic interpretation.
* Cortez owns cause-of-death evidence.
* Helen challenges the personal cost Ally avoids.

Do not make supporting characters ineffective so Ally can solve everything. Ally's distinctive competence is connecting evidence, people and meaning that specialists see separately.

Use only characters the story needs. Report current portrait status from the roster. A proposed character or appearance creates art work but is not automatically disallowed.

Closed endearment map:

* Gerald says "love."
* Mo says "my lovely."
* Dot says "love."
* Del calls Ally "Quinny."
* Nobody else uses these endearments.

Do not assign pronouns to Vega unless the pitch deliberately proposes that first-script canon ruling and flags it for Stephen's approval.

## 8. HAVENBAY TEST

Each case must answer:

> Could this case happen only in Havenbay?

Atmosphere must affect what can be seen, proved, reached, reconstructed or prevented.

Rain, fog, tides, harbour machinery, transport, redevelopment, district geography, civic records, working-class institutions and neighbourhood routines may be used when causally relevant.

Decorative weather does not count.

Identify the specific Havenbay fact without which the solution breaks.

Prefer established locations and institutions where they naturally fit. Do not force landmarks into the story as fan service or invent replacements for suitable canon locations.

## 9. PODCAST AND EVIDENCE-MEDIUM TEST

The medium must generate the mystery rather than merely present it.

Each pitch must contain a primary evidence system the player physically reconstructs, compares, restores or interprets, such as:

* photographs;
* manifests;
* maps;
* receipts;
* access records;
* conflicting public records;
* physical transcripts;
* damaged documents;
* recordings used outside the excluded missing-person shape;
* mechanical or environmental traces.

State:

1. what the player physically does;
2. what conclusion becomes possible;
3. what new action that conclusion unlocks.

The Tip Line is a full object-character with a portrait and no case file. Use it only when structurally necessary. Do not make all three pitches Tip Line stories.

## 10. FOUR CRAFT LENSES

Apply these as separate tests.

### Connelly

Can every important conclusion be traced through:

obtained evidence → interpretation → verification → lawful or practical action?

Identify the credible wrong hypothesis and the evidence that defeats it.

### Harper

Could this case happen only in Havenbay?

Identify the place fact that changes the solution rather than decorating it.

### Hallett

Does the player reconstruct the truth through media, records and artefacts?

Identify the object that becomes a storytelling participant.

### Lehane

Give the criminal system an economy rather than relying on threatening iconography.

Answer:

1. Who profits?
2. Who performs the dangerous work?
3. Which institution enables or ignores it?
4. Which ordinary person pays the cost?
5. What compromise allowed it to continue?

Expose only one episode-sized part of that economy.

## 11. CANON AND SEASON HANDOFF

* One self-contained case.
* Exactly one new Thomas, Ferryman or Hart season-arc fact.
* One new fact and one new cost, never the full answer.
* Do not unmask the Ferryman or Hart.
* Do not reveal or describe the Ferryman's face.
* Do not casually connect an existing unnamed antagonist to the Ferryman.
* Do not use the coin as a substitute for motive, organisation or economy.
* Invent episode-level victims, witnesses and offenders as required.
* Use established recurring characters for their existing functions.
* Mark any proposed season-level addition as REQUIRES STEPHEN'S RULING.
* State when the supplied material does not support a conclusion.

### Episode 2 handoff

The winning pilot must hand naturally to Episode 2, *The Ferryman*.

Plant one precise season notch that Episode 2 can recontextualise or escalate. Do not resolve it in the pilot.

State:

* the pilot's new season fact;
* its immediate personal cost;
* what remains misunderstood;
* how Episode 2 uses or reinterprets it.

Do not invent the Episode 2 connection beyond what the current sources support. Mark any unsupported connection as a proposal requiring a ruling.

## 12. STORY LENGTH, GAMEPLAY AND ECONOMY

Story determines length.

Expect approximately fifteen to twenty played leads across two or three phases, but do not force any candidate into a predetermined count.

The Field Kit audio generator begins the episode. The Investigation Lab may enter when forensic work becomes necessary. The Kestrel Corner Diner supports the food family.

Merge requirements must feel like:

* examining evidence;
* reconstructing information;
* preparing protection;
* interviewing or supporting witnesses;
* testing a hypothesis;
* preparing an intervention.

They must not function as arbitrary toll booths.

Do not invent:

* energy totals;
* reward values;
* completion time;
* monetisation results;
* exact production cost.

Economy regeneration follows premise selection.

## 13. HOUSE PROSE RULES

Apply these rules to all sample game-facing text:

* No em dashes.
* No exclamation marks.
* Grounded deadpan noir.
* Understatement over melodrama.
* Never explain the subtext.
* Every spoken line must parse at listening speed.
* Spell numbers out in spoken lines.
* "Grandad," one d.
* "Okay," spelled out.
* Tip Line announcer format: "Message received: [weekday], [time]."
* Never give a calendar date in a Tip Line announcement.
* No villain monologues.
* No exposition dumps.
* No puns in titles.
* No spoken line longer than approximately forty-five words.

Use the locked L1-L5 dialogue as the voice reference. Do not treat it as evidence that *The Listener* succeeded as a pilot.

Requirements:

* no title;
* no pitch letter;
* no unnecessary proper nouns;
* no prior Havenbay knowledge required;
* only enough dialogue to test the hook;
* ends on the exact question, discovery or action intended to create continuation need.

### C. Complete truth

Reveal:

### D. Human engine

### G. Havenbay engine

### J. Agency map

Provide a table containing:

### K. Ally and cast

### M. Production reality

Provide:

* branching and QA burden;
* relative art burden: light, medium or heavy;

### N. Constructive-adversary finding

State:

* strongest reason it could hook;
* strongest reason it could fail;
* hidden assumption;
* likely tester objection;
* cheapest test capable of disproving its central promise.

Finish with one verdict:

* PROCEED
* PROCEED WITH CHANGES
* TEST BEFORE COMMITTING
* REWORK
* REJECT

Do not use numeric scoring.

## 15. FINAL COMPARISON AND SLATE CONTROL

### Step 1: Kill-gates

Mark every gate for every pitch:

* PASS;
* FAIL;
* UNKNOWN.

The cold-open compulsion gate must remain UNKNOWN until tested.

### Step 2: Forced rankings

Force-rank the three new pitches with no ties under:

* predicted cold-open compulsion;
* victim or keeper jeopardy;
* Ally jeopardy;
* clock compatibility;
* midpoint activity transformation;
* Connelly evidence chain;
* Harper Havenbay necessity;
* Hallett medium integration;
* Lehane criminal economy;
* meaningful agency;
* affordability of agency;
* emotional keeper;
* production feasibility.

Do not convert these rankings into points, percentages or an aggregate score.

### Step 3: Overall ranking

Give one overall ranking from first to third.

Explain why the first-place pitch wins despite the strongest advantage held by the second-place pitch.

### Step 4: Forced cut

Drop one of the three from further consideration.

Give the specific reason it does not justify another tester slot.

### Step 5: Incumbent displacement

Only after completing the intrinsic assessment, compare the two surviving challengers with the pivot summaries and supplied occupied-territory boundary for G, H and I.

For each challenger state:

* which incumbent it most directly challenges;
* what it appears to improve;
* what the incumbent may still do better;
* whether the challenger has earned a table-read place;
* which incumbent it should displace, if any.

Do not pretend the one-paragraph incumbent summaries permit full-pitch comparison. Mark the displacement judgement provisional.

It is acceptable and useful to conclude that neither challenger earns a place.

### Step 6: Recommendation

Recommend:

* exactly one new pitch for the next table-read;
* or no new pitch if none clears the bar.

Present the strongest case against your recommendation.

State the missing evidence that could reverse it.

Distinguish fact, inference, proposal and unknown throughout.

Do not treat internal analysis as evidence of audience appeal.

The final decision remains provisional until the blind cold opens are tested with at least five of the twelve testers who rejected the previous pilot.
