# CHAPTER 7 · THE BOX NUMBER · Ally's seventh broadcast week (Days 305 to 311)

Read `00-common.md` first. This file is everything else you may know.

## What Ally holds entering this week

Everything from chapters 2 to 6: the letter that cleared the four and its lines (Liam's third; Ruby's nights; Margo not at the slip; the kind line: Brad paid back the van money that autumn, every penny; not the police); the room that landed on Liam; the café (Violet was calling money in that autumn, no name); the sister and the ashes; the panel Violet carried round the trade for a month, two said yours, one asked had she shown her lad, unseen since she died and absent from the letter; Liam's side, all true and innocent; the boat owner's tip held with Del and never aired (Brad at her store door at twenty past seven that Monday, a flat parcel between two doors); Margo's line: the thing in the cupboard is wrong, it can't mean that; the deduction: something is missing from the world. Ruby has told Ally that Brad agreed with whoever spoke last, and that he was up at five to run, and that one line in the letter did not sound like Vi. Ruby has also, in an earlier sitting, mentioned a small thing she never told the others: in spring Brad told the four he was at a wedding, and Ruby saw a results page from a patient's son showing he had run a marathon in another city that weekend. She said nothing then.

## The frame

The intellectual climax. Chapter 6's ask brings letters about the panel. Ally builds the week toward one question about one man, and asks it honestly, as a question. Two things the week depends on: the solicitor's slip in Ruby's envelope, and the survivors' account of the evening the notes arrived. The chapter has two versions of its first two packages, depending on last week's decision D3.

## Facts available this week (use only these)

- **Verbatim branch** (`aq.fk.d3.verbatim`): two signwriters answer Ep 6's exact words with independent written descriptions of the panel: black ground, gold capitals and an ampersand, her own alphabet done better than she'd ever done it, a crack across one corner, a small mark bottom right that was not her name. Double testimony. **Paraphrase branch** (`aq.fk.d3.paraphrase`): one signwriter answers the softened ask; Ally works the second description out of the café owner by legwork; the crack is uncorroborated and stays off the spread.
- The slip, in Ruby's envelope: the solicitor's covering slip lists four addressees, three of them street addresses and one a post office box number. The envelopes were posted on the Friday evening after the funeral, after the last collection, and carry the Saturday postmark. The solicitor confirmed the lodging three weeks before Violet died.
- When each read theirs, on the record: Ruby, off night shifts, woke about three and read hers at half past three; she sat with it because the note said together, and rang Liam at five when the crane stopped. Liam read his at half past five at the yard, in the yard's afternoon post. Ruby rang Margo at six, by Ruby's memory. Liam rang Brad at a quarter to six.
- What Brad said, by Liam's account, on the record: Liam said his had been in the yard post that afternoon and asked whether Brad had seen his. Brad said: same, nothing in the box this morning, it must have come in the afternoon sort.
- The habit both survivors knew: Brad cleared his post office box at seven every working morning, on his way to the first job. The box number is on the side of his van.
- The post office counter, on the record as a general fact: there is one sort to the boxes a day, overnight; it is in the boxes before the lobby opens at seven; there has never been an afternoon sort.
- The kind line: the letter's one kindness is to exactly one man; the café says Violet was calling money in that autumn; the letter says he paid her back that autumn unasked. Ally may set those beside each other and say she does not know what to make of it.
- The marathon: Ruby's held pebble. He lied about where he went, once, provably, about nothing that mattered. Or so it seemed.
- Nobody alive ever saw Brad write in cursive; the letter is in cursive.
- Gerald (canon character, the old newsman in his booth; dry, method not facts): a letter that passes examination is not proved hers, love; it's just not proved anyone else's.
- Liam's two-line return to the show (D2 market branch only, `aq.fk.d2.market`): his first words to Ally since the sheds, naming his silence, in package 03.

## The turn, package 07 (write it with care)

Ally puts the pieces in a row, as questions, not claims. Brad cleared his box at seven every working morning; the sort is in the boxes before seven; that Monday evening he told Liam there had been nothing in the box that morning and it must have come in an afternoon sort; there is no afternoon sort. So either the one sort missed his box on that one day, or he had his key at seven and said he had not. She does not say which. She notes what else is true: he argued Liam's side every week and lost; the letter's one kindness is to him; the panel the town saw her carry is nowhere in the letter. And she says, honestly, what this is and is not: not proof of anything, a question about a dead man nobody asked while he was alive. Then the ask, on air: if you saw Brad Collins anywhere that Monday morning, write to me, not to anyone else. (She never mentions the boat owner.)
