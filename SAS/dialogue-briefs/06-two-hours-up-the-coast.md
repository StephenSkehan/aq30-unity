# CHAPTER 6 · TWO HOURS UP THE COAST · Ally's sixth broadcast week (Days 298 to 304)

Read `00-common.md` first. This file is everything else you may know.

## What Ally holds entering this week

The public account; chapters 2 to 5: the letter that cleared the four and its listed odd things (Liam's third; Ruby's nights; Margo not at the slip; the kind line: Brad had paid back the van money that autumn); the room that landed on Liam; the café (money called in, no name); the sister and the ashes; the panel Violet carried round the trade, unseen since; Liam's side, all true and innocent, "built" held; the boat owner's tip held with Del and never aired (Brad at her store door early that Monday); Margo's last months; the man in the lane the wrong build for Liam; the dashboard clock; and the ask: did she say anything, to anyone. Three days of nothing.

## The frame

The ask is answered once. A letter arrives (Day 300) from a woman two hours up the coast: Tessa, Margo's partner of eleven years, whom nobody in Havenbay knew existed until the police rang her from a card in Margo's kitchen drawer. She attaches a screenshot. Ally drives up the coast road Margo drove every second Sunday. This is the week of the deduction.

## Facts available this week (use only these)

- The letter and its writer's fear of being found; she consents anyway. Eleven years of secrecy taught her a sentence can be an address.
- Tessa: eleven years; Margo had a whole second room to her life and kept the door shut without effort; every second Sunday, the coast road.
- The card in the kitchen drawer: in case, ring Tessa, a number. Margo prepared for exactly this eleven years ago. That is how Tessa learned Margo was dead.
- The screenshot, authenticated and dated: Day 77 after Violet's death (about two and a half weeks after Brad vanished). A message from Margo to Tessa on the app they used: "The thing in the cupboard is wrong. It can't mean that." Tessa screenshotted it because Margo did not say things like that. "The cupboard" is what the four called Violet's store. Tessa did not know what cupboard and Margo did not explain.
- The deduction Ally builds: Margo doubted the letter's meaning, said so once, to one person, and never again. If the thing in the cupboard was wrong, then something about it did not fit. What did the room verify? Liam's third of the yard, true. The boat requests, true. The lock changed, the key taken in, true. The kind line about Brad, unchecked by anyone. And what was Violet carrying round the trade in her last months that nobody has seen since: the gilded panel. It is not in the store, not in Brad's boxes, not in the estate. Something is missing from the world. Ally says exactly this much and no more: she does not say the letter is false, she does not say who.
- Close reading of "that": what did Margo think the thing could not mean? The board holds the question open. Nobody alive can answer it.
