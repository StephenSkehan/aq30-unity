## What Ally holds entering this week

Everything in the public account. From chapter 2: Ruby's account of the room; the note's text; the letter behind the door, which cleared the four, said someone with Violet's key had been getting in or she was imagining it, gave the gate, the lock change told only to the four, the key taken in; asked them to list who had been near each of them; listed a few odd things (Liam's third of the yard; Ruby's nights; Margo not at the slip; and one kind line: Brad had paid back the van money that autumn, every penny, the one thing that went right); and said not the police. There were lines about Violet's mother that Ruby would not read. The room landed on Liam within a month; Brad argued his side and lost; Margo went quiet; Brad drove to Kestrel Head. The solicitor's slip: lodged three weeks before, four addressees. Brad's habits. Nothing else.

## The frame

Ally leaves Ruby's kitchen and goes to the town: the harbour café where Violet ate and whose menu boards she lettered; the cinema whose owner argued with her about the colour of an R; two signwriters up the coast; and, for the first time, Violet's sister, who administers the estate and did not get on with her. Money enters, and what Violet found in her last months gets a shape.

## Facts available this week (use only these)

- The café owner, on record: in her last months Violet said, in passing, that she was calling in what she was owed, for the lawyer. No name. Nothing more.
- The sister, on record and bitter: the two sisters had been fighting over their late mother's house for years; Violet had a solicitor on Civic Row; Violet left no will, so the sister administers everything. And this, which the survivors have never said: the thing beside the letter was their mother's ashes. Violet had kept them four years on a shelf and told her sister they'd been scattered. A friend gave them back to her in the tenth week after the death, in a tube, and would not say where they had been, only that Violet had locked them in her store. The sister has them now. Ally may air this; the sister consents. The survivors still will not speak of it. (Ally now knows what the object was, from the sister, and says so plainly, once.)
- The cinema owner: Violet ranked people's handwriting out loud and told the truth about your kitchen; their running argument was the colour of an R on the marquee.
- The Regent marquee: three of six letters back, the ladder still chained under them, nothing finished. That is how you know she was there. Texture only.
- The panel: for about a month, two and a half months before she died, Violet carried a gilded lettering panel round the trade asking whose hand it was. The café owner saw it; the cinema owner saw it; two other signwriters up the coast saw it. Two said "yours, in a very good year". One asked whether she'd shown her lad (her former apprentice; Ally may note that Brad Collins was her apprentice for seven years; nothing more). Loose descriptions only: gold letters on a dark board, an alphabet, in her own style, better than anyone had seen her do. Nobody has seen it since she died. It was not in the store when the four opened it, according to Ruby; it was not in the boxes; the sister has never seen it. Ally does not know where it is and says so.
- The fall on her record six weeks before she died: a witnessed stumble on the weed-covered slip, walking backwards talking to another swimmer; bruised all over; swam anyway; Ruby treated the bruising at the surgery. The coroner read it as a prior fall. It was innocent and witnessed. Ally says so.
- Ally's own download map: one town, four hundred miles away, listening every week since the show began. She files it as odd, tells nobody, never airs it, never explains it.
