# CHAPTER 8 · THE FIRST TRAIN · Ally's eighth broadcast week (Days 311 to 318)

Read `00-common.md` first. This file is everything else you may know.

## What Ally holds entering this week

Everything from chapters 2 to 7. On air last week she asked whether anyone saw Brad Collins on the Monday morning the notes arrived, and set out, as a question, that his post office box is cleared before seven, that he told Liam that evening his letter had not been in the box that morning, and that there is no afternoon sort. She holds, unaired and with the police: the boat owner who saw Brad at Violet's store door at twenty past seven that morning. She has said on air that the letter's one kindness is to Brad and that the panel is nowhere in it. She has never said Brad is alive, and she does not believe it yet.

## The frame

Alive becomes thinkable and is never said. Two letters from the mailbag matter, and both go to the police and never on air. Ally's private working changes this week; her broadcast does not.

## Facts available this week (use only these)

- The skipper's letter (the first of the two): a fishing skipper writes that a deckhand who used to drink at "Brad the signwriter's, the one with the van" went missing from his boat three years before Violet died and was never found; an itinerant man with no family, reported missing, closed as drifted. One passing line: the skipper still keeps a small box of the man's things left aboard, in case anyone ever comes asking. He gives no surname for the deckhand in the letter beyond a first name and a trade. To Del, never aired. Brad acquires a shadow.
- The harbour years, as the skipper knew them: a man who lived aboard, kept a dry bag at a mate's, drank ashore, missed a sailing one morning, and had a first name and a trade nobody ever asked for.
- The harbour master's ledger of leavers: itinerant crews, men who go between tides, files closed as drifted. Men the sea is allowed to keep. Texture, lawful.
- Ally files the skipper's letter: she cannot see what it means, says so to herself, and keeps it. A drawer with a weight in it.
- The platform woman's letter (the second): she was on the platform at the inland halt for the first train out, the 05:47, on the Saturday morning Brad's car was found at Kestrel Head. A man bought a cash single and the clerk complained about breaking a note; she noticed the man's boots were wet. She writes because Ally asked, weeks ago, for anything odd. To Del, not aired.
- The timetable against the search: the last train in reaches the halt on Friday at 17:10; the first out is 05:47; between the halt and Kestrel Head is a ridge path a fit man could walk in a night. Built on the board, held, never aired.
- The clerk: he complained about breaking a note at twenty to six and remembered the boots because of it. Small memory outlives tape.
- On air: if you were on that train, write to me, not to anyone else. The letter itself goes to Del.
- The re-string (the chapter's wall): every card on the wall re-read in one sitting against a possibility Ally will not say on air. Her private note: I have stopped saying "was". Brad's survival becomes her operative belief, and the player's, and it is never spoken.
