## What Ally holds entering this week

The public account; chapter 2 (Ruby, the note, the letter that cleared the four and its listed odd things, the room that landed on Liam, the slip, Brad's habits); chapter 3 (the café: Violet was calling money in for the lawyer, no name; the sister: the house fight, no will, the ashes were the object; the panel Violet carried round the trade for a month, two said yours, one asked had she shown her lad, nobody has seen it since; the innocent slip fall; the download map, held).

## The frame

Liam talks. Liam Bryce, boatyard crane driver, has known Violet since the school bus thirty-five years ago; he drove Violet and her mother in on the mother's last night; he found Violet's body. He owns a third of the yard and lets the town think he is staff. He is the man the room landed on, and he knows it. Ally goes to the yard, then to the slip boatsheds where Violet's store is. The R family carries the yard and the market. This is the week a stranger rings the Tip Line.

## Facts available this week (use only these)

- The yard: the crane, the slings, boats out of water. A yard hand mentions Liam sings, a real tenor, only in the crane cab with the door shut.
- Liam's side, as he gives it: thirty-five years; the third confirmed and owned (he bought in eight years ago and never said, because a man who is staff gets asked for nothing); he drove them in the night her mother died.
- The letter's lines about him, and his answers: he had asked Violet twice this year, March and July, to take her mother out to the point on his boat, and she said no both times (Ally may now say this, since the sister has made the ashes public; Liam says: it had been four years, and I thought if somebody offered she wouldn't have to be the one who decided). He had been in her back bedroom once this year, in spring, to fix a window catch, and rehung the back door on the same visit. He says these things plainly and knows how they sounded at the table.
- The lock and the key, from Liam's side: in October Violet told him in her kitchen that she'd moved the spare to the second pot by the yard door, and when he looked at the new cylinder and asked, she said the old one was sticking. On the day he found her, the pot was empty; he saw her through the kitchen window and broke the pane by the back door. The back door was latched; it only catches if you lift it as you pull, and he fitted it. The pot is under the step inside the yard behind the wall and the gate he fitted, which latches from inside; you can't see it from the lane. He says: five of us knew that pot, and one of us is dead. He also says: a wall can be climbed. We knew that. We didn't want it.
- The letter's items about him were all true. Every one, and every one innocent. Laid side by side they read like a case built to fit him. Ally can say "built" to herself and not on air.
- August 2014 (character fact): Violet's kitchen, a bottle open, one question about her mother's house, and a laugh two people have spent twelve years hearing differently. Liam gives his half flatly. Nobody knows whose side Violet was on.
- The slip boatsheds at 05:40: brick boat stores and a corridor of lockers along the back wall, the slip road where the vans park, no camera on any of it, worked from first light by boat owners; anyone with a key and a reason. Violet's store is a brick bay with a steel door; the sister had the padlock cut and it has been empty since.
- The tip (Day 290): a boat owner who works the slip before the tide rings the Tip Line. He knew Violet by sight (she lettered his boat) and Brad (he painted his tender). On the Monday morning the notes arrived, at about twenty past seven, he saw the young one, Brad, at Violet's store door, hours before the four of them came at night, and saw him carry a flat wrapped parcel from her door to another door in the same corridor. He said "early for you" and thought her pupil was collecting her kit. He consents to Ally passing it to the police. Ally gives it to Sergeant Del Cruz the same day and does not air it. (Ally does not know what Brad took or why; she does not speculate on air; she notes privately that a dead man went to the store early.)
