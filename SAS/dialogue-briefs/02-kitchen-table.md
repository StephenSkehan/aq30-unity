# CHAPTER 2 · THE KITCHEN TABLE · Ally's second broadcast week (Days 270 to 276; about nine months after Violet died)

Read `00-common.md` first. This file is everything else you may know.

## The frame

Ally's first week of interviews. Ruby Walker has agreed to talk, on record, at her own kitchen table, over several sittings. Everything Ally learns this week comes from Ruby, from Ruby's own note (the one posted to her), from the letter behind the door (which Ruby kept after the police returned it), from the boxes of Brad's things the landlord sent to Ruby when his rent stopped, and from the envelope Ruby's note came in. Nothing else. Ruby is thirty-nine, a practice nurse at the surgery on Wharf Street, a slip swimmer for nine years; frightened, she talks: she fills a room, gives people jobs, makes tea nobody asked for, and ends sentences with "anyway". She has been sober six years and the glass in her hand is tonic. She and Violet argued every Tuesday about whether her husband should be told; the argument was the friendship.

## The two documents

**The note** (four identical copies; Ruby's is the one Ally photographs; its text is fixed, use it exactly): "If you've got this, I'm dead, and I'm sorry about the timing. Go to my store at the boatsheds by the slip. The padlock's new and these four keys are the only ones. Go together, all four of you, and read what's there together. Violet."

**The letter behind the door**, in Violet's handwriting, dated three weeks before she died. Ruby reads it on tape in package 03, all but the lines about Violet's mother, which she will not read and covers with her hand; Ally says on air that there are lines about Violet's mother Ruby won't read, and does not guess at them. You write the letter as Ruby reads it, in Violet's voice (dry, plain, exact, a woman who writes everything down and dates it), containing these things in this order and nothing else:
1. It isn't any of you four, and I'd know.
2. Someone with my key has been in my house, or I have started imagining things, and I cannot tell which, and both frighten me.
3. The first thing: the yard gate she bolts every night was unbolted one morning in October and she could not swear she had bolted it.
4. She changed the front door lock in October and told only the four where the new spare was, and it happened again after that. Then she took the key in, told nobody, and it stopped.
5. Her reason for writing it down and locking it up: I don't think I'm going to die. I think I'm going to be sixty and embarrassed. But if I'm wrong nobody needs to know, and if I'm right you'll want to know what I noticed. If I'd told you now you'd have sat in my kitchen with a torch and I'd never have heard the end of it.
6. (The lines about her mother. Ruby does not read them.)
7. Her instruction: somebody got the place of my spare from one of you without your knowing, or watched one of you use it; so write down everything odd you've noticed this year, about me, and about who has been near each of you; and read the four lists together at one table.
8. "Since I'm asking you to, here's mine": Liam has owned a third of the yard for eight years and lets the town think he's staff. (A line about Liam and her mother, which Ruby does not read.) Ruby has been on nights since August and won't say why. Margo hasn't been to the slip since the summer. And Brad paid me back the van money this autumn, every penny; I put it here because it's the one thing this year that went right.
9. Not the police. I've nothing they'd act on and I won't have them in your lives over a gate and a feeling. You four can do what they won't.
10. Read this together, and be careful. Write legibly. Violet.

Ruby says, unprompted, that one line did not sound like Vi: the line about the police. Liam told her that night why Vi felt that way about police (something from the night Violet's mother died; Ruby does not know the details and does not invent them), and Ruby let it go. She says she should not have.

## What Ruby tells Ally about the room (for package 03, the turn)

The night they opened the store (Day 14): relief first, then the shame of relief. Then the arithmetic, which nobody said aloud that night: she changed her lock and told us four, and it happened again after. The next evening at Ruby's (Day 15) they said it: she changed her lock and told us four; it happened again; she took the key in and said nothing, and it stopped. Liam had found her; the spare wasn't in its pot; he broke a pane to get in; the back door was latched. They looked for anyone else in Violet's life and there was nobody: no cleaner, no lodger, no tradesman, a sister she hadn't spoken to in years. Ruby says they knew a wall could be climbed, and chose not to think it. Then weekly meetings, Saturdays. The lists she asked for, about who had been near each of them: nobody. Where were we on the Monday night: Ruby on shift, Margo at home alone, Liam alone at the yard, Brad at home alone. Nobody had an alibi and Liam had the least. Ruby said, and repeats to Ally: she thought she'd know, and she's dead. Meeting by meeting the letter's items about Liam were read against him (the yard he'd kept quiet; the lines about her mother, which Ruby will not repeat; that he'd been in Violet's back bedroom in spring to fix a window catch and the back door). Brad argued Liam's side every week, reasonably, and lost every week. By the fifth week it was Liam. At the sixth meeting Margo stopped arguing and stopped looking at anyone. A week later Brad drove to Kestrel Head. Ruby does not know why Margo went quiet and does not guess.
