She gives her address and asks Ally to send the police, not to come, and not to say her name. Never aired.

- Ep 10 airs, Day 332. Silence holds.
- The news, Day 333: arrested four hundred miles away, under a name that had sat for three years in a missing-person file in Del's own precinct, the deckhand's name from the skipper's letter. Brad Collins, alive. Confirmation of what Ally has held, not a new belief.
- **Aired branch** (`aq.fk.d4.mark_aired`): because the mark went out on Ep 9, every claim that followed was untestable, and the accounting is done through a flood of false sightings; the mark sits in the public record forever. **Held branch** (`aq.fk.d4.mark_held`): the held detail let Del test the landlady's message against something nobody else could know; one verified chain; the mark never enters the public record.

She said "the thing in the cupboard is wrong" to one person and to nobody else, and withdrew from the friend she loved rather than say it at a table where he sat. Ally re-ranks everything Margo did in the middle weeks. She does not claim to know what Margo knew; she says what fits.
- The close: he is alive, and what that un-writes: the room's month, the two survivors' war, which ends; Liam and Ruby in the same room for the first time since the sixth week. The landlady is never named. Tessa's closing line differs by D3 (verbatim: she says the exact words were worth it; paraphrase: she says she was glad they weren't hers on air). The sister takes her mother out to the point on Liam's boat, the sister asking Liam this time, and Ruby carries the tube down the slip. The honest limit as ever: Ally does not know what was in the true letter, or whether there was one; she knows a man who was believed dead was in a room four hundred miles away with a black and gold thing on his wall. What she described, she erased: the wall gap appeared the day after she aired the description. She says that plainly. Sign-off.
