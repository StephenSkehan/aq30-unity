What the two generators agree on

  • Both independently produced the mother's mantel clock (F1, G6). Convergence says the object is intuitive; the gates say the ghost version and the cash version both fail. If the ashes is ruled too dark, the clock is the fallback, played as a stopped clock found wound and then let run down, never as a clock that "started".
  • Both agree the locker is justified by only three things: size, value or meaning. GPT chose value ten times and paid for it with money in the room. Fable's best candidates chose meaning.
  • Both agree a physical sign of handling (turned, opened, moved) beats a missing thing. Under v0.3 the sign must be small enough to doubt.

Recommendation

The object in the locker is her mother's ashes, in the plain crematorium scatter tube, four years unscattered on the back-bedroom shelf. The forged letter, in her voice: "Mum is in the locker. She was on the shelf facing the window and on the ninth she was facing the door. I do not turn her. I am not leaving her in a house I can't say is empty." Brad carries out something the size of a biscuit tin on Day 0 and nobody can ever date its absence. Liam's two requests to take her out on the boat go on the starter list as a true item, and the room does the rest.

Costs, stated plainly.

  1. Tone is a knife edge. One word of "ghost" or one line of grief theatre and it goes gothic. The rule for every brief: the tube is handled like luggage and spoken of like a person, and nobody at the table describes it.
  2. The writers must know, and the close must eventually say, where she is now. Proposed for the ruling, not ruled: the survivors have not scattered her; Liam still has the boat. That is a close-of-episode beat, not a chapter 1 fact.
  3. The sister exists (row −1500) and would have a view on her mother's ashes. One line of canon needed: the mother left it to Violet, or the sister asked for nothing.
  4. Fable's Margo beat (the maiden name on the label) is struck. It brings the house to the table. Under v0.3 Margo's turn is the omission of the debts, and the object needs no second turn.
  5. Replaces the board in spine rows 5b, 31 and R16 (the empty hook and the pale rectangle become nothing; the police photograph a shelf). The survivors' account "she took it down herself" becomes "she moved Mum herself, she said so in the letter", which is stronger.

Fallback if the ashes is ruled too dark: the mother's mantel clock, with Fable's engineered coincidence removed: she let it run down after the funeral; on the ninth it had been wound; she stopped it and locked it away, and her letter says so.

Salvage to the idea bank

  • A woman quietly buying her mother's things back one at a time (F4's Margo discovery).
  • The oldest mend in the house read as new by frightened people (F5's turn).
  • A 78 the survivors will not let a podcast play (F4's silence).

Two findings that belong to the spine, not to either object.

  1. Brad's foreknowledge on Day 0. Both treatments put the same crack under the object, and it is already under v8.1's board: Brad takes the decoy at 22:20 on Day 0 (row 94), but the notes are posted Day 12 and rows −18 to −1 read "nothing visible". Why does he carry an object out of the house a fortnight before he learns there is a locker to dress? Proposed fix, one row, and it strengthens the whole: the debt book is her book of everything, and Brad reads it that night. In it, in her hand: padlock changed, four keys cut on Lantern Street, envelopes lodged with the solicitor, "letter and the thing in the store". He learns on Day 0 that a letter exists and where, and he takes an object because he already knows he will have to replace what is beside it. This makes the Day 14 forgery a plan begun on Day 0 rather than an improvisation, and it makes the debt book's burning (row 108) matter twice.
  2. "The ninth" must be pinned. Whatever the object, the forged paragraph dates an intrusion and a move. The date must fall after the last time any of the four was in her house and before Day −19, when the true letter went into the store. One date, chosen once, and the standing check (nothing in a key letter may date the store letter) still passes because the key letters do not mention it.

My read of the two, for the ruling. The clock plays cleaner at the table: winding an eight-day clock is a deliberate act, so the room's reasoning is sound and not sentimental, and Margo's key-on-the-hook beat is the best single moment in either sketch. But the clock is weak on the one gate this whole round was called for: she stopped it, so a locker is a choice rather than a necessity, and "I did not want to be in the house with it ticking" is answered by "then don't wind it". The ashes is absolute on that gate, gives Ally the best discovery ("she was not on the shelf"), gives Liam the episode's closing act instead of a mantelpiece, and makes the room's crime the exact one v0.3 is about: they convicted the kindest man at the table for a kindness. Its cost is tonal and it is paid in every scene. I still recommend the ashes, and I would take the clock's Margo beat with it in spirit: her one private look is at the thing that points at nobody.
