## What the real cases settled

- **A wired bath or shower is not a television device; it is a documented husband's method.** Peter Ellis, Cardiff 1993 (bath assembly connected to the mains; the melted plug chain was the tell; the prosecution said a death would have read as a heart attack) and Maria da Penha, Fortaleza 1983 (bath electrocution attempt during convalescence). Both sourced by GPT. That clears gate G for the electrical bath candidates in a way round one could not.
- **The record of pre-rigged domestic mechanisms is thin, and the thinness is the point.** Fable said it, and GPT's nine, once the staging and the product-tampering cases are set aside, come to the same two or three patterns: electricity in the wet room, gas where she sleeps or lights a match, and a heat source left to do its work. A mechanism that reads as wear never gets a case name; that is Violet's position exactly.
- **The first officer reads the story he is handed** (Backhouse, Wangler's "defective heater", Smith's fits, Barlow until the dry pyjamas). The tell that breaks it is a small domestic continuity that the cover story needs and does not have. Violet's eye for a line that used to meet itself is the fictional form of that.
- **Natural gas does not poison** (Fable's technical note). A gas candidate kills by explosion, which needs an accumulation and a flame and a nose that does not work, or by a faulty appliance's carbon monoxide, which is the excluded standard.

## Forced ranking of the survivors

1. **F-A, the shower with the lost earth.** The only candidate that is certain, private, single, keepable, dismissable and real. Certain: the metalwork is live the moment she pulls the cord, and she is standing in an earthed iron bath, wet from the sea. Private: the shower straight from the slip is known to the four; the lost earth, said once at her own table, is known to the four and nobody else, and it is the fact that made the house unable to protect her. Single: one conductor moved. Keepable: the cut silicone bead, and photographs of a green-and-yellow sleeve in the live terminal. Dismissable: the electrician says wired wrong since it went in, the earth went when the street was dug, nobody can date either; the officer writes "unsafe installation, no injury". Real: Ellis, Cardiff, 1993, and the prosecution's own line that the death would have read as a heart attack. It beats F-C because the death is certain rather than likely, and because the lost earth turns "one of you" from an inference into a fact only they held.
2. **F-C, the brass switch.** Pure Violet in its discovery (a plate a degree off vertical) and it needs no planted fact at all. It loses to F-A on certainty: wet feet and a wet hand across the chest is a death a reader will accept, but "certain" is one word too strong. It is the candidate to take if Stephen judges the lost-earth planting too heavy.
3. **F-B, the gas union behind the tin.** The strongest image for "one of you" (the cupboard every one of them has seen opened) and the most Violet tell in either round (her own paint skin cracked in a ring). It loses on the smell: the whole death rests on her nose being dead for an hour after the sea, and a reader who has smelt gas once will not grant it.
4. **G-4, the heater baffle.** The soot feather bending off her painted line is a fine discovery, and the argument that one reversed component is not the two-tampering television murder is honest. It stays carbon monoxide, and Stephen has heard that one.

## Recommendation

**Rule F-A, the shower with the lost earth.** Two canon facts come with it and both are cheap:

- The house lost its earth when the water company relaid Mariner's Row in plastic the previous winter; her electrician told her; she said so at her kitchen table with all four present and never had it done. (One line in the five's Violet paragraph or in the spine's A1; it also gives the genuine letter its "you were all there when I said it".)
- She ran the silicone bead round the shower unit herself, straight as a pinstripe. (Already who she is.)
