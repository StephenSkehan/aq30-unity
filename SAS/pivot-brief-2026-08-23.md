## 1. What happened

Episode 1, "The Listener", was written, voiced-locked through L4, fully playable end to end, and taken to twelve friends-and-family testers (ten women, two men, ages sixteen to sixty-four).

**All twelve rejected it.** The words that came back: weak sauce, no jeopardy, too safe, not real true crime, didn't finish.

Immediately before that, an internal doctrine rubric had scored it near-perfect. A second internal rubric then scored three replacement pitches at 94, 92 and 94 percent, which discriminates nothing. Both rubrics were written by the author of the work they scored.

Stephen ruled on 2026-08-23: **no compelling pilot story means no release.** Launch dates are shelved until the pilot is right.

## 2. What is now true

1. **"The Listener" is demoted.** It becomes a mid-season episode (roughly Ep3) after a dread pass. It is not the pilot, and it is not a model to hold up as a success. Its craft was not the problem; its stakes were.
2. **The pilot must be murder-led with present-tense jeopardy.** Restrained murder is licensed by the tone charter (Ch 5.9): no graphic violence, no bodies described in detail.
3. **Jeopardy is scored on two tracks and takes the lower.** The original rubric had a bug: it measured jeopardy to Ally and never to the victim, so a case whose worst outcome was an inconvenience could score full marks. Both tracks must carry.
4. **Episode length is decided by the story, not the skeleton.** Expect fifteen to twenty leads. The economy models regenerate to fit rather than the story being cut to fit them.
5. **Player agency is integral.** Multiple decisions that change what the player DOES, not what a character says.
6. **Internal scoring is not decision input.** Kill-gates first, then forced ranking with no ties. Tester evidence outranks all internal judgement.

## 4. The live decision (unruled as of this writing)

Three murder pilots are on the table. Stephen rules; nobody else decides.

- **G "The Confession"** — an eighty-one-year-old man confesses to a 1989 killing in Gerald's booth at the Rusty Anchor, and is dead of a provable murder by morning. The midpoint turns the episode from investigation into protecting Gerald, who is now the last living witness. 17 leads, 3 phases. Cheapest art.
- **H "The Overnight Girl"** — a diner worker witnesses a body disposal, triggers a childhood escape plan, and disappears; a patient professional works through everyone she ever trusted, one polite conversation at a time, always a day ahead. 15 leads, 2 phases. Heaviest art.
- **I "The Last Caller"** — a tip-line confession is cut off mid-name by someone in the room, and the witness he was about to name does not know she is being hunted. 18 leads, 3 phases. Middle cost.

G and I share a 1989 spine and are mutually exclusive. H banks cleanly alongside either. The loser becomes season stock.

**The decision input is a table-read of the three cold opens to five or more of the twelve existing testers**, not any internal analysis. Full detail in `episode-1-pitches-v3.1.pdf`, Appendix A.

## 5. Standing rules that bind any advice

**Kill-gates, evaluated before any other assessment. Any failure caps the verdict at REWORK.**

1. The cold open, read aloud to a stranger with zero Havenbay context, produces the question "what happens next" unprompted.
2. The central jeopardy states in one sentence containing a countdown or a closing window.
3. The midpoint changes what the player is **doing**, not merely what they know.
4. **Agency:** count decisions that change which leads spawn, what the player does next, or what the ending is. Fewer than four is a fail. **A single end-of-episode gate is an automatic fail.**

**Method rules.** Forced ranking with no ties, never absolute scores. External benchmarks (real true-crime pilots), not internal comparison. Tester evidence outranks everything. Say "I do not know" rather than filling a gap.

**House prose rules, binding on every line of game-facing text.** No em dashes. No exclamation marks. Grounded deadpan noir; understatement over melodrama; never explain the subtext. Every line must parse at listening speed. Numbers spelled out in spoken lines. "Grandad", one d. "Okay" spelled out. The tip-line announcer format is "Message received: [weekday], [time]", weekday and time only, never a calendar date.

**Endearment map, closed.** Gerald says "love". Mo says "my lovely". Dot says "love". Del calls Ally "Quinny" and nobody else does. Do not add new endearment users. No endearments or gendered address in UI copy that speaks to the player rather than to Ally.

## 6. What has NOT changed

Everything below is still canon and still authoritative in its named master document.

- **The cast, their names, appearances and voices.** Note two additions later than some copies of the roster: Del Cruz's canon adds of 2026-08-03 (she calls Ally "Quinny", her watchful table outside the Kestrel Corner Diner, and her origin as the rookie constable on the Quinn doorstep the night Thomas died), and **the Tip Line as a full object-character** with an evidence-board portrait and no case file, canon 2026-08-11. The Tip Line matters structurally: it is the precedent for a victim who exists only as a recording, which two of the three live pitches rely on.
- **Havenbay itself.** Districts, landmarks, the Rusty Anchor, Pier 13, Kestrel Point, the Kestrel Corner Diner (renamed from Corner Diner, 2026-08-04), the Gazette.
- **The season arc.** Thomas Quinn's death, the Ferryman, Councillor Hart, the coin as calling card. The Ferryman and Hart are unmasked only in the season finale, live on the podcast. No earlier episode may do it.
- **The Pilot Doctrine (bible Ch 5.4)** and the **tone charter (Ch 5.9)**. Both still bind. 5.4 is the reason the pivot happened: it always demanded present-tense jeopardy, and the rejected pilot did not deliver it.
- **The art render language.** Stylized painterly illustration, not photoreal.
- **The economy.** Regen 150 seconds, energy cap 100, ads +20 up to five a day, Platinum Ingots, Case Cash. Schedule B and the subscribers ladder will be regenerated once the premise is ruled, because the lead count changes.
- **The board and its families.** Field Kit (audio) is the starting generator; the Investigation Lab (forensic) is granted mid-episode; the Kestrel Corner Diner drives the food family.
