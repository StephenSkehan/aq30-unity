1. **The lock**

**Fatal.** It ends this cut as written. Brad has no fifth key, so he cannot enter on Day −9, read the book or steal the paper and pen. The new lock does not answer the old competence problem. For this to be answered, Violet must have a compelling reason the outdoor spare cannot be withdrawn even temporarily, must be unable to monitor its use, and Brad must already possess another means of entry. The document supplies none of those things and expressly excludes a fifth key.

2. **Margo and the estate**

The accountant refers a disputed estate asset to the sister and her solicitor. The solicitor asks Brad for the amount, date, place and circumstances of the alleged cash repayment, any receipt or contemporaneous communication, and an explanation of why the deceased’s missing loan book contains the only expected record. The estate’s bank and cash transactions are checked. Brad can supply nothing beyond his assertion.

**Serious.** An accountant does not personally extinguish a material receivable because its debtor says he paid cash. Margo can attest to the original loan, Violet’s recent intention to collect it and the absence of any repayment record. The estate may ultimately decide the claim is uneconomic or unprovable, but it remains a disputed asset until the administrator makes that decision on advice. Brad is drawn into a formal account weeks before his planned disappearance, and his unsupported story remains active rather than closing.

For this to be answered, the amount must be too small to pursue, the claim must be legally or evidentially unrecoverable, and the sister’s solicitor must advise a documented write-off. “Unpapered cash loan” alone is not enough.

3. **Brad’s death**

Brad does not need Violet’s death to supply believable grief. He can stage an accidental death, which requires no grief motive at all. If he insists on an apparent suicide, his approaching financial exposure already supplies a comprehensible private crisis. More damagingly, living Violet is the person most likely to authenticate his supposed death emotionally. She trusts him completely and has explicitly ruled out his authorship of the panel.

**Fatal.** Killing the one convincing mourner before staging his own death is not the cheapest route to being believed dead. The debt might make Violet mention money, but it does not make a drowning false, and she presently possesses no evidence connecting Brad to the panel or an assumed identity. Murder creates a second death, a homicide exposure and the very group inquiry he must manipulate.

For this to be answered, a living Violet must be certain to challenge the staged death with specific evidence capable of leading investigators to Brad’s survival or deckhand identity. The cut establishes the opposite: she trusts him and believes he cannot gild.

6. **The whole**

The earliest competent action is on Day −37. Violet changes the lock because she suspects somebody has entered her home, but then creates another unattended outdoor spare and announces its location to every person within her only possible access pool. A competent security response does not reproduce the compromised arrangement. She removes uncontrolled access or silently monitors it.

**Fatal.** This happens before the locker, the forgery, the murder and the room. The later shelf movement only proves that her first response failed and makes continued inaction less defensible. Her reluctance to accuse the four explains why she does not confront them. It does not explain why she declines private, non-accusatory control of her own door while constructing a padlock, four solicitor-held keys and a posthumous investigation.

For this to be answered, uncontrolled access by all four must be indispensable, observation must be impracticable, and Violet must have a reason to accept continuing entry into her bedroom. None is present.

**REJECT. Confidence: high.** The finding that most drives the verdict is Brad’s death. The cut asserts that Violet must die so the town will believe his later death, but living Violet is already his strongest credible mourner and holds no actionable evidence that he survived. Without a demonstrated reason her continued life defeats his escape, the murder remains the most dangerous act in his plan rather than the necessary one.
