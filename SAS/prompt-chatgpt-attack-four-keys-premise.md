### Attack in this order. It is our own ranking of where it is weakest.

1. ★★ **The disappearance as alibi. This is the load-bearing claim and I think it is the weakest joint.**
   The premise says he vanishes, then a fourth friend is murdered, and that going first makes him look like a victim rather than a suspect. **But a man who disappears days before his friend is murdered is the first person any competent officer would think about.** A vanished person is a classic suspect, not a classic victim.
   **So: does the alibi work at all?** If it does not, does the premise survive by having police privately suspect him while the public account and the two survivors do not? Or does it need something that positively indicates he was taken, which he would then have had to stage?
   **We would rather be told this fails now than after a spine.**

2. **Why does she make the arrangement instead of going to the police?** She is frightened enough to hide proof and set up delivery of keys after her death. **Name the reason that is not cowardice or stupidity.** Our candidate: what she had was not yet proof, and using it meant accusing somebody she loved.

3. **The empty cupboard.** Four keys go out at the same time. **Why did only one of them go straight there?** And what does the killer do with proof that names him — destroy it, or keep it because it names something else as well?

4. **The second murder is committed by a man who is supposed to be dead.** He has to stay hidden and kill somebody, in a city where his face has just been in the news as a missing person. **Cost that.**

5. **Can a podcast actually establish that a man is alive?** That is the third act. The proof is meant to be a person in his new life who knows him under another name. **Find the reason a serialised public investigation reaches her and the police did not**, or tell us there isn't one.

6. **The two survivors suspecting each other.** It requires them not to suspect the missing man. **Is that psychologically credible, or is it the plot needing them to be wrong?**
