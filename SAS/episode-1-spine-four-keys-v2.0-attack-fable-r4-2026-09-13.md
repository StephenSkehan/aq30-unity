# Adversarial review of THE FRIENDS WITH FOUR KEYS, Spine v2.0.5 (Fable, round 4)

Read: Parts A to G in full, the header notes for v2.0.1 to v2.0.5, Part H ignored. Every objection below is checked against the tables; where I contradict a stated fact I say which and why it fails. No alternatives proposed.

## Front 1. Violet, Day −37 to Day −19

**Strongest objection.** The competent act she does not take is silence at the store door. On Day −19 at 10:00 she tells Brad, alone, while she is alive, that the four get the keys if anything happens to her. The document's own header (v2.0.3) says this line was added because it "was the route foundation 2 lacked": without it Brad does not know that her death opens the door, and C2 row −3 has no plan. So the whole mechanism of the murder hangs on one sentence she says to one man, and the document's defence of it is symmetry: "telling him tells him nothing the other three will not hear on the day she dies; to keep it from him alone would be to name him." Both halves fail. The three hear it dead; Brad hears it with her alive, and that asymmetry is precisely the fact that turns her death from useless to him into the thing that hands him the letter. And she does not keep it "from him alone": she lies to Liam about the cylinder that same week, jokes to the solicitor an hour later, and tells Ruby and Margo nothing. Silence to Brad would have been equality with the other three, not an accusation. The document pays the telling as "the same refusal as Day −75 and Day −29," but those refusals were refusals to know; this is a disclosure, and disclosure to one of five suspects is not the same act wearing a different coat.

Does it end the episode? Yes, as written. Without the telling, Brad watches a padlock go on and does not know the store opens on her death or to whom. Killing her then sends the true letter, with his "yours" recorded in it, to whoever cuts the lock: the sister's people, and the police behind them. His pre-panel plan (go early, faked death before December 1) is the only coherent one left, and there is no Day 0.

**Severity: serious.** Not fatal, because a character refusal at the door is a legitimate place to hang the episode, and the document does put it on the page. But it is paid in the wrong currency and defended with a false symmetry.

**To answer it:** the telling needs a reason that is specifically about Brad and specifically true at 10:00 on Day −19, not "he is the one I talk to about the others." One is lying in the table unused: he has just told her "yours," and she knows it is not hers, so she has just watched him lie to her about the hand; telling him about the keys is her choosing to trust him against what she has just seen. If that is the refusal, write it as that, and cost it as that.

**Secondary, serious:** the will. She sits in the Civic Row office on Day −19 lodging "post these after my funeral" envelopes, in the middle of litigation with a sister who inherits everything on intestacy, and declines the will the solicitor raises for the second time. The Day −19 problem she solves with one copy behind one door (the sister's people can demand a file copy on intestacy) is solved better by a will naming an executor. "When the house is settled, not today" is a line, not a reason, and "she would rather not know which" does not cover it: the envelopes already concede she may die. It does not end the episode, but it makes the day's second most consequential choice unmotivated.

**Cosmetic, clock:** A1 −19 has the letter arrive at the sheds written, at 10:00, before Brad says "yours"; D3 has the genuine letter say "Brad says it's mine," and C2 row 3 has Brad read in it that she showed him last. The letter cannot contain his answer if it is locked in the moment after he gives it. One line fixes it (she adds the sentence at the door), but as written the load-bearing document predates its own content.

## Front 2. Brad, Day −19 to Day 14

**Strongest objection.** The act that serves him better than anything written is one sentence, and the document dismisses it in one clause. On Day −40 he learns she is carrying his panel round the trade asking whose hand; on Day −19 she asks him directly. On either day: "It's mine. I made it at your bench. I hid it because it wasn't finished, and because I wasn't ready for you to see it." C2 row −6 rejects this with "he cannot want it without saying why." He can say why, because the why is the truth and it is banal: a pupil surpassed his teacher on her own bench and did not know how to tell her. The café's own question ("have you shown your lad") shows the trade already half expects this answer. The monogram: "a name I might work under, one day," or a client's, or nothing at all. He gets the panel back. Nothing is locked, no store opens on anyone's death, the pre-panel exit (Day −110: go early, by the faked death, before December 1) proceeds untouched, and nothing in the world of Parts A to G looks for him. The marathon, the crane cab and the falling turnover already tell the four he wants more; a monogram adds nothing they would act on.

The document's D1 makes this worse, not better. Constraint 6 says the panel is "innocuous in a stranger's house four hundred miles away; the mark is his own initials there," and C2 Week 3 says on Day 14 "the risk of keeping it is nil, because nobody alive can connect it to him." Two initials with no face on any document are traceable by nobody; the document relies on exactly that fact to let the panel hang on his wall for 260 days. Under the world's own rulings (R19, R20: presumed drowned, no reopening without evidence of an act), a store that opens in some future decade to four people holding two gilt letters is a risk of nothing. So the danger of the mark is asserted in Foundation 2 and refuted in D1 and C2. What survives is the want: the panel is "the best thing he has ever made, in the self he could not show, and she wrote that he could not have made it." A want is admissible under C2's own test. But a want for the panel was satisfiable by claiming it, and a want not to be seen (the only reason he could not claim it) is nowhere written or costed. So the murder is, as written, a choice the reader undoes with a sentence.

Two supporting failures in the same column. C2 row −16 rejects paying the debt because "the cash is the exit"; Foundation 1 says the sum is modest. Both cannot hold. If it is modest against two years of skimmed cash, paying it removes the December 1 date, and the fake death proceeds at leisure with the panel locked in a box nobody will open for decades. And C2 row −3 rejects "fake his death without hers" because "the four open it one day with his mark inside and her account of where she found it": "one day" is Violet's natural death, decades off, and the four then hold initials, not a name.

**Severity: serious, and fatal if the row cannot be written.** It contradicts no ruled fact; it exposes that the document's own coherence test (C2) has not been run on the one branch that matters.

**To answer it:** Brad's refusal to claim the panel must be written as his own lock, in his own currency, and paid: for instance that claiming it is showing the self he has built to disappear in, to the one person whose eye he cannot bear on it, and that this is the same failure of nerve that later makes him hang it on a wall; or a world fact that makes the claim costly, such as Violet, told he has a second name, being the one person who would read his drowning as flight and who forgets nobody. Either would do. Neither is on the page. Foundation 1's "modest" and C2's "the cash is the exit" must also be reconciled with a number's order of magnitude, not a number.

## Front 3. The room, Days 14 to 47

**Strongest objection.** The line is Ruby's, and it is the most ordinary line in the document: at the wake on Day 11, or at the café any day after, "What was she like, the last few weeks?" The café owner, the cinema owner and two signwriters each hold, with no confidence owed and no lock on them, that Violet spent a month carrying a gilded alphabet panel round the trade asking whose hand it was, and that one of them asked whether she had shown her lad. Row 11 gives their earliest action as "none, it is a panel," and C8 says "the police review never reaches the trade." Neither considers the four. These are her four closest friends, in her town, at her funeral, for 290 days, one of them a nurse who "knows every regular by name and by house." The document's own Day 15 has the room enumerate "nobody else in her life" and its Day 26 meeting is titled "everything odd about Violet." The oddest thing in her last two months is held by four townspeople and never crosses a café counter to any of them. That is not a computed silence; it is an uncomputed one.

Once said in the room, the line is: "She was round the whole trade with a gold board asking whose hand. Where is it?" Not in the house (the sister cleared it), not in the workshop (Liam, Day 2), not in the store. A missing object she was obsessed with, absent from a letter that lists a bolted gate and a turned tube, and exactly one gilder at the table. Brad can say "she showed me, it was hers, an old job" (as he does to the four on no day in the document), but the room then has the D3 retrospective tell on Day 26 rather than Day 304, and Margo's reading acquires a second leg five weeks early. The room does not land on Liam on Day 47; Brad has to go by Day 30; the Day 180 hook ("you're the one who stopped believing the letter") has nobody to catch, because everybody stopped.

Note also that Brad knows this route exists. He heard about the panel from the café. On Day 14 he cuts it from the letter "because it names a gilder," knowing the town can name the panel to the four any morning. C2 does not compute that risk.

**Severity: serious.** It does not end the episode (Del still does nothing under R19), but it collapses the shape of Days 14 to 60, which is the episode.

**To answer it:** the trade's silence toward the four has to be written and paid. The cheapest true version is that the café owner does ask, at the wake, and asks Brad, because he is the lad she meant, and Brad closes it in a sentence; and that the other three are not the café's people. That is a row. It is not there.

## Front 4. Margo, Days 8 to 60

**Strongest objection.** Her earliest competent action that changes the outcome is not to the estate or to Del; it is to the room, and it is one sentence on Day 15 or Day 26: "Vi kept a book. Everything went in it. It's gone." Row 20 answers the room with "Ruby and Liam never heard of it, so on Day 15 and Day 26 the room has nothing to ask about." Margo is in the room, and she has heard of it since Day −120. Her professional confidence is fully computed toward the sister and the estate (A2 Day 8, C5, and I accept that computation), but it has no purchase in the room: the room is Violet's own appointed forum, reading Violet's own private letter, and all four already know Violet set Brad up. Confidence cannot be the reason. Yet A3 Day 26 has Margo "read the paragraph for what is missing and find nothing missing" while she is the one person at the table holding the one physical absence in the case: a book of loans and fears gone from a house a man walked out of at half past ten. Her withholding from the sister is owned; her withholding from the estate is owned as her second omission; her withholding from the room across six meetings is neither owned nor computed, and it is the one that carries weight.

What happens if she says it. The room acquires a stolen object with a known partial content (Brad's debt) before it acquires a suspect. Brad's move is the one he makes anyway ("she'd have written it paid; whoever took it wanted her fears, not my debt"), and he can lean on the only one of the four who has been upstairs, so the room may still drift toward Liam. But the debtor's name is now in the room beside the missing record from Day 26, said aloud by the accountant, and Margo's Day 50 reading is no longer private; it is the room's, and Brad hears it made. Her isolation, her withdrawal from Ruby, the Day 180 approach, and the second death all stand on that reading being hers alone. The document has either to compute that the room still lands on Liam with the book on the table, and write why, or to give Margo a reason of her own for six meetings of silence about it.

**Severity: serious.**

**To answer it:** a written, paid reason Margo does not name the book to the four; or the room computed with the book named, showing it still closes on Liam. Note also a drafting contradiction that sits under this: Part G says "nobody but Violet had ever seen it or heard of it before Day 0," while row 20 and the v2.0.4 header say Margo and Brad knew. The canon annex an adversary is told to trust contradicts the ledger on the fact this front turns on. Cosmetic in itself; fix it.

## Front 5. Chapter 7

**Strongest objection.** The tell is idiom, and a listener finds it wrong by saying it aloud. Liam's question is "have you seen yours yet, mine only came tonight." Liam's letter did not come tonight either: post to a yard arrives in the day, and he read it at half past five. "Only came tonight" is how people say "I only got to it tonight." Brad's reply, "same, just now," is the same idiom returned. Ally's claim on air is that this is "a claim about arrival, and it is false." If it is, so is Liam's, about Liam's own letter, in the same breath, and Ally must either accuse the witness she is quoting of the identical falsehood or concede that neither man was speaking about the postal service. Every source in the chapter is sound (the slip, the postmark, the habit, the counter clerk, Liam's account), and they add up to a parsing of two words that no listener who has ever said "it only came today" about a letter they opened at night will accept. The chapter is labelled a suspect turn and Ally says "not proof," so it does not break the rules; but a turn that a listener can dismiss as a word game is not a turn.

A second check the listener makes: the clerk's statement is that the overnight sort is in the boxes before seven. It does not say that is the only sort. Ally's claim "his post was in his box at seven" needs the clerk to say one sort, once, and needs Brad's letter to have been in Monday's sort rather than Tuesday's, which she infers from three street deliveries and cannot source for a box.

**Severity: serious.** The chapter's turn stands on an unfalsifiable colloquialism.

**To answer it:** Brad's words on the call must contain a specific claim about arrival that the counter fact contradicts and that Liam can quote nine months later, for instance a reference to the box or the morning ("wasn't in the box this morning, must've come in the afternoon"), and the clerk must say one sort, not "before seven." The document's own C2 verdict calls the box lie a costed mistake; the cost has to be a checkable sentence, or it is not a cost.

**Cosmetic, but it is the terminal link:** the landlady is a listener of a Havenbay true crime podcast 400 miles away, from about chapter 3, with no reason given (C8: "his habits, from six weeks of strangers on air"). F5 says the leak is the only coincidence wearing a coat; this is the second, and the arrest depends on it. One line (the show's reach; Brad playing it in the house) answers it. It is not there.

## Front 6. The whole

**Strongest objection.** No innocent party ends the episode before its time. I tried every open route in Parts B and C against R19, R20, R22 and R27 and could not break them: the solicitor on Day 6, Ruby on Days 14, 33 and 62, №14 at the door-knock, the boat owner on Days 61 to 63 and 265, the sister on Day 82, Del on Day 222. Each produces nothing, and the document says why, correctly. The police rulings are the load-bearing walls and they hold.

The earliest competent action that ends the episode is Brad's own, on Day −40, and it is Front 2: claim the panel. That is not a leak; it is a coherence fault at the motive, and it is why this front returns to Front 2 rather than finding a new door.

The earliest uncomputed innocent route is the skipper's, on Day 62, not Day 311. A high-risk missing person at Kestrel Head with a three-day coastguard search is a named man in the local paper: "Brad Collins, signwriter, van found." The skipper's connection on Day 311 is triggered by exactly those words on air; row 10c and C8 do not say why the same words in print on Day 62 do not trigger it. Computed, it moves Del's Day 312 revival to Day 222 (a note on Brad's file linking him to a second vanished man with no family; an activity check on the deckhand's name returns nothing under row 10d; the name goes live in the system) and ends nothing, because the arrest still needs a face in a doorway 400 miles away. Write it, or write why the MP was never publicised.

**Severity: cosmetic for Front 6 itself** (nothing ends early); the two serious findings it exposes are already Fronts 2 and 3.

**Drafting faults an adversary is entitled to hold against a document that tells him Parts A to G are facts:** "R16" is cited eight times as a police ruling on low-risk missing persons and is defined nowhere in Part E or Part G (B7's R16 is a retired residue row); Day −120 is July 30 by the document's own calendar (Day 0 the last Monday before a Thursday month-end), yet Margo's note is "the August conversation" in A2 Day 8 and Day 45; A3 Day 62 says none of the three mentions the letters while C4 Week 9 gives Ruby telling Del as her action; and the letter clock fault under Front 1.

## Verdict

**REWORK**, confined to C2 and the room (A3 Days 14 to 47, C5), not the premise, not the foundations, not the police rulings, which held under everything I put at them. Confidence: moderate to high on the finding, high that it is the one that matters. The single finding that drives the verdict is Front 2: the document's own coherence test has not been run on the one branch that decides whether there is a murder at all. Brad can claim the panel on Day −40 or Day −19 with a true sentence, the document's rejection of that sentence ("he cannot want it without saying why") is false in the world as written, and the danger of the mark that Foundation 2 kills for is refuted by the document's own D1 and C2 (initials with no face, innocuous on a wall for 260 days, nil risk on Day 14). What is left is a want, and the want was cheaper to satisfy with words. The materials for an answer exist in the world (the self he could not show; Violet as the one person who would read his drowning as flight), but they are not written, not costed, and not in the column where the reader will go looking. Behind it stand two uncomputed routes into the room, the trade's panel and Margo's book, either of which, said on Day 26 by the most ordinary line in the case, moves the room off Liam and takes Days 47 to 214 with it. Write the three rows and re-run C2 and the room; if they write cleanly this is PROCEED WITH CHANGES, and the changes are Fronts 1 and 5. If the Brad row cannot be written, Foundation 2 fails on its own terms, and nothing downstream matters.
