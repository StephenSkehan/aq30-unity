# THE STORY SO FAR: FABLE'S CUMULATIVE DIGEST

*Maintained by the orchestrator per the kickoff (`SAS/prompt-claude-fable-briefs-four-keys-kickoff.md`). This file is part of Fable's standing base and must stay as blind as Fable: after each chapter's prose lands, append only what a listener of the show now knows and what the room has experienced. Never why. No graph facts, ever.*

## Before chapter 1

Nothing has aired. The listener knows nothing beyond the public account in the standing brief. Ally has the front page, Ruby's willingness, Liam's silence, and a sergeant's "we looked, properly, there's nothing".

## After chapter 1 · The Front Page · Ep 1 aired

- Episode 1 aired the public account exactly as the cold open holds it: Violet's death and the accident ruling; her work all over the city; the four keys and the letter in her handwriting; her instruction (not the police, a locked door, and the reason she gave); the Saturday pact; behind the door a second letter that said she knew, and an object of Violet's beside it that the two survivors will not name; Brad's disappearance at Kestrel Head seven weeks after the funeral; Margo's car in the same car park five months later, her body in the harbour three days after; the two survivors' separate walks to the police, each afraid of the other; the police look that examined the letters, found them really hers, and found nothing they could use; the survivors' public war as Havenbay takes sides; the ruled sign-off.
- The room also experienced one scene not on the tape: Ally and Del on the precinct steps after the police result. Del confirmed the look was proper and there is nothing; the letters are Violet's, the solicitor confirmed when she lodged them, the coroner's finding stands. She said closed means procedure found nothing it could hold, that whatever has been going on with those five friends does not live on the paper but in the people, and paper is all she is allowed to act on. Six weeks of good specialists' time found nothing to chase. She declined to say which survivor she believed, and told Ally: I can't officially tell you to do this, but if you do, be careful.
- Standing state at the boundary: Ruby has agreed to speak. Liam has not, yet. The Tip Line has had nothing. Ally's stated unknowns stand: what was behind the door, whether Violet really identified her killer, whether Liam or Ruby has a reason to be afraid.

## After chapter 2 · The Kitchen Table · Ep 2 aired (DRAFT prose, lines unruled)

- Ruby Walker spoke on record at her kitchen table, over several sittings: the table the four worked the letter at every week for months; the Tuesday text at six and the car with the heater on; the same argument every week about whether her husband should be told.
- The listener has heard Violet's key letter in her own words (Ruby's copy, one of four identical): she thought one of the four was planning to kill her, did not know which and might be wrong; not the police, because she could prove nothing, they would find nothing they could act on, and the one she feared would learn she had been; what she found was behind the door of Cage 4, her storage cage at the fish market, new padlock, four keys the only ones; open it together, nobody alone. Ally has the solicitor's covering slip from Ruby's envelope: the four envelopes were lodged three weeks before Violet died, to be posted after her funeral; the postmark is the day after the funeral. Ally noted the date and nothing more.
- Ruby's account of the room, as hers alone: the Saturday, a second letter and beside it the name board off a boat that had hung in Violet's hall for years, which the four took as Violet telling them something; week two the boatyard line read against Liam; the next day Liam confirmed he owns a third of the yard and lets the town think he is staff ("that's the day it started"); week three the tin, the letter's claim that the money was his every quarter, Liam's denial, Brad's argument that if she was wrong about the money she was wrong about the rest and they could not do this to Liam on a maybe, which lost; week four the ladder, the letter's claim she had told Liam which ladder the week she fell, Liam's "she told everyone"; week five the board and the money again; Brad went, one message to the three that Ruby will not read out; the three kept meeting, Ruby drove it, it landed on Liam, she said it to his face, he left. Margo was early every week and said the true thing in the worst way. Ruby reads bodies for a living and saw nothing in that kitchen, and no longer trusts that.
- The second letter's words exist as Ruby holds them (the text is in ch02.md): it says I know where the first said I think; each claim leans at one unnamed man: a third of the boatyard kept quiet, how he paid for it told to her the day he bought in, the board in her hall given that day, the tin's money his every quarter for her silence, the ladder she had told him about the week she fell; the one she sent up her ladder is thanked once, kindly; read this together, and be careful.
- Decision D1 (Ally's): either the second letter aired whole (flag aired), or only what Ally could verify: that it exists, says she knew, was examined and is hers, and leans at one man she will not name (flag held). Episode 2 aired accordingly.
- Character facts now in the room: Ruby, frightened, talks and allocates jobs and ends sentences with "anyway"; the glass is tonic, six years dry, she lets people assume gin. Violet rang Ruby once at four in the morning three years ago; Ruby drove over; neither ever said what it was, and Ally did not press. Ruby has grade eight piano and lets the surgery think carols are the extent of it. Brad, as Ruby has him: keys on the table moved twice; thank you like it cost him; up at five to run; frightened he joked, drank, went still; swam badly and only in summer; Ruby loved him without quite respecting him.
- The boxes: Brad's landlord cleared the flat months after he went and sent everything to Ruby. Clothes, brushes, tape, paint tins, mugs, post, books, a laptop the police had looked at and returned, his passport and bank cards. Nothing in them explains anything, and Ally said so.
- What airing cost Ruby: her name on it in a small city, the surgery, the slip, and Liam hearing her account of him before anyone has heard his own. Ally's unknowns restated: what was behind the door beyond Ruby's word, whether Violet really identified her killer, whether Liam or Ruby has reason to be afraid. Liam still has not answered.
