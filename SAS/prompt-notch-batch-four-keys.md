## Paste block (identical for both generators)

You are generating **season-hook candidates** for the final minutes of Episode One of a true-crime podcast drama. Output exactly one flat list of 15 to 20 numbered one-liners, each 25 words or fewer, and nothing else: no headers, no rationale, no ranking.

**The show.** Echoes of Havenbay: Ally Quinn, 26, podcaster and licensed PI in Havenbay, a rain-soaked coastal city. Grounded, contemporary, noir-lite, never campy. Recurring cast available to you: Sergeant Del Cruz (her quiet police ally), Gerald (her grandfather, a retired detective, the warmest person in her world), Mo (barkeeper of the Rusty Anchor, hears everything), Arthur Finch (newspaper archivist), the anonymous Tip Line. It is publicly known that Ally's father, a journalist, died ten years ago in a car accident; his laptop was never found. The season will eventually concern that. You may touch it only feather-light, or not at all.

**How Episode One ends, which is all you may build on.** A woman's death, ruled an accident, was murder. One of her four closest friends faked his own suicide, lived four hundred miles away under the name of a real man: a deckhand with no family who went missing three years earlier and sat on the missing-persons list as "drifted", because nobody ever looked. The podcast's listeners found him; he was arrested under the dead man's name. The one physical object that proved everything was destroyed by him the day Ally described it on air: she knows her broadcast erased the evidence it found. Her source stays anonymous. The case is closed, the episode ends on the arrest and its cost.

**What you are writing.** The episode's final minutes carry ONE small unexplained moment: the season's opening thread. It must intrigue a first-time player who knows nothing beyond this episode, and hook them toward the next one. It lands underplayed, is never explained, and Ally may notice it without understanding it.

**The bar, calibrated with real verdicts:**

- WEAK (rejected): a brass coin appears somewhere odd. Why: a prop with no intrinsic meaning to a new player.
- WEAK (rejected): a line of backstory trivia about a minor character that will "pay off later". Why: retrospect has no pull; the player feels nothing now.
- WEAK (rejected): the tip line receives a silent call of harbour sounds. Why: contrived; menace with no anchor.
- STRONG (shipped, the previous episode): Ally photographs a small object at a scene and her grandfather, the gentlest man in the story, looks at it and says "Don't put that on the show, love", and never explains. Why: the intrigue is a trusted character's fear, not the object.

**Rules.**

1. Each line names what the player sees or hears, concretely: a person's reaction, a wrong detail, a sentence someone says. Never a theme, never a promise that something is significant.
2. It must be feelable with zero season knowledge, and it must generate a question the player carries out of the episode.
3. It must not contradict the ending above: the arrest stands, the source stays anonymous, the destroyed object stays destroyed, the case stays solved.
4. It must not resolve anything or explain itself. Small is good. Wrong-small is best.
5. No em dashes anywhere.

Output the numbered list only.
