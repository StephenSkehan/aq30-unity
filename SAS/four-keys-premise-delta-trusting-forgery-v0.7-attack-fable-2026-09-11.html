Front 1. The kind line.

Objection. The person other than Margo who tests the sentence before Day 60 is the estate's accountant, and he tests it in the same phone call the delta uses as Margo's arrival. Section 5 has Margo tell him three things: Violet's private loan book existed and is not among the books she handed over on Day 8; the van loan to Brad was known to everyone; and "the letter in Violet's hand says it was repaid in October, every penny." The delta then closes the estate's interest on "a certified letter and no paper." That closure is not competent. The accountant has not seen the letter. He has been told of its existence by the debtor's friend, who is also the deceased's own accountant and who, by her own account, advised the deceased on Day −110 to get paper on this exact loan. He has a missing loan book from a house that was locked the night its owner died. He has a sum large enough to buy a van and a first stock of a business, said to have come in as cash in October, with no deposit in any account of a woman whose solicitor's bill for a contested hearing fell due at the month's end and was unpaid at her death. The one thing a competent estate accountant does with that is ask to see the letter, or tell the sister that one exists. Either way the four's Day 14 decision, "Violet's sister hears nothing until they know what they are looking at," is broken on Day 45 by Margo's own mouth, and the delta does not notice that Margo has broken it. What he finds: a letter from the deceased, dated nineteen days before her death, saying someone with her key had been in her house, held back from the next of kin by the four keyholders, containing an instruction not to go to the police, and whose only money claim clears one of the four holders. He also finds that the deceased's mother's ashes are on a sideboard in Ruby's house on that letter's instruction. Section 13's row for the kind line ("Believe it; close the estate's interest; they do") records the wrong outcome for the professional in the chain.

Severity. Serious on its own; fatal in combination with front 6, because it moves the sister's knowledge of the letter and the store from Day 82 to Day 45.

What would have to be true. Margo must answer the accountant without citing the letter, in which case she is lying to a professional about a debt she advised on, not certifying, and section 5's arrival beat is a different beat; or the estate must be shown to have no interest in an unpapered cash loan at all, in which case the accountant never rings and there is no Day 45 call to arrive in. The delta cannot have both the call and the closure.

A smaller point under the same front. Section 2 says only Brad and Margo know "this autumn she started calling it in," and section 5 says Violet told Margo "in July." July is not autumn; and, more to the point, a July conversation is consistent with an October repayment, so what Margo actually holds is not "July against the letter" but Violet's silence to her own accountant through November plus the missing book. That is weaker than section 5 states, and section 5 should say what she holds accurately, because Fable is writing her blind.

It is the thing any bookkeeper who has just answered a professional's question does the same week: confirm in writing what she said, and write it the way a competent one writes it, "per a letter in the deceased's hand held by her friends; I have no independent confirmation; the deceased told me in August she intended to call the loan in for the hearing." Front 3. The back door.

Objection. The pool-closing claim is "the only people who ever used that door were the people who came in by the yard: them." The house has a yard, and the workshop whose roof shed the panel is at the house. Violet runs a trade from it. Paint, timber, "garden concentrates" and eight years of collected signs come and go through a yard door, and the back door is the door between that yard and her kitchen. The trick is not knowledge; it is a one-sentence instruction the householder gives to anyone who has ever failed to shut the door in front of her, because the door does not work otherwise. Every delivery driver, every customer given a cup of tea, every person she let out the back after dark in eight years has been told "lift it as you pull," or has discovered it in ten seconds, because a sagging door that catches only when lifted is the commonest door fault on the coast and lifting is what any adult does by reflex. Section 5 forecloses this with two sentences, "the solicitor has never been past her front room; the trade has never been in the house," and neither sentence covers the yard door or a delivery. So the room's Day 15 inference is emotionally right for a frightened room and factually weak, which the chapters may indict later; but the delta presents it as "what lets the four cross out her clearance," and Ruby's line at meeting one, "she thought she'd know, and she's dead," answered only by the door, is carrying more than the door can bear.

On Liam's word. His word is against his own interest (a killer wants the room to look outward; "latched" closes it on himself) and the police who attended on Day 1 would have recorded the doors, so his word is corroborable. The room can rely on it. The problem is not Liam; it is the premise that the trick was private.

Severity. Serious. Not fatal, because a frightened room believing a weak inference is the story's own indictment.

What would have to be true. The door must have become that door recently: Liam fitted it, and the delta does not say when. If the fault dates from a repair this year and only the four have been through it since, the pool closes honestly. As written, "Liam fitted it" and "eight years" of a working yard sit in the same house.

Front 4. Day −9 to Day 0.

Objection. I take the intent as given, since the tin precedes the book and the motive is ruled. On method, the landing holds against the obvious rival: a second, better dose, or a dose in the two glasses on Day 0, leaves a toxicology and the book in the house, and the book records a tin, a panel and a store; a fall closes the police and the book leaves under the coat. That is coherent and I do not attack it. What does not hold is the date, and it is the v0.6 answer to finding 5 that fails. The delta says Day 0 is "the last Monday before the month's end" because "the solicitor's account falls due at the month's end" and she "wanted the van money by then." That is her deadline. It is not his. The loan is on no paper by her choice; the book is the only record and he has read it; her fear names no one; three judges have told her the panel is hers; the store cannot be opened by anyone while she lives; the letters go out only at her death; and he holds deckhand's papers after nine hundred days of preparation. Every danger he faces (the tin in a nurse's hands, the panel in the trade's hands, the letter, the keys, the sheds) becomes live at her death and only then. What a competent man does with nine days of that knowledge is nothing on a date: he lets the month's end pass, lets her be out of pocket to her own lawyer, and leaves on his own schedule, and she cannot sue, cannot show the police anything, and has nothing but a loose lid. The knowledge does not move Day 0 earlier or later; it removes the reason there is a Day 0 at all, unless the world supplies what non-payment by month's end costs him that he cannot walk away from.

Severity. Serious. The device survives it as a story about a man who miscalculated, but the delta claims the opposite: "the plan is made in knowledge, which is this man." A man made of knowledge does not bring every danger forward to avoid a debt he can default on by walking.

What would have to be true. Verdict.

The single finding that drives it is the estate's accountant on Day 45: the fifth cut answered the v0.6 money findings by replacing Brad's candour with a kind lie and giving Margo a phone call in which she certifies it to a professional, and that call is the one act in the story that puts the letter's existence in front of the sister's own people before Day 60. Everything else here is survivable with rulings (date the door; put a consequence on Day −110; strip "that fast" from the kind line and stop chapter 7 using the café against a letter that agrees with it; swap the box and the flat; rule Margo's sentence at meeting six). The Day 45 call is not survivable as written, and Margo's arrival has to be found somewhere that does not involve a third party with a duty to ask.
