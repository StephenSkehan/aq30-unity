## PASTE FROM HERE

You are writing the opening of Episode One of **Ally Quinn: True Crime Merge**, a mobile game framed as a true-crime podcast hosted by Ally Quinn, a 26-year-old podcaster and licensed private investigator in the fictional coastal city of Havenbay. **Podcaster is always the lead identity in anything a player sees.**

You have written for this project before and you are the best prose writer on it. **That is why you are getting a spine this time instead of a premise.** Six openings were written from premises alone and all six were holed by readers on the same question: *how did she get that.* Everything Ally knows in your opening must be traceable to the attached documents.

### What to write

**The cold open. The first ninety seconds to two minutes of the episode.** Ally's voice, in the studio, opening the case. It may contain a scene, a call, a recording or a memory, but it is a podcast opening, not a screenplay.

**One version. Finished. Not a menu, not variations, not "here are three approaches."** Two people will read it and answer two questions: *does it grab me* and *do I believe it*. Give them one thing worth ten minutes.

### The situation, in one paragraph

A care worker rings the show and will not be recorded. She says the woman she looks after, eighty-one, is being killed by the man who does her shopping, and that he did it twice before in the same house. Everything the care worker says turns out to be checkable and true. Nothing about it is provable. **The woman in danger does not want to be rescued and is not wrong to refuse.**

### Hard constraints

**Four names, maximum.** Ally, Winnie, Colin, and one of Josie or the doctor. Del, Marion, Edith and Gordon can be *the sergeant*, *a daughter*, *the woman before her*, *the man before that*. A reader said "too many characters to get my head around" at fewer people than this spine contains.

**Not one procedural or clinical word.** Banned: digoxin, toxicity, therapeutic window, blood level, certification, coroner, referral, medical examiner, probate, executor, next of kin, safeguarding, registration, conveyancing.
Permitted: *her heart pills · the house · the will · the doctor · the garden · her shopping · the lamps had gone green.*
**If the opening needs a procedural word to make sense, the opening is wrong, not the reader.**

**Ally never speaks to something that cannot answer.** A reader killed a previous opening on one line: *"Why would Ally say 'Wait. Where are you.' to a recording?"* She does not question a document, address a recording, or ask the air. **Silence is what a person actually does with a tape.**

**No em dashes anywhere in player-facing text.** Comma, full stop, colon or a middle dot. This is a hard project rule.

**No body is shown and no death is staged.** A physical detail is permitted when it establishes mechanism, testimony or consequence, and refused when it exists for effect. Nothing gore-led, ever. This is rated for twelve and up.

### ★ A voice note that is bigger than this episode

A reader said of the shipped pilot and of a later draft: **"The speech and phrasing Ally uses sometimes is just weird. If that is caused by the noir styling then it needs to change."** That is a project-level flag, not a note about one opening.

**So: grounded and contemporary, not hard-boiled pastiche.** Ally is witty, sharp and empathetic, and she balances tension with humanity. The noir is in the weather and the city, not in her sentence construction. **If a line sounds like a detective in a film, cut it.** Write her as a smart woman with a microphone who is talking to one person in a pair of earbuds, because that is literally the listening situation.

### ★ The three things the opening has to do

1. **Make the crime explicit and immediate.** A man kills old people for their houses and he is doing it again, right now, to a woman who is alive.
2. **Put a person in it, not a mechanism.** He is charming, he is genuinely kind to them for a year or two first, and that is the method rather than the disguise. **The audience must want access to him.**
3. ★ **Make Winnie a person before she is a victim.** Sharp, rich, rude, funny, unwell and furious about being fussed over. **She is the one who says no, and the whole episode runs on her right to say it.** If she reads as a helpless old lady waiting to be saved, the opening has failed even if every sentence is good.

### What the opening is NOT allowed to promise

Ally has no powers. She cannot compel anything, enter anywhere, or obtain any record that is not public or freely given. **The doctor, the police and the care agency all do their jobs correctly and immediately, and none of it is fast enough.** Do not write an opening that implies she is going to out-investigate anyone. She is going to have to change one woman's mind.

### Two things worth your attention as a writer

**The best detail in the spine is a daughter remembering, sixteen years later, that her mother said the lamps had gone green.** It is a true symptom, a real memory, and the evidence, all at once. **You are not obliged to use it in the opening and it may be too good to spend this early.** Your call.

**The title is *The Same House Twice*.** If the prose earns a better one, propose it in a single line at the end. One, not a list.

### Deliverable

The opening. Then, separately and briefly:

- **One line** on anything in the spine you could not make work in prose. That is the most useful note you can give us and it is not a criticism of the spine, it is what prose is for.
- **One line** if you want to propose a different title.

**Nothing else. No commentary on the premise, no alternatives, no analysis of your own choices.**
