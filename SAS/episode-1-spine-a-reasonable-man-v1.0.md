# 0. THE PROBLEM THIS PREMISE ARRIVED WITH, SOLVED FIRST

**It was chosen over a warning and the warning was real:** no body at the top and no living jeopardy is the combination that cost the last premise its slot. **Nothing here is written until that is answered.**

**The answer is not to invent a second victim.** It is that the stake is **freedom**, which is on the permitted list in its own right, and that the danger is **Ally being used.**

> ★ **Ray Kessler is coming on the show in nine days, and if he is good on air the case closes forever.**

He is not hiding, not refusing and not obstructing. **He wrote first.** A courteous retired officer explaining his methods to a sympathetic host is the most effective thing that could possibly happen to him, and it costs him nothing. **If Ally takes that interview unprepared, she becomes the instrument that ends Adam Fisk's last chance**, in public, with her name on it.

★ **And there is no version where Bernie Astley is not badly hurt.** She has believed for twenty years that the man who killed her son is in prison. **If Ally is wrong she destroys a woman who has already lost a child. If Ally is right, Bernie learns her son's killer was never caught and has been walking around for twenty years.**

**That is the jeopardy: a man's freedom, a mother's certainty, and a microphone that only fires once.**

# 1. THE CARE LINE

**Episode.** A man has been in prison twenty years for something he may not have done, and the officer who put him there has asked to come on the show and say so to your face. Nobody knows whether he is a decent man being turned into a villain or the reason four men confessed. Whatever happens on that recording is the last word anybody gets.

**Phase One, leads 1 to 6.** A retired policeman has offered to explain himself and there are nine days until he does. Nobody knows yet whether the pattern in his old cases is a pattern or a coincidence. Everything Ally learns before that date is everything she will ever have.

**Phase Two, leads 7 to 11.** A mother is about to have the worst thing in her life reopened by a stranger who might be wrong. Nobody knows whether telling her first is decency or cruelty. She is the only person who can say what her son's last hour actually was.

**Phase Three, leads 12 to 16.** The recording happens whether or not Ally is ready. Nobody knows whether the man in front of her will be caught out or will simply be better at this than she is. There is no second interview.

| When | What |

## 2.2 What Kessler actually does, and it is not a conspiracy

**He is not corrupt and he has never taken anything.** He is a detective from a generation that believed you could tell, and who was usually right, and whose method is a sequence:

1. **Pick the one who can be broken**, which is the frightened one who ran, not the composed one.
2. **Long, and unrecorded where the rules of the day allowed it.**
3. **Sympathy first.** He is kind. He is the only person in that building being kind.
4. **Supply the detail**, once, in a question, and then let it come back to him in an answer.
5. **Offer the exit.** Say it and you can go home. **Fisk was twenty-four and had been awake for two days.**

★ **He believes Fisk did it. He still believes it. That is why he is coming on the show.**

# 9. THE FOUR DECISIONS

| # | Lead | Class | Choice | Rejoins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | **A** | Take Gerald's read, or work it without him | 7 |
| 2 | 8 | **B** | Tell Bernie what Ally thinks, or interview her without saying | 10 |
| 3 | 11 | **C** | Go at the pattern, or go at the one case | 14 |
| 4 | 15 | **A** | Broadcast the interview whole, or cut it | close |

**Arithmetic:** two Class A, one Class C, rejoins 3 / 2 / 3 / 1, no fork opens before the current branch rejoins, leads 7, 10 and 14 shared.

### Decision 1 · lead 4 · Class A
**Use Gerald.** He knows how that generation worked and he will say so, and it is the fastest way into the method. **It also puts a retired detective on the record against another retired detective, in a city where they drink in the same rooms, and Gerald has to live here afterwards.**
**Work it without him.** Ally is slower and reads the transcripts cold. **Gerald finds out later that she did not ask him, which is its own wound.**
*Irreversible:* what Gerald spends, or the week she does not have.

### Decision 2 · lead 8 · Class B
**Tell her first.** Bernie hears from Ally rather than from a broadcast that the man in prison may not have killed her son. **She will not thank her, and she may stop the interview, and she has every right to.**
**Interview her without saying.** Ally gets the one true thing about Kieran's last week before Bernie has a reason to withhold it. **And Bernie will hear the rest on the radio, having helped.**
*Irreversible:* Bernie's ability to refuse consent she was never asked for, or the last week of her son's life.

### Decision 3 · lead 11 · Class C
★ **The real trade, and it is the one the wrong hypothesis was built for.**
**Go at the pattern.** Four cases, two collapses, a sequence. It is the strong-looking case and **he has already prepared for it, and the fourth man is his answer.**
**Go at the one case.** Nineteen hours, a frightened man of twenty-four, and one detail that was in the room before it was in the mouth. **Narrower, harder, and it does not prove a pattern about anything.**
*Irreversible:* the confrontation she does not get to have twice.

### Decision 4 · lead 15 · Class A
**Broadcast it whole.** Including the parts where he is sympathetic, reasonable and possibly right. **The audience may side with him and Ally will have given him the platform herself.**
**Cut it.** She controls the story and the version that airs is the one that helps Fisk. **She becomes a person who edits a man's answers, which is a version of the thing she is accusing Kessler of doing to Fisk.**
*Irreversible:* control of the story, or the right to have been fair.

### Counterfactual
Remove every decision: the application is refused in a year, Kessler gives his interview somewhere friendlier, Bernie is never asked, and Fisk serves the rest. **Named people's outcomes change on every branch.**

# 12. RISKS

**1. ⚠ The biggest one, and it was flagged before this premise was chosen.** No body at the top. **The answer is section 0 and it is not a body, it is nine days and one microphone.** ★ **That answer is untested and it is the thing the reader check must be pointed at.**

**2. Representation.** Victim a nineteen-year-old man, wrongly convicted man in his forties, the person who pays a mother of sixty-six. **Not elderly, not an intimate partner, not a young woman.** ★ **Set beside the parked premise and the mid-season one, this pulls the set further out of the pattern rather than deeper in.**

**6.
