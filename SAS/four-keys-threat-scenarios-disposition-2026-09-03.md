## Forced ranking of the survivors

1. **F6, the car in the yard.** It is the only candidate that passes every gate without a "weak". The habit is exactly the kind only the four know (twenty minutes in a shut car with the heater on after the swim, alone most days and with Ruby on Tuesdays), the yard padlock key hangs on the hook in her unit so all four are in the frame, the death it prepares is a real one and a quiet one, the trace is hers to keep (a grommet that stepped out whole beside bright threads), and a mechanic would tell her what an officer would: a loose clamp. It beats G4 because it needs one hand on one bolt, not two coordinated tamperings, and because it is the one nobody has seen on television.
2. **G4, the bathroom heater.** The habit is true and private (the shut room after a cold swim), the death is quiet, the traces are keepable. It beats F1 because it is an attempt, not a preparation. It loses to F6 on the doubled tampering and on familiarity. If Stephen prefers a house-interior scene, the single-tamper form (the vent alone, the alarm left working but the room small) is the version to write.
3. **F1, the back door and the tin.** The most frightening image of the twenty (her own key under her own money) and the one that names the four hardest. It loses only on lethal legibility: a screwed door is a rehearsal, and the fire it rehearses for stays in the reader's head. Worth keeping as a second beat in the genuine letter if the ruled scenario needs a companion that says "they have been inside".
4. **F7, the blanket controller.** Patience is its virtue and its cost. It beats G2 because it is inside the house and needs no timetable.
5. **G2, the clocks.** The cleverest of GPT's ten and the most contestable: it is only a killing if the trawler is a clock too.
6. **F2 and G1**, the unit scenarios. Both real, both land suspicion on the holder of the unit key before anyone else, which is against the brief's spirit and Stephen's ruling that the threat lives inside her house.

## Recommendation

Rule F6, the car in the yard, as the inciting threat, with F1's image available to the genuine letter as a second sign of entry if the letter needs one.
