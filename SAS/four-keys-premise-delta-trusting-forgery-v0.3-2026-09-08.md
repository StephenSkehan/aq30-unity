*Third delta against premise v8.1 and spine v1.4 with amendments v1.5 (the locker), v1.6 (the tin), v1.7 (the same night) and v1.8 (terminology). v0.1 (the trust version) and v0.2 (the comfort blanket) were each attacked by two adversaries and rejected; their files and dispositions stand beside this one. v0.3 was built differently: seven load-bearing questions were walked one at a time with Stephen on 2026-09-08, each with the strongest objection stated and an in-world answer attached, and each was accepted before the next was asked. ## 1. The device, before and after

**Before (v8.1, ruled).** A dead woman's accusation tears four friends apart. Her key letter: I think one of you is planning to kill me, I don't know which. The locker letter, forged by Brad: I know. Chapter 7's turn: dead women don't get surer.

**After (v0.3).** A woman who trusted four people completely asked them, from behind a padlocked door, to find out who had been getting into her house. She said it was none of them. Three weeks later the four were sure it was one of them. Violet never accuses anyone, alive or dead. The accusation is built at Ruby's kitchen table by the four themselves, out of a list of true things, and the list was written by the one of them who killed her. Brad does not forge her fear. He forges her trust, keeps her voice, keeps her instruction to investigate, and salts it so that four honest people, doing exactly what she asked, arrive at Liam. The room convicts a man she cleared, against her stated word, and never notices that it has.

Everything downstream of the forgery holds as ruled: the room works the letter, it lands on Liam, Margo holds her silence, Brad vanishes off Kestrel Head, Margo dies there five months later, the survivors accuse each other to the police, the police look properly and find nothing, Ruby talks to the Gazette, Ally begins.

## 2. Violet, under this premise

She is forty-four, the best judge of a hand on the coast, and she writes everything down. This autumn two things happen inside her own house. Her tobacco, two a day rolled from the tin under the stairs among the paints and the garden concentrates, comes up stained through the paper, and the lid is loose, and the capped bottle on the shelf above has been moved six inches or has not. And a gilded alphabet panel comes down out of her workshop roof with a ceiling board, in her own alphabet, better than she has ever done it, signed with a mark that is not a name. She carries the panel round the trade asking whose hand it is: the café, the cinema, two other signwriters. Two of them say yours, in a very good year. She does not show it to the four, and the reason is hers and not the plot's: to strangers in the trade it is a professional question; to her pupil of seven years it is a confession that she cannot account for a piece of her own work and it is better than his teacher's. She will not hand Brad that. The other three are not lettering people and a signwriter carrying a panel is not news to them. (Nobody in town knows Brad can gild; his public work is boat names and pub signs.)

She believes someone with her spare key has been in her house. She also believes, on alternate days, that she is imagining it. She does not suspect any of the four for a second; they are the people she would call first, and the reason she cannot call them is not fear of them but that she has nothing to show them except a loose lid and a panel she may have made herself, and she would rather be laughed at after she is gone than watched while she is here. So she does what she does with everything: writes it down, plainly, and puts it where it cannot be got at. She changes the padlock on her store at the slip boatsheds, has four keys cut on Lantern Street, locks the letter behind the door with the panel, and lodges four bare notes with her solicitor to be posted after her funeral. Then she gets on, and is calmer for a few weeks, because it is out of her head. She does not expect it to be needed.

## 3. The three letters, sketched (Fable writes prose, blind)

**The key letter (genuine, four identical copies, posted Day 12, read Day 14).** If you've got this, I'm dead, and I'm sorry about the timing. Go to my store at the boatsheds by the slip. The padlock's new and these four keys are the only ones. Go together, all four of you, and read what's there together. Violet. Nothing else: no trust stated, no fear, no police, and nothing that says when the letter behind the door was written (the standing check from v0.1's failure).

**The locker letter (genuine; Brad reads it at seven on Day 14; burned in her sink that afternoon with the drafts and the debt book).** In her voice. It isn't any of you four, and I'd know. Someone with my key has been in my house, or I have started imagining things, and I cannot tell which, and both frighten me. The tin: the two a day she lies about, the stain through the paper, the loose lid, the bottle on the shelf above; I don't know whether I did that. The panel: the leak, the board, her own alphabet done better than she can do it, a mark instead of a name, two people in the trade who said yours; I don't know whether I made it, and if I didn't, somebody has been in my roof. Mum, on the shelf, facing the wrong way one morning. Then the instruction: each of you write down everything odd you've noticed this year, about each other and about me, however small, and read the four lists together at one table. Then the police, with something in your hands. Write legibly. Brad reads it and knows two things the room never will: pooled, the odd things name him (Ruby holds the marathon he called a wedding; Liam heard him price the van from the crane cab; Margo holds the debt and the calling-in), and the panel names a gilder. The letter must go.

**The locker letter (forged; what the four read on the Monday night).** Her paper, her pen, her hand, one long day at her bench with the debt book open beside him for her cursive. Her frame, kept word for word where he can: it isn't any of you four; someone with my key has been in my house, or I'm imagining it, which frightens me more. Her instruction, kept: write down everything odd this year, about each other and about me, and read it together at one table. The paragraph about the object: I have put Mum in here with this. Somebody has been in the back bedroom, or I have started moving things and forgetting. Either way she is not staying on a shelf for whoever it is to stand and look at. Keep her where all four of you can see her until this is sorted. Then a starter list, "to show you what I mean", every item true: Liam has owned a third of the yard for eight years and lets the town think he's staff; Liam asked in March and again in July to take Mum out to the point on his boat, and I said no both times, and he didn't ask why; Brad told us a wedding in the spring and ran a marathon, I saw his time. His one addition in her voice: not the police; I've nothing they'd act on and I won't have them in your lives over a shelf and a loose lid; you four can do what they won't. Her close, kept: read this together, be careful, write legibly.

What he cut: the tin (a poisoning in her hand sends a nurse to the police through any ban), the panel (it names a gilder), and every word about money (Margo holds the list). What he added: the ban, and three true things that lean one way. Nothing in the letter accuses anyone. Worked by four people for three weeks, it lands on Liam.

## 8. The three tests any adversary will run first

- **Monday night.** Do the two letters contradict? No: both trust, both instruct. The questions that killed v0.1 (chronology) and v0.2 (purpose) do not arise. The room's first feeling is relief.
- **The middle.** Does a room told "it isn't any of you" end on Liam in three weeks? Only if the salt is true and the room is honest, and both are the case. The yard is confirmed on Day 20. Liam admits the window catch and the two requests because they are true. Brad defends him. Ruby never liked him. Nobody says it, and everybody has.
- **The end.** Is an omission a turn? The letter's brief was everything odd. The town supplies the two oddest things. The letter has neither. That is a contradiction between a document and the world, not an interpretation, and it is available to Ally only when she holds both the letter and the witnesses, which is chapter 7 and not before.
