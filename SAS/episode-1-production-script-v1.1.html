7Ally QuinnDot, if you're hearing this: goodnight, the harbour misses you. Everyone else: this is Echoes of Havenbay. As always, the tip line is open. That last message was meant for me. This time, I'm listening. To every second of it.

L2 · e1_forty — "The Forty Seconds" (Evidence) · Day 1

1Ally QuinnI loaded the whole of Friday's message into the studio. I've analysed all forty seconds, one sound at a time.
2Ally QuinnFirst: a floorboard, twice. The same squeak opens all of her older messages, the same three footsteps before she starts talking. Her hall, by the phone. I've never seen it. I've heard it a thousand times. Whoever moved on Friday was standing where Dot stands to talk to me.
3Ally QuinnThen a strained spring. A door, opened slowly. The way you open one you don't want heard. And outside, faint but steady: a diesel engine, idling. Waiting for somebody.
4Ally Quinn(surprised)The steps are the tell. Eight of them, unhurried, and every second one lands harder. It sounds like a limp. But I can't be sure. Yet.
5Ally QuinnSomebody uninvited was in Dot's house at three in the morning. She kept the line open and her mouth shut, braver in those forty seconds than most of us can ever manage.
6Ally Quinn(worried)And here's what three years of goodnights never gave me: her face. Her full name. Her address. I couldn't pick Dot out of a bus queue. Today, that changes. Let's find her.

L3 · e1_archive — "Three Years of Goodnights" (Data) · Day 1, Monday evening

1Ally QuinnThree years of messages, most under a minute. I'd often pick one to close the show. Dot knew: she'd hear herself on the next episode, and answer in the next message. "Play the one about the gulls," she said in one of them. "I was very good in that one."
2Ally QuinnTonight they're not a heartwarming outro. They're pins on a map. A school bell in the daytime messages, so close she'd need to raise her voice over it. And under the late calls, a low mechanical note every twenty-odd seconds.
3Ally Quinn(worried)And the boats. She loved the boats. Years of them, mentioned like weather. Then three weeks ago, her tone was different: "the pretty one came very close tonight. Two-note horn, no lights, and a funny mark on the funnel, like a fish-hook." I played that one on the show.
4Ally QuinnA school bell she had to talk over. A machine that breathes every twenty seconds. Grandad has heard every sound this city has ever made. Time to buy Gerald a drink.

L5 · e1_pod1 — "Case Alert: Dot Ellis" (Podcast, VO — milestone) · Day 2

1Ally QuinnThis is Echoes of Havenbay. I'm Ally, here with a case alert. It's about a friend of ours. You've heard her say goodnight for three years: her name is Dot Ellis, from Rivermouth. What we know: at 3:04 on Friday morning, someone uninvited was inside her house. She hasn't been heard from since.
2Ally QuinnHavenbay PD have her name. They knocked, walked the fence, and got silence. So it's logged: a welfare check, no forced entry, no report from family. And an adult is allowed to leave home without explaining herself. They won't call it an abduction. I'm not asking them to. Yet.
3Ally Quinn(worried)So we do what this show does. We knock on Rivermouth's doors ourselves. And you'll notice I'm not saying her street on this feed. If you knew Dot Ellis, the tip line is open, and it keeps no names.
4Ally QuinnOne more thing, for whoever needs to hear it. She left us your footsteps. Eight of them. Eight steps too slow to stay invisible. That was Friday. We're faster now.

L6 · e1_cottage — "The Cold Kettle" (Evidence) · Day 3, Wednesday

1Ally QuinnEleven Chandler Road. A blue gate, a garden kept the way you keep a promise. And Dot's car in the drive, dry ground beneath it. It hasn't moved since before the rain.
2Ally QuinnMrs. Vale next door has held the spare key for years. Dot's own arrangement: if the curtains stayed shut and she couldn't raise her, use it. She couldn't, and hadn't for days. She went in first. I followed.
3Ally Quinn(sad)Kettle cold. Bed made. Reading glasses gone, coat gone, the little case she took to church gone. She packed. Quick, in the dark, but she packed. You don't pack for the river.
4Ally Quinn(surprised)The hall table: her phone charger, empty. The phone was gone. I hoped that meant it went with her. And on the cradle, dead centre, where the phone should sleep: a coin. Old brass, worn smooth. Placed. Not dropped.
5Ally Quinn(worried)I didn't touch it. I photographed where it lay, backed us both out of the hall, and kept the room untouched until Del arrived. Gerald's answer to the photo took a full minute: "Where was it." Then: "Don't put that on the show, love."
6Ally QuinnI've heard my grandfather discuss murders with his mouth full. I've never heard him go quiet. Back to work.

L7 · e1_inspector — "The Man Who Came at Noon" (Interview) · Day 3

1Ally QuinnRivermouth doors open for coffee and a box from the Kestrel Corner. They packed it heavy when they heard why. Mrs. Vale remembered him without prompting: a gas man, eight days before Friday's message, lanyard doing the work. No company name she knew. Nobody had scheduled anything.
2Ally Quinn(surprised)Her doorbell camera kept eleven seconds of him. Tall. Careful. Face angled off-lens like a man with practice. Every second step lands heavier. The house wasn't imagining. At the kerb, a pale van, left rear lamp dead. And as it pulled away, one clean frame of the rear plate.
3Ally Quinn(worried)He went to Dot's door first. Twenty minutes inside, in daylight. My working theory: the first visit mapped what her window could see. The second came back for whatever she'd kept of it.
4Ally QuinnWhether he came to frighten her, or silence her, or worse: I don't get to know that yet, and neither do you. What I know: he plans, he returns, and he's on camera. Eleven seconds, a dead lamp and one clean plate are more than he ever meant anyone to have.
