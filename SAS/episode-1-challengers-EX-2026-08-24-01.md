## 1.3 The five closest rejected seeds

Presented without repair, per the prompt.

**1. The thirty-one days.** A man is beaten to death in a wharf car park; the only camera covering it belongs to an operator whose retention schedule deletes at thirty-one days; the body is not found until day thirty-three because of where it was left. Someone knew the schedule.

*Decisive reason for rejection: Havenbay necessity.* The premise is strong, the process is verified (UK ICO guidance points at roughly thirty to thirty-one days, and Australian practice commonly sits at thirty), the reconstruction is genuinely native to a merge board. But retention schedules are identical in every city on earth. Relocate it and nothing breaks, which is a Gate Seven failure. **Bankable for a later episode where Havenbay necessity is not the pilot's burden.**

**2. The safety report.** A dockworker is crushed by a container; the death is investigated by a contractor engaged by his own employer; the family is told he failed to follow a procedure that the maintenance record shows was impossible to follow.

*Decisive reason: antagonist rationality, marginally.* The cover-up is rational and cheap, which is good. The murder is not. Nothing in the premise requires anyone to have killed him, and once the death is genuinely accidental the episode is a negligence story rather than a murder-led pilot. Forcing a murder into it made the antagonist behave worse than the negligence already did.

**3. The dredging permit.** A harbour dredging programme is scheduled across a stretch of seabed where something was disposed of; the permit is signed by someone who knows.

*Decisive reason: no living human cost inside the episode.* It is a good season-arc mechanism and a poor pilot. The victim is historical, the keeper is absent, and the jeopardy is to evidence rather than to a person. It fails Gate Six.

**4. The aged-care visit log.** An elderly woman dies in a fall; her home-care provider's electronic visit-verification record places a carer in the house at the time; the record is later corrected.

*Decisive reason: tonal and demographic collision.* It is operationally excellent, the process is real and auditable, and the institution is self-protective rather than corrupt. But an elderly woman alone at home, betrayed by a carer, is structurally the pitch twelve testers already rejected, wearing a different hat. Distance from the known failure mode is a selection criterion, and this fails it.

**5. The custody gap.** A man dies in police custody; the custody record has a twenty-two minute gap and the cell camera was under maintenance.

*Decisive reason: it makes the police the antagonist in episode one.* Del is the series' proof that not all of Havenbay's police are compromised, and the bible reserves institutional-police corruption for later escalation. Spending it in the pilot burns the season's most valuable reversal and leaves Del nowhere to stand. Rejected on canon and structure, not plausibility.

## SURVIVOR ONE — "Slack Water"

### ELEVATOR PITCH: WHY THIS SHOULD BE THE GAME'S EPISODE ONE STORYLINE

Because it is the only premise in which Havenbay itself is the evidence. A young woman went into the harbour thirty years ago and washed up three kilometres up the reach, and a man went to prison largely because of where she was found. The tide that carried her is a measurable, documented, public thing, and thirty minutes either side of slack water moves a body kilometres. The prosecution's expert was confident and wrong. The man he helped convict has four months left to live. Somebody has been walking around this city for thirty years knowing exactly which half hour it was. Nothing about that story can be moved to another city, because the water is the witness, and the player's work is literally the reconstruction of a tide.

**High concept.** A wrongful conviction, overturned or not overturned by the player's investigation, in the last months of the convicted man's life, with the real killer still living quietly in Havenbay.

**Opening promise.** The record says one thing and the water says another, and one of them can be checked.

**Player activity.** Reconstructing a tide: tables, harbour logs, weather records, ferry timetables, the original trial exhibits and the drift model that was never run. Every merge builds a piece of the reconstruction that contradicts a sworn account.

**Human engine.** The convicted man never confessed and never protested loudly either, which Ally has to sit with. His daughter, who was eleven at the time and is now forty-one, gave evidence that helped convict him, and has believed for thirty years that she did the right thing.

**Agency promise.** Whether Ally publishes before the appeal, which risks prejudicing the very hearing she is trying to enable; whether she tells the daughter what her evidence actually did; and which of two irreconcilable expert routes she funds with the time she has.

**Havenbay necessity.** Absolute. The tidal reach, the ferry steps, the harbour's own records and thirty years of an obsolete gauge station are the case.

**Strongest honest reservation.** It is a cold case with a medical clock rather than a hunt. The canon says that is exactly what true crime is. The pivot asked for present-tense jeopardy. Those two things are in tension here and only Stephen can resolve it.

## SURVIVOR TWO — "What the Log Says"

### ELEVATOR PITCH: WHY THIS SHOULD BE THE GAME'S EPISODE ONE STORYLINE

Because it is the cheapest kind of evil to believe in, and therefore the most frightening. Nobody planned anything. A twenty-six-year-old deckhand was killed in a fight on a working boat, at night, by a man who did not mean to, and then a small company with port contracts and a safety record to protect typed him out of the night. Two versions of one page. His mother has the second one, framed, because it is the last document with her son's name on it. There is no conspiracy, no mastermind and no coin. There is a form, and the people who signed it, and the fact that the vessel's own transmitted position proves the boat was nowhere near where the form says. The player rebuilds a voyage that a company would rather stayed unbuilt.

**High concept.** A death at sea, recorded as a man lost overboard, which the vessel's own automatic position record contradicts, exposing a cover-up performed not by criminals but by an ordinary business protecting its contracts.

**Opening promise.** A cleaner emptied a bin and found the same night written twice.

**Player activity.** Rebuilding one voyage from the fragments that outlive an operator's paperwork: transmitted position fragments received by third parties, fuel and tide, a crew roster, a harbour master's log, a mother's framed copy of the wrong page.

**Human engine.** The killer is not a monster; he is a crewmate who has been carrying it for five months and is the only person in the story who wants to be found. The mother wants the correct page. That is her entire request, and the episode is about whether a document can be an act of love.

**Agency promise.** Whether Ally protects the cleaner who tipped her off or uses what she gave; whether she goes to the regulator or to the company; and whether she gives the killer the chance to speak first or takes it from him.

**Havenbay necessity.** The port, the tide window that dictates when the vessel could have been where it was, and a waterfront economy where everyone's livelihood is downstream of the same three contracts.

**Strongest honest reservation.** The murder is unpremeditated, which is unusual for a pilot and may read as a smaller crime than the genre promises. The cover-up carries the weight, and a cover-up is a slower villain than a killer.

## SURVIVOR THREE — "No Fixed Address"

### ELEVATOR PITCH: WHY THIS SHOULD BE THE GAME'S EPISODE ONE STORYLINE

Because the murderer used a filing category as a weapon and it worked for eleven months. A woman was found under the viaduct last November and written down as unidentified female, no fixed address, death by misadventure. She had an address. She had a job. She had a mother who is still ringing a phone and telling herself her daughter is angry with her. The man who killed her did one cheap thing and one clever thing: he took her bag, and he chose where to leave her, because he knew what this city calls a woman it finds under a bridge. Giving her back her name is the whole episode, and it is the most satisfying ending the genre has, because the player earns a fact rather than a chase.

**High concept.** An unidentified woman is identified, eleven months late, and the identification is what convicts her killer, because the place he chose to leave her was the plan.

**Opening promise.** Three of the five things on her death record are wrong.

**Player activity.** Reconstructing a person from residue. A laundry tag, a library card with a different surname, a bus ticket, a mural photographed by someone else, a dental chart held in another jurisdiction that nobody ever asked for.

**Human engine.** Her mother, interstate, who did not report her missing because she believed the silence was anger. That belief is the thing the episode has to take off her, gently, and the player is the reason it becomes possible.

**Agency promise.** Whether Ally names her on air before her mother has been told; whether she pushes the coroner publicly or privately; and whether she trades the one piece of leverage she has for access she needs.

**Havenbay necessity.** The viaduct camps, the murals that function as a community's own records, the city's classification habits, and a redevelopment that is clearing the arches and taking the paint with it.

**Strongest honest reservation.** The victim is dead before the episode begins and stays dead, so the present-tense danger belongs to somebody else: a second woman, and the mother's exposure once the name is public. That is thinner than a hunt and it is the same reservation as Slack Water.

# SURVIVOR ONE — "SLACK WATER"

## A. Reality dossier

**Verified baseline.** Bodies in water drift under currents, tides, waves and wind, and drift models are used forensically to estimate where a body entered. The fragility that powers this episode is documented: in a published forensic drift study, **shifting the assumed entry time by half an hour changed the modelled drift distance by tens of kilometres** under strong tidal currents. A confident expert opinion about entry point is therefore only as good as its assumed entry time, and misapplied forensic science is associated with a substantial share of wrongful convictions.

**First five causal events.**

| # | Event | Actor | Authority | Custody | Normal response | Safeguard | Bypass | Trace |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1996: a woman is killed at the ferry steps and goes into the water | The killer | None | Nobody | Body recovered days later, coronial referral | Investigation | None needed; he is not suspected | The body, the tide |
| 2 | Police build a case on where she came ashore | Detectives | Lawful investigation | Police hold exhibits | Retain an expert on drift | The expert's assumptions should be tested at trial | **Not a bypass. An honest error nobody caught** | The expert's report, still on the file |
| 3 | A man is convicted | Court | Judicial | Court record | Appeal within time | The first appeal happens and fails | None. The system worked as designed on bad inputs | Public transcript |
| 4 | Thirty years pass; the man is diagnosed as terminal | Prison health | Medical | Corrections | Palliative care, possible compassionate release application | None obstructing anyone | None needed | Medical records, his own consent to share |
| 5 | Ally re-runs the drift with modern data and the archived gauge record | Ally, with an oceanographer | None required; the inputs are public | Public | Fresh evidence is put to a lawyer, not to a newspaper | **The court, which will not act on a podcast** | No bypass. She must go through counsel | The new model, the archived tide record |

**Antagonist bypass.** There is none, and that is the point. The killer never defeated a safeguard. He was never suspected, because somebody else was convicted. His entire method for thirty years has been *to do nothing*, which is why the least-cost test passes cleanly.

**Professional objection, and the answer.** A defence lawyer would say: fresh drift modelling is opinion, not fact, and courts are wary of re-litigating expert conclusions. Correct. Which is why the episode's proof cannot be the model alone. The model reopens the question; **the archived gauge record and one contemporaneous document place the killer at the steps in the half hour that matters.** The model is the door, not the answer.

**Remaining non-foundational unknowns.** Whether Havenbay has a statutory second-appeal right on fresh and compelling evidence, or only a petition-to-government referral route. Both exist in comparable jurisdictions. **PROPOSED CANON, requires Stephen's ruling**, and either works dramatically: the statutory route is cleaner, the petition route is slower and more humiliating and therefore better.

## B. Identity

**Title:** Slack Water. **High concept:** the tide is the witness and it has been saying the same thing for thirty years. **Dramatic question:** if she could not have entered the water where they said, who was standing where she actually did? **Player fantasy:** you personally rebuild a piece of the physical world and it contradicts a court. **Exclusion statement:** no interrupted recording, no confession to a mentor, no hunted witness, no coded ritual, no staged drunken fall, no audio reconstruction to locate anybody. The reconstructive object is a tide, not a tape.

## D. Complete truth

**Victim:** a twenty-two-year-old ferry office clerk, 1996. **Killer:** the man who ran the ferry office's night float, who she had found taking money out of it. He met her at the steps to talk her out of reporting it, it went wrong, and it took ninety seconds. **Motive:** a small theft, not a big one. He has never stolen anything since. **Method:** a shove and the water. **Staging:** none. He did nothing clever at all, and that is why he was never caught. **Why now:** he is not active. He is a respectable man in his sixties who has been at every memorial. The threat is not that he acts; it is that he *does not have to*. **Decisive clue:** the ferry office's own float reconciliation sheet for that night, which he completed and signed at a time that puts him at the steps inside the half hour the corrected model requires. **Fair plant:** the sheet appears in phase one as background paperwork the player merges past, its timestamp visible and meaningless.

## E. Human engine

**Grievable detail:** she had put a deposit on a flat that week and the receipt was in her coat. **Keeper:** the convicted man's daughter, forty-one, who as an eleven-year-old told police her father came home wet. **What the keeper wants:** not exoneration. She wants to know whether she lied. **What she does:** she has kept every document from the trial in a suitcase and has never opened it, and she opens it in phase two, herself, before Ally asks. **Warmth cost:** Ally spends a full visiting-day session with the convicted man during which he tells her nothing useful, and she does not record it.

## F. Jeopardy and clock

**Living target:** the convicted man dies with the conviction standing, which is permanent. **Ally:** if she publishes before the appeal is decided, she risks prejudicing it and being blamed for the failure of the very thing she caused, which is the Teacher's Pet outcome exactly. Under Havenbay's all-party consent rule, publishing her prison recording is a separate offence. **Weaker track:** the living target, and it is still severe, because his window closes with or without her. **Closing window:** four months, day-stamped, pause-safe. **Central jeopardy sentence:** *a man has four months to stop being a murderer, and the only person who can prove he never was is the one who could also destroy the hearing by saying so out loud.*

## G. Evidence chain

Obtained: archived tide gauge data, harbour records, trial transcript, the original expert's report, the ferry office paperwork. Interpretation: the entry time assumed at trial is unsupported. Verification: an independent oceanographer re-runs it, and Priya cross-checks the original report's method. Action: counsel files. **Wrong hypothesis:** Ally spends phase two convinced the original detective buried something, and he did not; he was simply given a confident expert and believed him. Ally says this on air and has to unsay it. **Midpoint recontextualisation:** the float sheet, already seen, becomes the clock. **Competent cast:** Priya reads the old report as a scientist, not a villain-hunter; Arthur has the Gazette's 1996 coverage and the reporter's notebook; Del cannot touch a closed conviction and says so, which is the constraint; Sabine Rourke supplies the only lawful route.

## H. Havenbay engine

**Indispensable:** the tidal reach, the ferry steps, the harbour's obsolete gauge station, and a ferry office that no longer exists but whose paperwork went into the Gazette's archive when the company folded. **Causal fact:** the reach runs hard on the ebb and the drift window is genuinely half an hour wide. **Relocation test:** the case does not survive relocation at all. There is no version of this story inland.

## I. Criminal economy

A man took a few hundred from a float. **Who does the dangerous work:** he did, once, for ninety seconds. **Which institution absorbs it:** the court, which accepted a confident expert; and the ferry company, which folded and whose records nobody read. **Who pays the ordinary cost:** a man's thirty years, and his daughter's belief about herself. **What compromises decent people:** an expert who was sure. **Season notch, PROPOSED CANON:** the ferry company's records were bought at wind-up by a holding entity that also bought three other failed waterfront businesses that decade. # SURVIVOR TWO — "WHAT THE LOG SAYS"

## A. Reality dossier

**Verified baseline, in shape.** Certain commercial vessels transmit their position automatically and continuously; those transmissions are received and archived by third parties entirely outside the operator's control; and a *gap* in transmission is itself a recorded event with a start and an end. A company can control its own paperwork. It cannot retrospectively control what other people's receivers already wrote down.

**NEEDS SOURCE before script:** carriage requirements, archive retention windows and evidentiary status of third-party position archives. The premise depends only on the shape above, which is safe to rule on. It must be confirmed before a script shows a track on screen.

**First five causal events.**

| # | Event | Actor | Authority | Custody | Normal response | Safeguard | Bypass | Trace |

# SURVIVOR THREE — "NO FIXED ADDRESS"

## A. Reality dossier

**Verified baseline.** Formal identification of the deceased is a core coronial responsibility and a large component of death investigation. Bodies nonetheless remain unidentified, and the documented causes are the absence of personal identification, the condition of the remains, and **limited availability, quantity and quality of antemortem information**. Identification runs on fingerprints where prints are on file, dental comparison where a chart is requested from the right practice, and DNA where a family reference sample exists to compare against. Every one of those requires somebody, somewhere, to connect two records.

Separately: **there is no legal waiting period to report a person missing.** Departments may in practice ask for twenty-four to seventy-two hours where an adult's absence is believed voluntary, and should not where the person is vulnerable or foul play is suspected.

**This is the whole premise, and it is not a loophole.** Nobody in this story fails at their job. The coroner discharges the identification duty with the material available: no prints on file, no dental chart requested because no practice is known, no family reference sample because no family has come forward, no missing-person report to match against because the one person who would have made it believes the silence is a choice.

**First five causal events.**

**Institutional-power check.** No institution lacks a power it has. No professional is absent, slow or incurious. He has done nothing since and needs to do nothing. He is not a mastermind; he is a man who guessed right about paperwork.

Prints not on file. Her dental records exist in another state and are never requested because no practice is known. Her mother does not report her missing, because their last conversation was a bad one and silence is what she is expecting. **PASS with this designed in, and if it is ever removed the premise fails.**

**Remaining non-foundational unknowns:** how long an unidentified deceased is held before disposition in Havenbay, and whether an exhumation would be needed for a later comparison. Affects one lead in phase three. **NEEDS SOURCE before script.**

## 4.6 The strongest case against my own recommendation

**No Fixed Address opens on a fact rather than an event.** A file number is an idea, and ideas are harder to hear at listening speed than a hand coming over a receiver or a boy reciting a phone code. It is entirely possible that a blind read shows it is admired and not craved, which is the worst result a pilot can get. Its second weakness compounds this: the victim is dead before it begins and never in danger, so the compulsion rests entirely on wanting to know who she was.
