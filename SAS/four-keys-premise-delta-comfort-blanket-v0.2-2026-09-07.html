1. The device, before and after

Before (v8.1, ruled). A dead woman's accusation tears four friends apart. Her key letter: I think one of you is planning to kill me, I don't know which, I might be wrong, not the police and here is why, what I found is behind this door. The locker letter (Brad's forgery): I know. Chapter 7's turn: dead women don't get surer.

After (v0.2). A woman who thought she was losing her mind was right, and the one friend who knew it used her fear against the other three. Violet never accuses anyone. She fears her own judgement: two things have happened that she cannot explain and cannot prove, and the explanation she believes first is that she is going the way her mother went. She will not say so to the four people who love her. She writes it down, locks it in her locker with the thing she found, and gives the four keys that came in the padlock's packet to her four people, with a bare note: if I am dead, open this together. It is a comfort blanket she never expects to be needed. Brad, who has the key to her workshop and knows where her house spare lives, reads her true letter at dawn on the Monday and puts a different one in its place by afternoon: I know one of you did this, and here is why, and do not go to the police. The accusation, the certainty and the police ban are all his. The other three never read a word of hers about the tin, the panel or her mind. Her true letter is not burned. He keeps it, as he keeps the panel, and it is found in his room the day he is arrested. Ally reads it in the close.

Everything downstream of the forgery is unchanged: the room works his letter, it lands on Liam, Margo holds the money fact and her silence, Brad vanishes, Margo dies, the survivors accuse each other, the police look and find nothing, Ruby talks to the Gazette, Ally begins.

2. Violet, under this premise

She is forty-four and the best judge of a hand on the coast. Her mother went at about fifty: the forgetting, the seeing things, the shame of it; Violet cleared her mother's house the week she died and took the cash from it, which is already canon and is where Margo's confidence comes from. So when, in the same autumn, her hidden tobacco is spoiled and she cannot remember whether she left the lid loose, and a gilded panel in her own alphabet, better than she has ever done it, comes down out of her own roof with the ceiling board and she cannot remember making it, the reading she reaches for first is not "someone is doing this to me". It is "it has started". She never sees a doctor about it, because seeing a doctor makes it real and puts it on paper; so there is nothing on her record for Ruby to read on Day 2. She carries the panel round town asking whose hand it is, half hoping somebody will say "yours, you idiot". She asks the café, the cinema, two other signwriters; she does not ask the four, and she does not ask Brad, because she will not let the people who love her watch her lose it. That is why Brad learns she is asking only on Day minus 40, from the café, exactly as the spine has it now.

She buys a new padlock for her locker at the slip sheds; it comes with four keys in the packet. She writes it all down, plainly, the way she does everything, and puts the letter behind the door with the panel. She writes four short notes, lodges them with her solicitor to be posted after her funeral, and makes a joke about her sister when he asks. Then she gets on, and is, for a few weeks, calmer, because it is written down somewhere and out of her head. She does not believe it will be needed.
