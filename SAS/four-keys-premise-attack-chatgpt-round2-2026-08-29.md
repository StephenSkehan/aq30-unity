## Ranked findings

1. **Bram cannot yet write the substitute letter from what he knows.**
   **Confidence: 99% · NOT YET DEMONSTRATED**

   When Bram enters the cupboard before Saturday, he has one handwriting sample and an object that is "damning beside what the four separately knew." He does not possess what Owen, Sian and Margo separately know. He therefore cannot reliably understand Nell's intended reading, decide how to redirect it toward Owen, or write the observations Nell would plausibly have made.

   He must also imitate both Nell's handwriting and her private voice across a substantial original letter that four close friends study repeatedly for weeks. An unused escape identity demonstrates preparation for flight, not skill at inventing convincing prose in a dead friend's hand.

   The swap leaves residue in four places:

   * entry and travel from Bram's two cupboard visits;
   * paper and ink not sourced from Nell;
   * drafts, practice writing or copied phrases;
   * information Nell could not have known, or omissions reflecting Bram's incomplete knowledge.

   This repair is currently more demanding than the empty cupboard it replaced.

   *For this finding to be wrong, Bram must already know every personal fact needed to interpret the object and must have a pre-existing, demonstrated ability to reproduce Nell's handwriting and voice without practice material surviving.*

2. **Nell's instruction cannot hold Owen once the reading begins to accuse him.**
   **Confidence: 98% · WRONG**

   The instruction can plausibly hold the group through Saturday while they discover what Nell left. It fails with Owen during the first week, no later than the first meeting at which the letter materially points toward him.

   Nell's stated reasons have then expired:

   * the killer already knows she suspected the group;
   * Nell is dead, so disclosure cannot increase her danger;
   * the exercise is no longer a vague allegation against four friends;
   * Owen knows the conclusion is false and therefore knows something is wrong with either the letter or the group's reading.

   Owen now has both a moral reason to report a possible murder and a personal reason to stop an unsupported accusation hardening around him. Holding him until after Bram's disappearance and Margo's death requires obedience to the plot, not loyalty to Nell.

   *For this finding to be wrong, Nell's letter must identify a concrete living person who would suffer irreversible harm if police received it, and that danger must remain active after the reading begins to implicate Owen.*

3. **"Nobody says forgery" does not remove the officer's reason to authenticate the letter.**
   **Confidence: 98% · RULING REQUIRED**

   When Owen and Sian finally enter the station, police receive four accepted examples of Nell's handwriting plus a fifth document that supposedly predicted her murder, redirected suspicion among her friends and preceded two further deaths.

   Authorship is foundational whether or not a civilian proposes forgery. Before acting on the letter's content, a competent officer has reason to establish that Nell wrote it. Owen's statement that the letter cannot truthfully describe him adds a direct reason to question either its meaning or its source.

   Add a Havenbay ruling: when a posthumous handwritten document becomes material to reopening a death, is authentication routine, discretionary or triggered only by an express dispute? The premise cannot currently rule the test out by reserving one word from the dialogue.

   *For this finding to be wrong, Havenbay must rule that police ordinarily accept an apparently authentic family letter supplied by unanimous witnesses unless somebody expressly disputes its authorship.*

4. **Margo's communications identify the stranger during the week she dies.**
   **Confidence: 97% · NOT YET DEMONSTRATED**

   Bram speaks with Margo for months and sends the invitation that takes her to the place where she dies. Her telephone or account therefore contains the last-contact route, the false leads and the meeting request. If the telephone is missing, erased or destroyed, that absence itself conflicts with an uncomplicated suicide.

   A competent inquiry into her final movements should find the stranger before Ally begins broadcasting, unless the communications method leaves no recoverable content or account trail and Margo concealed the contact from everyone. That execution is not yet present.

   Add a Havenbay ruling: what digital and last-contact review occurs after an apparent suicide at a known disappearance site, and what changes if the deceased's telephone is unavailable?

5. **Margo's key insight arrives weeks after the listener's.**
   **Confidence: 96% · WRONG IN TIMING**

   The moment the cupboard opens onto a letter, a listener can ask: why lock away one letter and distribute four physical keys when copies could have been posted?

   The premise survives the listener being ahead briefly because the question establishes that a swap occurred without identifying who made it. It does not survive four close friends studying the letter for weeks before the accountant notices the physical contradiction. By then Margo's celebrated insight risks reading as the recovery of something everyone else unaccountably ignored.

   The maximum credible lag is one additional meeting. Beyond that, the characters are being made slower to preserve the reveal.

6. **Bram's defence of Owen is a real tactic, but it is readable forwards.**
   **Confidence: 94% · WRONG AS A CONCEALMENT CLAIM**

   Bram repeatedly raises Owen, makes the others articulate the case against Owen and allows himself to be defeated. That can psychologically strengthen the group's conclusion while making Bram appear loyal.

   It is not legible only backwards. Over-defending the eventual suspect is itself a familiar suspicion tell, and Margo uses it prospectively to identify Bram. The premise cannot simultaneously claim that no tell can be read forwards and make this the behaviour through which Margo reads him.

   A listener may still enjoy proving an early suspicion. What the episode cannot safely depend upon is Bram's identity remaining a late surprise.

7. **The object's seven constraints are jointly possible, but one pair remains load-bearing.**
   **Confidence: 92% · NOT YET DEMONSTRATED**

   "Unique and uncopyable" can coexist with "describable" if the physical original carries the meaning. "He wants it" can coexist with later destruction because public description changes its risk.

   The difficult pair is:

   * damning only when combined with knowledge separately held by four people;
   * immediately recognisable to Bram alone as something he must remove and can convincingly replace.

   Unless Bram already possesses the other three people's knowledge, the object cannot perform both functions. Selecting an object will not repair that knowledge gap; the character graph must.

8. **Bram's return leaves an investigative trail, but only if Margo's death receives the competent inquiry still awaiting a ruling.**
   **Confidence: 91% · NOT YET DEMONSTRATED**

   The strongest immediate trace is not a camera or journey record. It is Margo's final communication with the stranger. From that route, police can seek device, location and travel evidence. Without it, a cash-funded return journey months after local coverage is difficult but not inherently implausible.

   The premise therefore does not fail because Bram returns. It fails only if professionals examine Margo's final contacts and inexplicably overlook the invitation.

## The woman's window

No finding on Bram staying. Once the object is publicly described, immediate flight would sacrifice his prepared life and might confirm that somebody nearby had recognised it. Staying lets him identify and control the emerging risk, which is coherent with both his practical interest and his need to remain superior.

The woman's unprotected window can survive only as hours, not days. She must contact Ally privately at the first safe opportunity after the object's disappearance confirms her suspicion. If she waits long enough for Bram to observe a sustained change in her behaviour, the premise again requires a threatened person to delay the obvious protective action.

## What it is about

The thesis is genuinely in the document, not merely imposed upon it. Nell's love shapes the keys, the group's trust sustains the false letter, Owen and Sian turn loyalty into mutual suspicion, and Margo's guilt makes silence lethal. The strangers matter because Bram cannot anticipate what thousands of unconnected people separately remember, and their information reaches Del before he can turn relationship into leverage. The slight overstatement is that strangers are people he has "no hold on": he begins acquiring a hold on the woman as soon as he notices her. What defeats him is not simply strangerhood but distance, plurality and speed. He can manipulate one room; he cannot manipulate a crowd whose members do not know one another and act before he learns which one has seen him.
