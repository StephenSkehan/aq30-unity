Margo sees the hands, stopped at ten past three. She knows Violet's habits from the books and the confidence, knows the letter's date, and does the arithmetic on an eight-day movement wound full and locked on that date: it would have run down on the night Violet died, in a padlocked locker a mile from the stairs, at about the hour the coroner gave. The message to Tessa is that arithmetic. The clock in her cupboard has stopped at the hour her friend fell and Margo cannot make herself believe it is chance, and cannot let herself believe it is anything else. The one thing that fails it is the image itself: a clock that starts on its own is a ghost story, and the first time anyone at the table says the word ghost the fear goes soft and stays soft.
