### 1. The kind line

**Objection:** The sister’s accountant can test it on Day 45. He has the business books, access to the estate’s bank records and the unpaid solicitor’s account. He finds no recorded repayment, no corresponding receipt or deposit, no reduction of the legal bill and no private loan book. What he has instead is an extraordinary claim of complete repayment inside a non-financial letter.

Calling that a “certified letter” does not make it evidence of settlement. Margo has authenticated the handwriting, not the transaction. A competent accountant asks Brad for the amount, date and method of payment. “Every penny” makes that inquiry unavoidable.

**Severity:** Serious.

**Answered only if:** The accountant has neither the remit nor the records to reconcile the repayment, the administrator expressly declines further inquiry, and an unrecorded cash repayment of the relevant size is normal enough that its complete disappearance raises no professional concern.

### 2. Margo’s lock, fifth time

**Objection:** Her earliest competent action is during the Day 45 call, before she hangs up. She says she cannot confirm repayment, that nothing entered the accounts she maintained, that Violet had told her the debt was being called in, and that the private loan book is missing. If she has already hung up, she calls back immediately and corrects the misleading impression she created.

The stated barriers do not hold. She does not need proof to report a material inconsistency. The client-confidence argument has already collapsed because she has just disclosed the loan and the private book to the estate’s accountant. “In front of him” is a false constraint because the professional conversation is private. The estate does not close its interest cleanly, and Brad is asked to account for the alleged repayment.

At meeting six, the room asks why Margo has abruptly stopped arguing and stopped examining anyone except Brad. Ruby has driven five meetings and is not going to ignore the accountant withdrawing from the discussion. Margo has no answer that preserves the mechanism. The truth points at Brad. Refusal points at Brad. A lie requires conduct not established anywhere in her character.

**Severity:** Fatal.

**Answered only if:** Margo is professionally prohibited from correcting the estate’s understanding, she has no competent basis even to identify a discrepancy, and her change at meeting six is genuinely imperceptible to everyone except Brad. The text establishes none of those things.

### 3. The back door

**Objection:** The room cannot rely on Liam’s word that the door was latched. Liam is the sole source for its condition after he found Violet. He necessarily opened or handled the door before the police could preserve its prior state. Once the four-person pool is entertained, his unsupported recollection cannot be used as independent evidence that closes that same pool.

The mechanical claim also exceeds the fact. A door that must be lifted while being pulled is inconvenient, not secret. An unfamiliar person who finds that it will not catch can manipulate it until it does. Knowing the trick in advance and discovering it while leaving produce the same latched door.

**Severity:** Fatal.

**Answered only if:** A contemporaneous independent witness or physical record establishes the latch state before Liam touched the door, and the mechanism cannot realistically be operated without prior instruction. Neither condition exists.

### 4. Day −9 to Day 0

**Objection:** Brad takes the book while it is already in his hands on Day −9 and leaves Havenbay then. He is already preparing to disappear, the debt has no paper, the panel is inaccessible behind a lock for which Violet retained no key, and Violet’s true letter explicitly clears him. Leaving sacrifices his local life but avoids murder, forgery, a witnessed return to the store and fourteen days of exposure.

His later conduct proves that disappearance is acceptable to him and that he can sustain another identity. Waiting nine days to manufacture a fatal confrontation, only to disappear sixty days later, is not his least-cost action.

This removes Day 0. There is no money meeting and no landing.

**Severity:** Fatal.

**Answered only if:** His escape route is unavailable before Day 60, the remaining nine days provide something indispensable to that escape, and abandoning Havenbay before the deadline is more dangerous to him than committing murder. The current facts point in the opposite direction.

### 5. Night one

**Objection:** The second line is the instruction to write down everything odd “about each other.”

Ruby has just read: “It isn’t any of you four, and I’d know.” A few lines later Violet instructs them to compile reciprocal lists about one another. A competent person asks the obvious question: if Violet had cleared all four absolutely, why did she direct them to investigate each other?

This is not answered by saying Brad kept Violet’s genuine instruction. That makes the deeper problem worse. The premise claims Violet never accuses them and that the four construct the accusation themselves, but Violet supplies the machinery that turns them upon one another. On night one they must resolve that contradiction before beginning the lists. Brad’s salted facts do not simply enter circulation as written.

**Severity:** Serious.

**Answered only if:** The four understand “about each other” as serving a clearly non-suspect purpose, and Violet has an established method that makes such exhaustive cross-checking compatible with categorical clearance. Neither meaning is present in the letter.

### 6. The whole

**Objection:** The earliest competent action occurs before Day −19, when Violet concludes that somebody with her spare key may have entered her house. She changes the lock and withdraws the outside spare from use.

The fourth-round answer merely moves the spare from the meter box to another exterior hiding place. That does not invalidate a copied key, prevent somebody searching for the replacement or secure a house Violet believes has already been entered. Telling four people the new location compounds rather than cures the exposure. Her embarrassment explains why she avoids conversation. It does not explain why she declines a private, ordinary security measure.

Once the lock is changed and the exposed spare is withdrawn, Brad cannot enter on Day −9. He cannot read the book, learn the complete key arrangements, take her paper and pen or construct the informed forgery. The fifth-cut device ends there.

**Severity:** Fatal.

**Answered only if:** Violet has a concrete need to preserve unattended access through an outdoor spare, has ruled out the possibility that the previous key was copied, and regards a silent lock change as intolerably more costly than suspected repeated entry into her home. None is established.

**REWORK. High confidence.** The decisive finding is Margo’s Day 45 action. The scene gives her a private, professionally appropriate recipient and enough information to report a material contradiction without accusing anyone or proving forgery. “It is a reading, not a proof” imposes a proof threshold that competent people do not require before correcting a false financial representation. Because her continued silence is load-bearing and the stated barriers do not prevent the call, the fifth cut does not hold.
