# THE FRIENDS WITH FOUR KEYS, SPINE v2.0.3
## Adversary review, Fable, round 3

Read in full: the header (v2.0.1 to v2.0.3 notes), the graph table, Parts A to G. Part H ignored. Facts stated in A to G are treated as facts of the world; where I say one fails I say why.

---

### Front 1. Violet, Day −37 to Day −19

**Strongest objection.** On the morning of Day −19 she holds, in one hand, the only copy of the only account she will ever give, and in the other, four sealed envelopes going into a solicitor's safe before noon. The account goes behind one padlock; the envelopes go in the safe; nothing of the account goes in the envelopes. Everything else she does that morning is redundancy: six keys cut and two drowned so the count is known; the keys never in her house; four addressees so no one of them can suppress the instruction; "go together" so no one of them reads alone. The one thing with no redundancy is the letter itself. A copy of it, or its substance written into the four bare notes, costs her ten minutes at the same desk and defeats the swap outright: on the night of Day 14 the four read her real words, the panel she says she did not make is not in the store, and the man who said "yours" to it is finished. The document gives a reason for the store (the panel and her mother read beside the words). It gives no reason for the single copy. Her shame (I would rather be losing my mind) explains why she tells the solicitor nothing; it does not explain why a woman who has just designed a key-control system trusts her whole account to one lock while she knows, by her own arithmetic, that one of the four keyholders is the intruder.

**Severity:** serious, not fatal. It is answerable in a row. But the act, if taken, ends the episode on Day 14, which is exactly what this front asks, so the document must show her not taking it and say why.

**What would have to be true:** a reason of hers on the page, paid like Day −29. The world already contains the material: a copy in a solicitor's file is a document her sister's people can demand on intestacy, and she will not have her sister read it; or it is the same refusal again, the four read it together or not at all, and one copy is the form of that. Either must be written into A1 −19 and C1 −3.

**Second objection, same front.** The v2.0.3 telling at the store door. Ten days earlier she took the key in and told nobody, because a told fact is a fact the intruder adjusts to; that was the whole rigour of Day −29. On Day −19 she tells one of the four, alone, the term that matters most (the store opens only on her death, to the four), while he watches her lock in the panel he has just called hers. The document says "lightly". She lies to Liam about the cylinder and to the solicitor about her sister, and tells Brad the truth. The world holds a fact that carries this (Day −600: Brad is the only one she talks to about the other three), but the row does not use it, and without it the route foundation 2 lacked reads as bolted on in round two. **Severity:** serious. **What would have to be true:** the telling written as the second refusal, the confidant she cannot let be the second hand, paid in the same currency as −75 and −29.

**Cosmetic.** A woman mid-litigation with her only next of kin, lodging after-my-funeral instructions with the solicitor running that litigation, has no will and "keeps meaning to". He raises it that morning. Her refusal is not on the page. Intestacy is decided and I do not attack it; the refusal needs one clause.

### Front 2. Brad, Day −19 to Day 14

**Strongest objection: the box lie, and the document's answer to it.** v2.0.3 owns the 17:30 line as the one act against his interest, a guilty man's reflex, the tell. I attack the word reflex. From 10:00 on Day −19 Brad knows: the envelopes go to four addresses and his to the box; the box lobby opens at seven; he clears it at seven and all four know it; Ruby's roster, Liam's yard, Margo's desk; the three will read after work. He has fourteen days of planning and ten hours on the day itself, and the single question anyone will ask him that evening is "when did you get yours". It is the most foreseeable sentence of the most planned day of his life, and the document has him fluff it on instinct. Reflex is what an unprepared man has. The document's own reason (since seven invites "and you went there alone") is a reasoned inference, not a reflex, and it is wrong on its own terms: a man who says "since seven, and I waited because she said together" is covered against the boat owner (I went to my locker), whereas a man who says "only just opened" is contradicted by that sighting outright. And he knew he had been seen: the boat owner said "early for you" to his face at 07:20. A planner who knows he was seen at the sheds at 07:20 does not, at 17:45, place his first reading of the letter after that sighting.

**Severity:** serious. Chapter 7 stands on this line. If the reader does not believe the lie, the suspect turn is authorial.

**What would have to be true:** a cause, on the page, for the prepared line failing in the moment: something in Ruby's question or in the day (the boat owner's "early for you"; a fear that "since seven" fixes him at the box at the hour it is emptied; an impulse to match three people who all read late) that makes the wrong sentence the one that comes out, and Brad knowing at once that he has said it. A costed mistake, not an instinct.

**Second objection: the corridor.** He knows from Day −19 that the store holds a 30 by 40 board. He brings nothing to carry it in, and is seen carrying a board between two doors in a corridor the document says is worked from first light. He also takes the tube out at 07:20 and carries it back at 14:00 (D2), for which no reason is given; it could stay where it was. Two visits, one board, one tube, in working hours, in front of a man who knows both his face and her door. The act that serves him better is a bag and one carry, and the man who wraps the panel for the ridge wall on Day 46 knows about bags. **Severity:** serious as written, cosmetic once answered. **What would have to be true:** the panel was wrapped and "board" is the boat owner's reading of a flat parcel, the tube never leaves the store, or a reason he had nothing to hand.

**Cosmetic, but under a pillar.** Row 4a and Day −110 say "by the month's end after next"; Day 0 says "the month ends on Thursday", 113 days later. "After next" from any day in August lands in October, not December. "The date is hers" is load-bearing and the arithmetic under it does not add.

### Front 3. The room, Days 14 to 47

**Strongest objection: "again" has no antecedent.** D3 keeps the arithmetic verbatim (I changed the lock and told only you four, and it happened again after) and cuts the tin. In the genuine letter the tin is the first time. In the forgery the first time is gone and "again" hangs. The room's Day 26 agenda is written as "the lock, and why she did not say why", and the document records no answer, treating it as her reticence. But this is a full account, written for after her death, by a woman the room knows wrote down times, and the four are reading it for holes. The line: "She says again. Again after what? She changed her lock on a Monday and told Liam it was sticking. Something happened before that Monday and it is not in here." **Who says it:** Margo, Day 26, because she reads documents for what is missing. The document has not answered it. Brad, who cut the tin because a poisoning sends a nurse to the police, would have known he needed a first incident in her voice, and the D3 table does not give him one.

**Severity:** serious. Not because it exposes Brad (a bland first incident is easy for him) but because as written the forgery has a seam in the paragraph the room reads most closely, and the document does not know it is there.

**What would have to be true:** a D3 row for the forgery's first incident, bland and in her voice, before the lock; and Day 26 recording that the room reads it and moves on.

**Second objection: the shape of her list.** Two things about Liam, one about Ruby, one kindness about Brad, nothing about Margo. Violet knew about Margo's Sunday and the green app (Day −600) and Brad knows she knew. A letter that "points inward by choosing" says nothing about one of the four. Ruby, Day 26, everything odd about Violet: "She had something on each of us but you." Margo hears either trust, or a thing Vi would not write down. Neither reading is on the page. **Severity:** cosmetic to serious; an unearned beat the room would notice and the document does not.

### Front 4. Margo, Days 8 to 60

**Strongest objection: the uncomputed fourth route.** C5 Week 8 computes (a) the room, (b) Del, (c) the estate. It does not compute (d): Ruby, alone, on the telephone, Day 50 or 51. Not an accusation; a report of facts: the estate asked me about the van money; Brad told them settled; there is no record; the only record was her book and it is not in the house. Ruby is Margo's friend, runs every meeting, and has already let go one line that did not sound like Vi. **What happens:** Ruby now holds a second line, from the one person qualified to read it, three days after the room landed on Liam. Brad reads two silences instead of one and goes on Day 60 regardless. The police outcome after Day 62 is unchanged (row 40; forgery allegation against a presumed-dead man; examination consistent). Everything after that changes: Ruby does not land on Liam at Day 96 with Margo's reading in her hand; Margo is no longer "the one who stopped believing the letter", Ruby is too; the Day 180 approach from a stranger who counts on Margo being alone finds a woman who is not; Margo tells Ruby about the stranger, and the second death does not happen as written. Route (d) does not end the episode early. It ends this episode. The lock as written (a money lie only she can catch, no accusation in it; to say what it means is to accuse the youngest of murder on a reading) covers Ruby by extension, and Days 54 and 97 onward show Margo withdrawing from Ruby specifically. But the load-bearing lock of the spine has an unwritten route through it, and the document's own rule is that every route is computed.

**Severity:** serious, not fatal; the character material to close it is already on the page.

**What would have to be true:** a C5 Week 8 row for Ruby alone, with the reason: Ruby cannot hold a thing without saying it in the room, and Margo knows it, so telling Ruby is telling Brad by Saturday; and Margo's withdrawal from Ruby from Day 54 written as the cost of exactly that.

**Cosmetic, on the front the document says it has computed to nothing.** A2 Day 5 and A3 Day 45 say Margo's baseline for the barrel was "spent down since by a van, a lawyer and a life". The baseline is the Day −1450 figure (row 2). The van was Day −2000, before it. Either Violet bought her own van and the document must say so, or the sentence is wrong in the one place where the barrel's arithmetic is asserted.

**Also.** Margo's Day 45 "in full" is not in full: she tells the accountant everything about the money and withholds that the deceased's own letter states repayment, while the estate is closing that claim. The document owns this as the room's pact. It should own it as Margo's second professional omission, beside Day 8, and pay it.

### Front 5. Chapter 7

**Strongest objection, and it is literal.** Ally's claim as written is "by the account of all three who knew him", and A5 Day 311 sources "the three survivors' own accounts". On Day 311 there are two survivors. Margo is dead and never spoke to Ally; her reading time comes by Ruby's memory of a call, which the document rightly has said "as that". So "all three who knew him" is a sentence Ally cannot source, in the chapter rebuilt twice to contain only what she can source. The habit (box at seven) rests on Ruby and Liam and must be said as two.

**Severity:** cosmetic in the fix, serious in what it shows: B6 row 62 carries the same slip ("the three survivors' own accounts", sourced to Ruby, Liam). The error is in the ledger, not only the prose.

**What would have to be true:** two named survivors, or a third living source for the habit (a post office counter clerk who consents), and B6 corrected.

**Second objection: what a listener checks.** Ally's turn is that a man who clears his box at seven said at a quarter to six he had only just opened it. That is a lie only if the Monday letter was in the box at seven. The world's fact (G) is that the box lobby opens at seven. It is not a fact that the Saturday-postmarked sort is in the boxes by seven. Any listener who holds a Havenbay box knows when the Monday sort goes in. If it goes in at nine, Brad cleared an empty box at seven, read the letter at 17:40, and Ally has aired as a lie a thing that is not one. She has not sourced the sort time and the document does not give it to her. **Severity:** serious for the chapter's logic, cosmetic to fix. **What would have to be true:** the sort is in the boxes before the lobby opens, stated in G, and Ally has it from someone who can say so.

**Cosmetic.** Ruby reads at half past three and rings Brad at a quarter to six; a listener asks why the woman who reads first waits two hours to ring the friend with a van and a mobile, and whether she is the right person to be counting another man's minutes. A2 Day 12 puts the posting on Saturday in the same sentence that says "after the last Friday collection"; pick one.

### Front 6. The whole

**Strongest objection: the book, and who knew it existed.** Row 20 and A2 Day 8 say nobody else knew the loan book existed (Brad and Margo only). Part G says Violet kept a book of everything, dated, timed, upstairs, and Brad has watched her fetch it; Day 0 has her go up for it to show him a figure; −75 and −29 have her writing her fears in it. Ruby at Day 33 says "Vi didn't write down things that didn't bother her." So the world's fact (a private ledger two people knew of) is contradicted by the world's own description: a decades-long book of everything that its owner fetched to show people, kept by a woman whose friend of twenty years let himself into her house and whose swimming partner knew her habits of mind well enough to cite them. If the fact fails, the earliest competent action is Ruby's, at Day 15 or 26: where is her book. The sister has the house; Margo already knows on Day 8 that it is not among the papers. By Day 26 the room holds that the book she wrote everything in left a locked house the night she died, and the one person who can say what was in it knows it held the only debt in the room. That is Margo's Day 50 reading, in the room, on Day 26, with Brad at the table and the room not yet on Liam. Brad goes on Day 30, the plan being ready. Nobody lands on Liam, Margo is not alone, there is no second death, and the episode as designed (three deaths, two survivors afraid of each other) does not occur. It does not end in an arrest, because R19 holds and a missing diary is not evidence of an act. But it ends this spine.

**Severity:** serious as the document stands; fatal if the world cannot hold the fact. I think it can be held, but by a ruling, not by assertion.

**What would have to be true:** a row stating that none of the four ever saw or heard of the book (she wrote upstairs, at night, alone; the loans and the fears went in the same book precisely because nobody knew of it), that Liam's twenty years of letting himself in were downstairs and daytime, that Ruby's Day 33 line is about the letter, and that on Day 26 the room does not ask because it has nothing to ask about. Row 20's "nobody else knew" then becomes a paid world fact instead of a convenience.

**Second, cosmetic.** Rows 10 to 10f: the deckhand's papers were in the dry bag at Brad's; the skipper reported him and kept his oddments. A constable identifying an itinerant adult asks where his passport is. If the skipper knew the bag was at Brad's, Brad's name goes on the file at Day −1090 and the exit dies. The document does not say the skipper never knew of the bag. One clause.

**Third, cosmetic.** The boat owner reads the front page on Day 265 (the keys, the store, the four together that night) holding a memory of Brad at that door at 07:20 on a Monday dated by a tide. His earliest competent action is Del on Day 265, not the Tip Line on Day 290. It changes nothing (Del's Day 292 becomes Day 266 and produces the same nothing), but "nobody asks until Ally" assumes he must be asked, and the front page asks.

### Verdict

**PROCEED WITH CHANGES,** with high confidence that the changes are rows and not machinery, and moderate confidence that all of them will close. The finding that drives it is the book: the document describes a book of everything and rules a book nobody knew, and the room, built to ask about everything odd about Violet, would ask where her book was at Day 15 unless the world says why it cannot; if that ruling cannot be made to hold, Ruby's question collapses the spine on Day 26 and the verdict is REWORK. Behind it, in order: the single copy of the letter on a morning otherwise full of redundancy (Front 1), the box lie written as a reflex on the one question he had fourteen days to prepare for (Front 2), the forgery's "again" with no first time (Front 3), and Margo's uncomputed route to Ruby (Front 4). None of the five contradicts a foundation; all five are places where the document holds a world fact it has not yet paid for.
